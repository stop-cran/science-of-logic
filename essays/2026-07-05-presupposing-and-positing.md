# Presupposing and Positing: Testing an Analogy at the Threshold of the Logic

*A note for friends and colleagues, occasioned by a question about the opening of the Science of Logic: why does pure **Being** seem at once immediately given and the result of a mediated standpoint? An earlier version read the beginning as **external reflection**, with **positing** deferred until the release into Nature and the whole completed as **determining reflection**. The close reading of §20 requires a correction: positing and presupposing are not making and finding at successive times. The analogy retains a question about the beginning's grounding, but cannot answer it by assigning the three reflections to the beginning, end, and completion of the System.*

---

Even if we grant the passage from pure Being to Nothing, the status of the starting point remains a question. Why begin with what is simply immediate, and how can that beginning also be mediated? A colleague proposed that the vocabulary of reflection, developed later in the Doctrine of Essence, might clarify the Logic's relation to its own beginning. The proposal is worth testing, but the terms must survive their application. A resemblance cannot establish the necessity of the System's shape if it changes the categories on which it relies.

## I. The beginning, twice characterized

Taken as the beginning of the logical exposition, pure Being is immediate: no further logical determination may be imported into it as an already established premise. Taken with respect to the standpoint of pure knowing, it is mediated. Hegel's phenomenological presentation traverses the opposition between consciousness and its object and reaches the standpoint of the science; the beginning is not the residue of merely stripping content away (*SL* §§93–97; §02). Hegel also permits immediate entry through the resolve to consider thought as such (*SL* §98). That resolve is a way of entering, not a substitute for the justification of the standpoint or the completed science's grounding of its beginning.

These are not rival descriptions from which the Logic must choose once and for all. The exposition begins immediately, but its subsequent development is also a return that grounds its beginning. Hegel explicitly assigns the completed knowledge of that beginning to the Logic in its whole compass (*SL* §§102–104). The question is how the development establishes this result, not when an initially withheld act of production will finally occur.

## II. What the three reflections establish

**Positing reflection** (*setzende Reflexion*) determines immediacy as a moment of self-related negativity. It is not an activity that makes an object and later encounters it as given. In sublating its own positing, reflection is also **presupposing**; its return is from an immediate that it genuinely finds, whose standing as presupposed belongs to that same movement (*SL* §§840–843; §20). Positing and presupposing are distinguishable moments, not a producer and its subsequent discovery of its product.

**External reflection** (*äußere Reflexion*) takes the immediate as independently given and its determining as external to it. Its own determining and cancellation of that determining sublate this externality. **Determining reflection** (*bestimmende Reflexion*) is the unity in which positedness is also reflected into itself (*SL* §§847–855). It is not a final recognition that the found object was made earlier. The result is the form of a determination of reflection, whose particular development remains to be followed (§21).

## III. The analogy's warrant and limit

There is a genuine textual basis for comparing the beginning with external reflection, not merely a visual resemblance between diagrams. At the close of the Logic, Hegel says that in the sphere of being the science's Concept appears as "a knowing in a subjective reflection external to that content"; in absolute cognition, that Concept has become the Idea's own content (*SL* §1816). The retrospective contrast is Hegel's. It concerns how the science comes to comprehend its own Concept, and supports the question that prompted this essay.

It does not, however, establish the proposed allocation of all three reflections to successive stages of the System. The Logic's immanent development cannot simply be identified with determining an independently given content from outside. Nor can its phenomenological mediation be identified with an earlier manufacture which reflection has forgotten. §20 supplies the decisive restriction: the relation of positing and presupposing is internal to one movement. To extend that relation to the whole System, we would have to demonstrate the corresponding movement there, not merely assign its names to a beginning and an end.

## IV. Grounding is not deferred manufacture

The original proposal preserved an important distinction: a beginning cannot contain in advance the whole development that will ground it. But it converted that distinction into a misleading postponement of positing reflection. Hegel's account instead makes progressive determination and retrospective grounding coincide within the method's movement (*SL* §1812). This is not a licence to assume an arbitrary premise and promise its proof later; the development must be determined by its content (*SL* §§105–107).

The Logic therefore does not leave its beginning wholly ungrounded until Nature arrives. Hegel says that in the Absolute Idea it has returned to the unity with which it began, now as fulfilled being, and has grasped its own Concept (*SL* §§1815–1816; §09). Whether the exposition has earned that claim must be judged through its determinate arguments. But the claim itself rules out using an unpaid debt of logical grounding as the automatic explanation of the release into Nature.

## V. The release into Nature remains a further question

Logical completion does not mean that the whole System has already been expounded. Hegel also says that the Idea is still logical, confined within pure thought, and speaks of its urge to sublate this subjectivity. Yet he immediately denies that its release into Nature is an ordinary transition or becoming: the Idea freely releases itself, remaining transparent to itself in its determination (*SL* §§1816–1817). Both sides matter. The distinction of spheres cannot be erased by logical completion; neither can it be recast as a missing moment of the reflection-triad.

Hegel also describes this resolve as positing the mediation through which the Concept returns from externality, completes its liberation in the science of Spirit, and comprehends itself in Logic (*SL* §1817). The passage positively connects release, mediation, and systematic return; it does not say that positing reflection had been withheld from the Logic.

The further question is whether the argument establishes why this free self-determination takes the form of natural externality (§09). Calling that step determining reflection does not answer it, and naming a debt does not prove a necessity. The circular structure of the sciences is explicit in Hegel (*SL* §1814), but a circle does not by itself warrant our proposed account of what compels each link. The earlier essay's strong thesis — that positing is withheld at the beginning and must therefore be supplied by the release into Nature — is withdrawn.

## Coda

The question survives the analogy's correction. An immediate beginning can be mediated with respect to the standpoint that reaches it and grounded through the science that develops it. Reflection helps us formulate that relation only if we preserve what its own derivation establishes. *What is found need not be an untouched datum; what is posited need not be manufactured. But those two corrections do not dispense with the particular argument by which a beginning is grounded or one sphere opens onto another.*

## An invitation to argue with this — and with Copilot

This note grew out of a conversation between a reader of Hegel and Copilot. Its revision is part of that conversation rather than a replacement of one unquestionable verdict by another. Two questions remain open: what more than the retrospective contrast in *SL* §1816 would justify extending the reflection-triad to the System's own organization; and what establishes the particular form of the release into Nature? An answer must preserve the beginning's double determination without turning the categories of §20 into a story of delayed production.

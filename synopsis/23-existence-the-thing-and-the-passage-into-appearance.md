# Existence and the Thing: The In-Itself, the Play of Matters, and the Passage into Appearance

*The fourteenth installment of Section III — the fourth in the **Doctrine of Essence**, and the first of its second section, **Appearance** (*Erscheinung*). §22 reached **Existence** (*Existenz*), an immediacy containing its mediation as sublated, not a product with an independent ground left beneath it. Its analysis distinguishes the **thing-in-itself** (*Ding-an-sich*) from **external existence**, then shows why their supposed separation cannot hold: the reflection relegated outside the thing becomes its **own determinateness**, its **property** (*Eigenschaft*). Hegel turns this result against **transcendental idealism**; we distinguish his argument against an empty in-itself from the further burden of answering **Kant's** restriction of cognition to possible experience. In the **reciprocal action of things**, property becomes the essential continuity and the bare things its inessential extremes. Properties thereby acquire the standing of **self-subsistent matters** (*Materien*), while the thing becomes their quantitative **"also"**. Yet its negative unity has not disappeared: the matters' own determinateness brings their supposed indifference into contradiction with their mutual exclusion and interpenetration. Hegel calls the result **Appearance**, existence whose subsistence is its own negative mediation. His discussion of **porosity** and gases is examined as a physical argument too: the logical criticism neither supplies nor invalidates a molecular explanation. **Essence must appear** names the development to be reconstructed, not a promise that everything is already known.*

---

## I. Where We Are: From Ground to Existence

The previous installment followed ground into **conditioning mediation**. **Formal** ground presented one content in two forms; **real** ground distinguished contents but left their connection externally determined; **complete** ground mediated that connection through another relation which it still presupposed. Ground and **condition** first stood as relatively unconditioned sides. Each then proved to contain the whole relation, and their common content and unity of form were the **absolutely unconditioned**, the **fact itself**. This was not an accumulation of circumstances crowned by a ground. The fact's mediation through its own moments sublated their externality; ground did not remain as an independent support beneath the result. What emerged was **Existence**, a **mediated immediacy**, with its mediation contained within it as sublated (*SL* §§1014–1035; §22).

The second section of the Doctrine of Essence opens with **essence must appear**. Its movement is more definite than an inward thing becoming visible to us. Reflection's determinations first had their standing as **posited moments** within essence; in Existence they acquire the form of **immediate self-subsistence**. Their reflection has not disappeared, but its negativity is initially submerged in that immediacy. The thing will make this negativity explicit and determine itself as **Appearance**. Appearance will then distinguish an essential world from the appearing world and develop their **essential relation**; only the ensuing **Actuality** is their fully interpenetrating unity (*SL* §§1036–1039). The opening formula announces this work; it does not abolish its stages.

## II. Existence — Reflected Immediacy and the Thing

**Existence** (*Existenz*) is neither the presuppositionless **Being** (*Sein*) with which the science began nor the qualitative **determinate being** (*Dasein*) of its first sphere. It is being that has proceeded from ground and contains that ground's mediation within itself. The *Encyclopaedia* expressly connects the word with having proceeded from something (§123 Addition); the argument, however, is the mediation already developed, not the etymology. Hegel's proposition is "whatever is, **exists**": being's truth is "not a first immediate, but **essence that has emerged into immediacy**" (*SL* §1040). This does not add existence as a predicate to an essence remaining apart from it. Rather, "**essence is Existence; it is not distinct from its Existence**" (*SL* §1045). The ground is not a second existent left behind its result.

Hegel therefore says both that whatever exists has a ground and is conditioned, and that "**it has no ground and is unconditioned**": in emerging through the sublation of ground's relation to condition, existence also sublates this emergence (*SL* §1041). His intervening discussion of **proofs of God's existence** tests the meaning of that mediation. An argument from the contingent must not leave God dependent on an independently subsisting ground; conversely, the sublation of the argument does not establish an immediate belief unrelated to mediation. Hegel takes the self-sublating mediation, not its simple absence, as essential. He distinguishes this from the ontological proof's passage from the Concept, whose syllogistic mediation is not the present subject (*SL* §§1042–1044). The category is not a licence to infer any desired existent from a definition.

The determinations of mediation persist in Existence as reflected into themselves and immediate (*SL* §1046). The *Encyclopaedia* develops this as a **world of existents**, each reflected into itself and into others, reciprocally conditioning and conditioned (§§123–124). It is not §22's conditions merely **congealed** into an external scaffold. Nor are existence and **Thing** (*Ding*) two independently available viewpoints. Existence, as the immediacy posited through sublation, is **negative unity** and being-within-self; in that determination it is an **existent**, a thing. Because this distinction belongs to its own mediation, Hegel calls the further determination "**not a transition but really an analysis**" (*SL* §§1046–1048). What the analysis first distinguishes is the **thing-in-itself** and its **external existence**.

## III. The Thing-in-Itself, External Reflection, and Property

The **thing-in-itself** initially claims the essential side: simple self-relation, with mediation sublated. The manifold determinateness falls on the other side, into **external existence**, treated as unessential and posited. Both sides nevertheless belong to Existence; the in-itself is not something that has yet to acquire existence. Hegel also refuses to make it the **ground** of an independently placed manifold: as initially conceived, it is only an unmoved, indeterminate **substrate**. Its colour, smell, and other differences are assigned to relations with something else, not counted as its own determinations (*SL* §§1049–1051).

The separation defeats itself through what **external reflection** is supposed to do. That reflection is not an independently subsisting manifold which happens afterwards to meet the thing: it is external precisely **in relation to** the essential identity. Its supposed independent standing therefore collapses into that relation, and its return into itself has the same character of essential self-identity claimed for the thing-in-itself. Conversely, the thing's identity is a **reflected** identity, containing the negation through which it returns into itself. What seemed wholly external is thus a moment of its own self-relation. This is the argument behind Hegel's conclusion that "the thing-in-itself is therefore **identical with external existence**" (*SL* §§1052–1054), not an inference from the mere fact that an object has relations.

Hegel develops the result through a **plurality of things-in-themselves**. Each is to differ from the others only through their external relation, while remaining without determinateness in itself. But then the supposed extremes have no difference of their own to sustain their separation: the relation is the thing-in-itself's relation to itself **as to an other**. The determinateness is no longer an alien surface attached to an indifferent core; it is this reflected self-relation. Hence Hegel's name for the result: "**This determinateness of the thing-in-itself is the property of the thing**" (*SL* §1055). The argument concerns the inadequacy of those indeterminate extremes, not the numerical identity of all empirical things.

**Property** (*Eigenschaft*) is accordingly not just the earlier **quality** renamed. Quality was an immediate determinateness; property is a **reflected** one, a relation in which the thing maintains its identity. Hegel recalls **constitution** (*Beschaffenheit*) and **determination** (*Bestimmung*): what stands in relation to another is now itself the thing's own determination, rather than a merely externally alterable side (§12; *SL* §1056). A property exists as a determinate relation between things, yet "the thing in this positedness **is in itself; it maintains itself in the relation to the other**" (*SL* §1057). The point is the unity of relation and self-relation, not the physical indestructibility of a bearer.

The relation is **conditioned**. A thing exhibits its property only if the other has a corresponding constitution; the property is nevertheless peculiar to the first thing (*SL* §1058). Hegel explicitly withholds **actual causality**: the thing is still the quiescent thing of many properties, the implicit reflection of its determinations, not yet itself the reflection that posits them. Its presence in its properties as **ground** must likewise not restore an independent bearer underneath them. Property is itself ground reflected into its externality, not a determination whose essential support has been left elsewhere (*SL* §1059). The **reciprocal action of things** here must therefore not be substituted for the developed causality and reciprocity of §26.

In that reciprocal action, the property is the relation through which things are both distinguished and connected. Remove determinate properties and the remaining thinghood supplies no determinate individuality: what is counted as one thing can equally be divided into several. Hegel's conclusion is "**The true in-itself is the in-itself in its positedness: and this is property**" (*SL* §§1064–1065). The relation that appeared to connect independently established extremes has become their essential continuity; the things **apart from** that continuity lose their independent standing (*SL* §1066). This prepares the reversal into matters. It does not yet establish a finished unity of properties, nor does a list of properties itself explain their connection.

## IV. Hegel's Criticism of Kant — Its Claim and Its Burden

The remark placed between property and reciprocal action draws the polemical consequence (*SL* §§1060–1063). Hegel's target is expressly **transcendental idealism**, not merely a popular misunderstanding of it. The thing-in-itself, taken apart from all determinateness, is "**the empty abstraction from all determinateness**"; that we can know nothing of it follows "for the very reason that it is supposed to be **the abstraction from every determination**" (*SL* §1060). Under that definition, the declaration of unknowability repeats the abstraction. Hegel's positive claim is not merely that an empty concept is empty: the development just traced shows, on his account, why external reflection must become the thing's **own** determination, so that holding the abstract in-itself fixed is an **untrue determination** (*SL* §1063).

He further characterizes transcendental idealism as placing every determination, of form and content, in **consciousness**. Against this he invokes the consciousness of **freedom**, in which the I knows itself as universal and undetermined and distinguishes the manifold determinations from itself. He charges the doctrine with merely transferring the limitation from the object into the subject. These are Hegel's claims, not a neutral statement of Kant's position. Hegel himself marks the logical scope: the external reflection considered here "**has not yet determined itself to consciousness, nor the thing-in-itself to ego**" (*SL* §§1061–1062). A development of thinghood and a refutation of a theory of cognition cannot simply be treated as the same achievement.

**Kant's** own restriction must therefore be heard on its own ground (§01; §12). In the *Critique of Pure Reason*, in the Transcendental Analytic's [chapter on phenomena and noumena](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm#chap61), a **negative noumenon** is a thing thought apart from our **sensible mode of intuition**; a positive one would be the object of an intellectual intuition we do not possess. The former is a **limiting concept**, not a positively known hidden plenitude. Its task is to prevent the conditions of sensible cognition from being asserted as conditions of all things in themselves. Kant expressly distinguishes **thinking** an object from **cognizing** it: without a possible intuition, the categories do not yield determinate cognition of objects beyond experience. The abstraction is thus not offered as a discovery of a rich but mysteriously concealed content; its negative use is precisely what Kant defends.

This gives the criticism its unresolved burden. Showing that an absolutely indeterminate in-itself cannot answer the question **what a thing is**, and developing a thing's determinations within Hegel's logic, do not by themselves answer Kant's question **what warrants their cognitive application**. Hegel rejects the finality of the separation and supplies a positive categorial alternative; the local argument does not establish that Kant must accept that alternative on Kant's own grounds. Nor does it establish that every empirical property is already accessible to us. The distinction does not soften Hegel's conclusion into Kantian modesty: it preserves the conclusion while refusing to count its assertion, or the emptiness argument alone, as the completed refutation.

## V. The Constitution of the Thing out of Matters

The advance to **self-subsistent matters** is a reversal of the relation of subsistence, not a decision to imagine each property as a separate object. The thing was to be the independent extreme, with property mediating its relations. But that mediation proved to be the essential self-relation itself; the thing apart from it became an **unessential compass**. Property now constitutes the thing's subsistence rather than depending on a further bare thing for its own. Hegel names it "**a self-subsistent matter**"; as simple continuities with themselves, the properties first have the form of **diversity**, and the thing consists of diverse matters (*SL* §1067). Unlike the earlier external abstraction from properties, this result is reached through the thing's own determination; the earlier abstraction also treated the empty thing as essential, whereas this movement reverses that valuation.

Hegel identifies the corresponding procedure in contemporary **chemistry**: colour, smell, and taste are represented as colouring, odorific, sour, or bitter matter; caloric, electrical matter, and magnetic matter are postulated in the same general form (*SL* §1068). The content-determination is not new; its **standing** changes from property of a thing to an existent in its own right. This is not an endorsement of every such scientific postulate. The *Encyclopaedia* distinguishes constituents that can be exhibited separately from properties merely imagined as self-subsistent stuffs, and rejects treating disintegration into constituents as the end of the inquiry (§126 Addition). Which constituents a science can isolate, and what that analysis explains, remain determinate scientific questions.

The **negative unity** of the thing has not simply vanished. Hegel insists that the reflection of property into itself is only **one side** of the whole reflection. Property became continuous with itself through the sublation of the things' difference; that continuity therefore contains the negative moment through which it arose. Over against the positive self-subsistence of matter, this moment is the thing as **this thing**. It has acquired determinateness, but determinateness in the element of **unessentiality**: the matters' continuity, not an independently determining thing, now bears the essential subsistence (*SL* §§1069–1071). This retained negative moment is indispensable to the contradiction that follows.

As **this**, the thing is only the matters' quantitative connection: some amount of one, **also** an amount of another. Each matter continues beyond this thing into others and is indifferent to the particular collection. Their distinct contents do not yet supply a connection governing the whole. Hegel calls it "**this connection of having no connection**" (*SL* §1072). The collection can alter by addition, removal, or change of proportions; in this respect the thing is "**absolute porosity without measure or form of its own**" (*SL* §1073). This is the standing of the thing under the determinations just developed, not a finding that a concrete body has no measurable structure.

## VI. Dissolution, Porosity, and the Passage into Appearance

The dissolution concerns **the matters too**, not merely a container unable to hold them together. Their reflection into themselves is the form of their subsistence; their content remains **determinate**, and therefore related negatively to other determinate contents. One is not what the other is; as independently subsisting matters, each also excludes the other's subsistence. Their own determinateness thus reinstates the **negative relation** that the indifferent **also** seemed to eliminate. The negative unity of the thing is not an extra bond brought back from outside: it belongs to the matters themselves (*SL* §1074).

The thing now contains opposed requirements in the **same unity**. It is the subsistence of the different matters together, yet their negative relation means that one subsists through the other's non-subsistence. They must interpenetrate in the thing, while remaining indifferent and independent in that interpenetration. Hegel calls this a self-contradictory "**mediation of independent self-subsistence through its opposite, namely, through its negation**" (*SL* §1075). The result is not simply that all dependence is impossible, or that the unity needs a more substantial bearer. What fails is the claim to **independent** immediacy when its own determinations make subsistence this negative mediation.

The remark on **porosity** gives the argument a physical target. Hegel insists that a thing is warm, coloured, and otherwise determined at the same point; assigning separate stuffs to these properties and lodging each in the pores of the others makes every matter's non-subsistence the place of another's subsistence. He rejects the appeal to the **smallness** of parts and pores as an obscuring quantitative substitute for the required conceptual relation. His charge is not simply that the particles are unseen: pictorial thinking gives its reflective posits the same sensible existence as colour or heat, while leaving the contradictory coexistence at one point intact (*SL* §§1076–1078). But he also grants that accounts of steam and of gases diffusing into one another bring out **one side** of the Concept of the thing: each gas remains continuous with itself and is "as good as **a vacuum for the other**". He nevertheless concludes that their subsistence at the same point is contradictory, and that the thing is therefore Appearance (*SL* §1079). Both the concession and the further conclusion are his.

That physical conclusion needs assessment, not protection by the word **categorial**. In the modern **ideal-gas** model, molecular size is negligible relative to separation and intermolecular forces are neglected apart from elastic collisions; pressure comes from momentum transfer to the container walls. Under these assumptions, nonreacting components each contribute the pressure they would exert alone at the same temperature and volume: *pᵢ = nᵢ · R · T / V*, with the total pressure the sum of these partial pressures. Here *nᵢ* is the component's molar amount and *R* the gas constant. The appeal to small parts and interstices is precisely the move Hegel contests. Here, however, smallness specifies a relation of scales, not an imperceptibility that merely obscures contradictory requirements. Molecules dispersed through a common **volume** need not occupy the **same point**; the model does not make every property a separate, continuous, impenetrable stuff. Its inferred molecular structure is assessed through quantitative consequences, not equated with the immediately sensible status of colour. Its approximate independence also has a limited regime: finite molecular volume and intermolecular attraction explain departures from ideal behaviour, particularly at high pressures or low temperatures (OpenStax, *Chemistry 2e*, [9.3](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-3-stoichiometry-of-gaseous-substances-mixtures-and-reactions), [9.5](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-5-the-kinetic-molecular-theory), and [9.6](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-6-non-ideal-gas-behavior)). This is our assessment using a later explanatory model, not an attribution to Hegel. The model's assumptions remain open to scientific examination, but its determinate explanatory content is not cancelled by the logical inadequacy of an absolutely independent matter.

Hegel's categorial result must likewise retain its positive content. Existence had already contained reflection; what is now **posited** is that its subsistence is **negative mediation with itself**, not independent immediacy. He states that "the truth of Existence is to have its being-in-self in unessentiality, or its subsisting in an other", and concludes: "**It is therefore Appearance**" (*SL* §1075). The former **shine** (*Schein*, §20) now has existent moments, while existence's immediacy has been reduced to a moment: "**Appearance is accordingly the unity of illusory being and Existence**" (*SL* §1084). The reflection making it appearance is its **own**, not an external comparison performed by a knower (*SL* §§1080–1083). This establishes the next logical subject; it neither removes the work of knowing concrete things nor anticipates the completed unity of inner and outer.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first makes the thing-in-itself a hidden store of properties.** Hegel's initially abstract in-itself is precisely the thing without determinateness, and the chapter develops why that abstraction cannot stand as its truth. But rejecting that bare substrate is not the assertion that nothing remains undiscovered. A logical claim about what a thing's determinateness is must be distinguished from a claim about what we presently know; Kant's critical restriction poses a further question, not answered by substituting a popular hidden-world picture for it.

**The second treats Existence as the bare assertion that something is there.** The category has a specific derivation from ground and a specific internal articulation. It is not interchangeable with Being or determinate being, and it does not name an independent predicate bestowed on a ready-made essence. The analysis into thing-in-itself and external existence reads out the mediation already contained in Existence; it is not a fresh set of observations added to an otherwise contentless assertion.

**The third reads the matters as Hegel's final theory of composition.** Their self-subsistence is a necessary station in his development and the object of its next criticism, not its final ontology. Nor is every analysis into constituents thereby false. The question is what independence those constituents possess and what their connection explains. A successful finite scientific account need not claim the absolute self-subsistence that the logical argument dissolves; conversely, naming that dissolution does not itself supply the account.

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **mediation must be reconstructed where it operates**. §22's passage from ground does not by itself yield the fully developed thing, still less Appearance. The analysis must show why external reflection becomes the thing's own determinateness, why property takes the standing of matter, and why the negative unity survives that reversal. Omitting the last step leaves the dissolution looking like an external complaint that a collection lacks a binder; deriving it locates the difficulty in the matters' own claim to subsistence. The result is richer than the disappearance of a substrate.

The second is that **recollection needs a determinate warrant**. Hegel expressly distinguishes the existent thing from the Something of Being (§11; *SL* §§1047–1048), and reflected property from quality and constitution (§12; *SL* §1056). The earlier determinations recur **transformed through the present argument**. Those comparisons do not license a universal sequence of One, Many, collapse, and renewed unity; still less do they replace the mediation that establishes each result. Existence contains a distinction because it is reflected immediacy, not because an earlier chapter supplied a pattern to repeat.

The third is that **a categorial development and an immanent refutation incur different burdens**. Hegel's demand to meet an opponent on the opponent's own ground (§27) makes it necessary to examine Kant's reason for retaining a limiting concept, not merely Hegel's reason for developing beyond an empty abstraction. The same discipline requires a physical theory to be assessed for what it claims and explains, not dismissed for failing to be the completed logical truth of matter. This does not retreat from Hegel's conclusions; it distinguishes their content, their demonstrated warrant, and the further claims for which the local argument has not yet paid.

## IX. What Comes Next

Appearance initially distinguishes its **essentiality** from its unessential side. The self-equal content within its changing determinations is the **law of appearance** (*SL* §1085). That distinction, not a claim of finished transparency, opens the next installment.

- **§24 — Appearance and the Essential Relation**: law and appearance, the **world in and for itself** and the **world of appearance**, then **whole and parts**, **force and its expression**, and **inner and outer**. Their determinate relations develop the unity that mere Existence did not yet posit.
- **Beyond**: **Actuality**, possibility, contingency, and necessity (§25), then substance, causality, and reciprocity (§26). The thing's conditioned properties do not already possess the developed causal activity to be examined there.

## Coda

The thing first seemed to have its essence in an inward identity untouched by its relations. Yet the identity was reflected, and the relations assigned outside it became its own determinateness. When those determinations claimed independence as matters, the thing seemed reduced to their collection; but the negative unity persisted in the matters' own determination and undid their claim to immediate self-subsistence. **Appearance** is the name of that result, not a veil laid over a ready-made reality and not a certificate that reality has no undiscovered content. *What the argument takes away is the independent ground beneath the showing; what it requires us to investigate is the showing's own essential connection.*

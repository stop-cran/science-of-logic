# The Determinations of Reflection: Identity, Difference, Contradiction

*The twelfth installment of Section III — and the second in the **Doctrine of Essence**, read closely. §20 disclosed **reflection**: essence's shining within itself, the negative that stays with itself in its other, culminating in **determining reflection**, whose product is a **determination of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmung*) — a positedness that yet subsists like a being. This installment develops the three determinations — identity, difference, contradiction — behind the fixed "laws of thought" the old logic handed down and never derived. **Identity** (*A = A*) proves not the empty tautology it seems: its very form promises a difference it fails to deliver, and self-sameness shows itself to be the *active excluding* of difference — so identity already contains **difference** within it. Difference unfolds in turn: as **difference in itself** (self-related negativity, each of identity and difference containing the other); as **diversity** (*Verschiedenheit*), difference gone external, the mere "also" of things merely unlike, compared by an outside third into likeness and unlikeness; and as **opposition** (*Gegensatz*), the **positive** and the **negative**, each of which *is* only as the not-being of its other (the poles of a magnet, positive and negative charge, debit and credit). Pressed, opposition becomes **contradiction** (*Widerspruch*): each side, taken as self-subsistent, is what it is only by excluding the very other it requires to be itself — the bugbear the old logic proscribed, which Hegel reclaims as the **root of all movement and vitality**. Contradiction does not simply cancel; it **resolves** — the self-excluding sides *go to ground* (*zu Grunde gehen*), and the unity that holds them as sublated is **Ground** (*Grund*), the threshold of §22. Here the categorial-not-empirical guardrail holds: the Logic supplies the *form* of opposition, not the physics of the magnet.*

---

## I. Where We Are: From Reflection to Its Determinations

The previous installment won reflection as essence's mode of movement — not *passing-over* into an other and vanishing, as in Being, but *shining* within itself, bending back, staying with itself in its distinctions. And it ended at **determining reflection**: the reflection that posits its own presupposition, whose deposit is a peculiar hybrid — a **positedness that is at once a reflection-into-self**, a shine that has stiffened into a *fixed* determinacy holding firm on its own. Such a determinacy is a **determination of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmung*). Hegel opens the new chapter on exactly that note: "Reflection is determinate reflection; hence essence is determinate essence, or it is *an essentiality*" — reflection being "*the showing of the illusory being of essence within essence itself*" (*SL* §860).

Now we must watch these determinations actually arise. The old logic bequeathed three **laws of thought** — the law of identity (*A = A*), the law of contradiction (*A is not not-A*), the law of the excluded middle (*either A or not-A, no third*) — and took them as fixed axioms, self-evident and externally collected, the bedrock of correct thinking, requiring and admitting no derivation. Hegel states the tradition's claim exactly as the tradition made it: these propositions "ranked as *the universal laws of thought* that lie at the base of all thinking, that are absolute in themselves and incapable of proof, but are immediately and incontestably recognised and accepted as true by all thinking that grasps their meaning" (*SL* §862). Hegel's wholly different claim is that such laws are the frozen deposit of three **determinations of reflection** — **identity**, **difference**, **contradiction** — moments through which essence distinguishes itself from itself, each *generated* by the movement of the last, each one-sided, each — taken as the whole truth — false. Their propositional dress "does not exempt them from transition and contradiction"; set side by side as absolute laws they are "*opposed to one another,* they contradict one another and mutually sublate themselves" (*SL* §867) — for if everything is identical with itself, "then it is not *different*, not *opposed*, has no *ground*" (*SL* §868). What the tradition laid side by side as a list, the *Logic* exhibits as a **movement**: identity giving way to difference, difference sharpening through diversity into opposition, opposition breaking into contradiction, and contradiction resolving into ground — the law of identity finding its place at identity, the "law of diversity" at diversity, and both non-contradiction and excluded middle answered within contradiction. Hegel announces that very itinerary in advance: essence is "at first, simple self-relation, pure *identity*"; then "the proper determination is *difference*," external or indifferent as "*diversity*" and opposed as "*opposition*"; then, "as *contradiction*, the opposition is reflected into itself and withdrawn into its *ground*" (*SL* §861). The "laws of thought" are the stations of that movement, frozen and mislabelled as first principles.

## II. Identity

Essence, reflected wholly into itself, is at first **simple self-relation**: the pure identity of essence with itself, *A = A*. This looks like the emptiest and safest of all determinations — the very type of a truth beyond dispute. It is neither empty in the way it thinks nor safe. Hegel grants the emptiness at once, and in the strongest terms: identity "is its determination, but as such it is rather the absence of any determination" (*SL* §861). Yet this is not the understanding's inert sameness: it is "not that equality-with-self that *being* or even *nothing* is, but the equality-with-self that has brought itself to unity," and therefore "not *abstract* identity" (*SL* §870).

Consider what the law of identity actually says. "*A* is *A*"; "a tree is a tree"; "A plant is a plant." The **form** of the proposition — subject, copula, predicate — *promises a difference*: to say "*A* is…" is to set out as though something will be predicated, some content unfolded. And then the sentence delivers nothing but *A* back again. Hegel puts the mechanism precisely: "*A is* is a beginning that hints at something different to which an advance is to be made; but this different something does not materialise; *A is—A*; the difference is only a vanishing" (*SL* §881). The form holds out a distinction and the content retracts it; the proposition refutes, by its own shape, the bare self-sameness it asserts — "such *identical* talk therefore *contradicts itself*" (*SL* §880). Hegel concedes the tradition's own verdict on the empty formula — "it has therefore been rightly remarked that this law of thought has *no content* and leads no further" (*SL* §875) — and tests it by the tradition's own appeal to experience: asked "What is a plant?", the answer "A plant is a plant" is granted true by everyone and "at the same time it is equally unanimously declared that the statement says *nothing*" (*SL* §879). Every attempt to *state* pure identity thus already performs the doubling — *A* as subject, *A* as predicate, the same held apart from itself in order to be equated with itself — that pure identity was supposed to exclude.

And that is the speculative point. Identity is not the abstract, inert sameness the understanding takes it for; it is **self-relation as the active excluding of difference**. To be identical-with-itself is to hold off, to negate, an otherness — which means the otherness is *there*, within the relation, as the very thing excluded. Hegel's own formula is that "identity is the reflection-into-self that is identity only as internal repulsion" (*SL* §873), and the conclusion he draws is unsparing: "Identity, therefore, is *in its own self* absolute non-identity" (*SL* §874). The very people who insist that identity is not difference convict themselves, for in saying that identity *differs* from difference "they are themselves saying that *identity is different*" (*SL* §875). Pure light and pure darkness — as the logic of Being already remarked — are equally invisible; one sees only in their difference. So too identity without difference is indistinguishable from empty night. **Identity contains difference within itself** — not as a foreign intrusion but as its own inner moment, the negativity it *is*. With this, identity has, of itself, shown itself to be its opposite. The chapter's verdict on the law is correspondingly blunt: taken as expressing abstract identity against difference, it "is not a law of thought, but rather the opposite of it," and such laws "contain more than is *meant* by them, to wit, this opposite, absolute difference itself" (*SL* §884). We turn to difference.

## III. Difference in Itself — the Absolute Difference

Take difference first in its purest form, as it issues immediately from identity: **difference in itself**, *absolute difference*. Not yet the difference *between* two given things — that comes later — but difference as such, the simple negativity that reflection is: "the negativity which reflection has within it, the nothing which is said in enunciating identity" (*SL* §885). Hegel insists it be taken in its simplicity — "It is essential to grasp absolute difference as *simple*" — for in the absolute difference of *A* and not-*A* "it is the *simple not* which, as such, constitutes it" (*SL* §886). Just as identity showed itself to contain difference, so this difference contains identity: it is *self-related* difference, difference that refers only to itself, and in referring only to itself it is *identical with itself* as difference. Each of the two — identity and difference — thus proves to be **the whole**: identity is the identity of itself and difference, and difference is the difference of itself and identity. Hegel says it exactly so — "Difference is the whole and its own *moment*, just as identity equally is its whole and its moment" — and marks the result as the load-bearing one, "the essential nature of reflection and… the *specific, original ground of all activity and self-movement*" (*SL* §887). Neither is a fixed determinacy standing apart; each is itself only through the other, and contains the other as its own moment: difference "has its other, identity, within itself, just as identity, having entered into the determination of difference, has not lost itself in it as its other, but preserves itself in it" (*SL* §888).

This is the abstract core of the matter, and it is easily lost, so let it be put flatly: *identity and difference are not two independent determinacies that happen to be compared, but two names for one self-relating negativity, each of which, pressed, becomes the other.* Difference held purely in itself, however, cannot remain in this equilibrium. Precisely because it is the difference that is *identical with itself*, it lets its two sides — identity and difference — fall apart as **self-subsistent**, each indifferent to the other. That is Hegel's own transition: since difference "has two moments that are themselves reflections-into-self," it *is* **diversity** (*SL* §889); identity "*falls apart* within itself into diversity" because its moments, being self-identical, "*subsist* as indifferently different towards one another" (*SL* §890). Difference-in-itself thereby collapses into its next, degraded form: **diversity**.

## IV. Diversity — Difference Gone External

In **diversity** (*Verschiedenheit*), the two moments of difference fall asunder into mutual **indifference**. Things are now merely *diverse*: each is self-identical, complete in itself, and its difference from the others is a matter of external comparison — they differ "also," alongside being what they each are. The difference has fallen *outside* the things and into a **comparing third** — into the observer, the understanding, who holds two items together and pronounces them **alike** (*gleich*) in one respect, **unlike** (*ungleich*) in another. Because each moment is now only self-related, "they are *not determined* against one another," and so "the *difference is external to* them" (*SL* §892); in diversity "reflection has become, in general, *external* to itself" (*SL* §893). Hegel's formulation of the resulting externality is exact: whether something is like something else "does not concern either the one or the other," since "identity or non-identity, as likeness or unlikeness, is the verdict of a third party distinct from the two things" (*SL* §895).

Likeness and unlikeness are thus the form difference takes when it has become external: no longer the thing's own self-relating negativity, but a relation instituted by comparison and split across "respects" — *A* is like *B* in colour, unlike in weight. And the comparing understanding keeps the two apart, in different regards, so that likeness and unlikeness never meet. But this separation cannot hold, for it is the *same* comparison that pronounces both, the *same* two items that are said to be alike and unlike; likeness and unlikeness themselves prove to be inseparable, each intelligible only against the other. Hegel names the evasion and kills it in one stroke: self-alienated reflection "separates them by the introduction of *'in so far', of sides and respects*" (*SL* §897), and yet "this holding apart of likeness and unlikeness is their destruction" — for "both are determinations of difference; they are relations to one another, the one being what the other is not… and both essentially have this relation and have no meaning apart from it" (*SL* §898). The much-repeated "law" — that all things are **diverse**, that no two are perfectly alike (*SL* §903) — reaches its limit and its truth in **Leibniz's** principle of the **identity of indiscernibles**, which Hegel reports in its anecdotal form: the proposition Leibniz "propounded… at court and caused the ladies to look at the leaves of trees to see whether they could find two alike," with the dry aside, "Happy times for metaphysics when it was the occupation of courtiers and the testing of its propositions called for no more exertion than to compare leaves!" (*SL* §904). Pushed to consistency, mere external diversity points beyond itself to a difference that is *internal* to the thing, a difference the thing makes to itself — and the law meant to secure it collapses: two things "are at once alike and unlike," which "involves the dissolution and nullity of the *law of diversity*" (*SL* §906). External diversity thereby sublates itself into a difference that is *determinate* and *self-related*: "Diversity, whose *indifferent* sides are just as much simply and solely *moments* of one negative unity, is *opposition*" (*SL* §901).

## V. Opposition — the Positive and the Negative

**Opposition** (*Gegensatz*) is the unity — the truth — of identity and difference: "In opposition, the *determinate rejection*, difference, finds its completion. It is the unity of identity and difference; its moments are different in one identity and thus are *opposites*" (*SL* §908). Here difference is at last **determinate**: not the empty "not the same" of diversity, but a difference in which each side is what it is *precisely and only* through its relation to its other. The two sides are the **positive** and the **negative**. And the deep point, the one the understanding cannot hold, is this: each of them is *self-subsistent* — each claims to be a whole standing on its own, "the positive in and for itself" or "in and for itself the negative" (*SL* §919) — and yet each *is* what it is only as the **not-being of the other**. Hegel states both halves in a single breath: each "*is, only in so far as the other is*; it is what it is, through the other, through its own non-being," and equally each "is, *in so far as the other is not*" (*SL* §913). The positive is the self-identical that is *not* the negative — "the *not-opposite*, the sublated opposition, but as a side of the opposition itself" (*SL* §916); the negative is "the independently existing opposite contrasted with the positive" (*SL* §917); strip away the relation to the other and neither has any determinacy left at all.

The world of opposition is full of instances, and Hegel's own are drawn, pointedly, from arithmetic and from bookkeeping rather than from physics. In bare arithmetic *+a* and *−a* are "simply opposite magnitudes," with *a* as the "*implicit*… unity forming their common base," and "the one is just as much an *opposite* as the other" (*SL* §920). His illustrations are an hour's journey east cancelled by the same distance travelled west, and an amount of liabilities that "reduces the assets by a similar amount"; in neither case is one side negative of itself, for "it is a third point of view outside them that makes one positive and the other negative," just as liabilities "are not in and for themselves the negative; they are the negative only in relation to the debtor; for the creditor, they are his positive asset" (*SL* §922). The same sum of money is accordingly "double: it is a means of subsistence both for the creditor and for the debtor" (*SL* §925). The general licence is stated in the chapter proper: "each side is of such a kind that it can be taken equally well as positive as negative" (*SL* §914). But — and this is the turn that repays attention — the interchangeability is *not* the last word. In multiplication and division "plus *and minus* receive the more determinate meaning of the positive and negative in themselves," the relation of the factors being qualitative and not merely one of increase and decrease (*SL* §928); there "the negative here is the intrinsically opposite as such, but the positive is an indeterminate, indifferent sign in general," so that "a determination as negation… is introduced solely by the negative, not by the positive" (*SL* §929). From which the sign rule *follows* rather than being stipulated: "−*a* times −*a* is also +*a*²… because it is to be taken *negatively*. But the negation of negation is the positive" (*SL* §930). Hegel's second set of examples is grammatical and familial rather than quantitative — "above and below, right and left, father and son, and so on *ad infinitum*, all contain opposition in each term," since "'above' is specifically just this, not to be 'below', and only *is, in so far as* there is a 'below'," and "Father is the other of son, and the son the other of father, and each only *is* as this other of the other" (*SL* §960). In each, the positive is not first positive and then, additionally, opposed; its *whole content* is to be the negation of its negative. (Two illustrations often attached to this chapter — the **north and south poles of a magnet** and **positive and negative charge** — are ours, not Hegel's: his polarity examples belong to the *Philosophy of Nature*, and the Opposition chapter's own are the arithmetical and economic ones just given. They fit the category exactly, and the guardrail of §17–§20 explains why that is unsurprising rather than probative: the Logic does not deduce magnetism or the sign conventions of algebra. It exhibits the categorial **form** — determinate opposition — of which these are the instances. The physics is found; the category is thought.)

Now press the two sides. Each was to be *self-subsistent* — a positive-in-itself, a negative-in-itself, each excluding the other and complete without it. Hegel puts both halves of the strain in one sentence: in opposition the sides are "determined only by the other, and therefore only moments; but they are no less determined within themselves, mutually indifferent and mutually exclusive: the *self-subsistent determinations of reflection*" (*SL* §931). Yet each *is* only as the exclusion of the other, which is to say only *through* the other it excludes: as the whole opposition "each is mediated with itself *by its other and contains* it," and at the same time "it is mediated with itself by the *non-being of its other*; thus it is a unity existing on its own and it *excludes* the other from itself" (*SL* §932). Each therefore contains, at its own core, the very other it was defined against — and cannot both contain it and exclude it. Opposition, taken seriously, has become **contradiction**.

## VI. Contradiction — and Its Resolution into Ground

Here is the pivot of the whole sphere, and the determination the old logic most feared. In **contradiction** (*Widerspruch*), each of the opposed sides is **the whole**: the positive is the entire opposition (itself *plus* its excluded negative held within it), and so is the negative. Each is self-subsistent, and each is, in the same respect and at the same time, the *sublation* of the other and therefore of itself. Each excludes the other and thereby excludes itself; each is only by being the perishing of what it needs in order to be. Hegel's sentence is the whole thought compressed: the self-subsistent determination "that contains the opposite determination, and is self-subsistent in virtue of this inclusion, at the same time also excludes it; in its self-subsistence, therefore, it excludes from itself its own self-subsistence… It is thus *contradiction*" (*SL* §933). Because the excluding is a single act, "each side in its own self excludes itself" (*SL* §934). This is not a mistake in reasoning to be tidied away. It is the determinate structure of the opposed determinations themselves — and Hegel says as much directly: it is "not, so to speak, a blemish, an imperfection or a defect in something if a contradiction can be pointed out in it" (*SL* §963).

The tradition met this with the **law of contradiction** — nothing can both be and not be, *A* cannot be not-*A* — and the **law of the excluded middle**: "*something is either A or not-A; there is no third*" (*SL* §952). Hegel's response is not to wave logic away but to locate what these laws *miss*. He grants the excluded middle a genuine content — that "everything is an *opposite*, is *determined* as either positive or negative," a proposition whose "necessity" lies "in the fact that identity passes over into difference, and this into opposition" — but observes that as ordinarily understood it collapses into the empty claim that of any predicate either it or its non-being belongs to a thing, which is "so trivial that it is not worth the trouble of saying it" (*SL* §953). Then the law is answered in two steps. First: there *is* a third, and it is given in the law itself — "*A* itself is present in it. This *A* is neither +*A* nor −*A*, and is equally well +*A* as −*A*… The something itself, therefore, is the third which was supposed to be excluded." Second: that substrate, pressed, "when taken more profoundly, is the unity of reflection into which the opposition withdraws as into ground" (*SL* §954). Contradiction, correspondingly, deserves a law of its own — "*everything is inherently contradictory*" — and this law "in contrast to the others expresses rather the *truth and the essential nature of things*" (*SL* §955). The horror of it is misplaced, because against contradiction "identity is merely the determination of the simple immediate, of dead being; but contradiction is the *root of all movement and vitality*; it is only in so far as something has a contradiction within it that it moves, has an urge and activity" (*SL* §956). Hence the famous formula: "Something moves, not because at one moment it is here and at another there, but because at one and the same moment it is here and not here, because in this 'here', it at once is and is not" — the paradoxes of motion the ancient dialecticians pressed (Zeno above all, though Hegel does not name him here) resolved not by denying the contradiction but by granting it: "it does not follow that therefore there is no motion, but on the contrary, that motion is *existent* contradiction itself" (*SL* §958). Everything finite, everything living, everything that changes, harbours contradiction within it; that is why it does not rest. **Something is alive only in so far as it contains contradiction within it, and moreover is this power to hold and endure the contradiction within it** (*SL* §959) — and speculative thinking is nothing else than "that thought holds fast contradiction, and in it, its own self, but does not allow itself to be dominated by it" (*SL* §959).

But contradiction is not the last word — it is precisely what *cannot* be a last word, and its unrest is the proof. Hegel opens the resolution with the flat announcement "*Contradiction resolves itself*," and describes the first result unsparingly: positive and negative, each sublating itself, yield "*this ceaseless vanishing of the opposites into themselves*," which "is *the first unity* resulting from contradiction; it is the null" (*SL* §939). The opposed sides, each excluding itself in excluding the other, **cannot subsist**; they collapse. Yet what they collapse *into* is not blank nothing — "contradiction contains not merely the negative, but also the positive… the result of contradiction is not merely a nullity" (*SL* §940). In perishing, each side negates itself, and the negation of the self-excluding opposition is the **unity** that was, all along, sustaining it — the affirmative result in which the opposed determinations are held as *sublated* moments. The German says it in one phrase Hegel cannot resist: the contradictory sides **go to ground** — *zu Grunde gehen*, at once "to perish" and "to go to the **ground**"; Miller preserves the pun by glossing that opposition "is not only *destroyed* (*zugrunde gegangen*) but has withdrawn *into its ground*" (*SL* §943), and that each opposite, sublating itself, is "*falling to the ground* (*zugrunde geht*); but in this process it at the same time only unites with itself" (*SL* §945). Contradiction resolved is **Ground** (*Grund*): "The resolved contradiction is therefore ground, essence as unity of the positive and negative," in which "opposition and its contradiction is… as much abolished as preserved" (*SL* §945) — the deeper unity from which the opposed determinations issue and which holds them together. With this the determinations of reflection have completed themselves — identity into difference, difference into opposition, opposition into contradiction, contradiction into ground — and essence has its first form of genuine **mediation**.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is the incoherence-misreading**: that Hegel "denies the law of non-contradiction," licenses asserting *P* and *not-P* together, and so forfeits the right to reason at all. This mistakes dialectical contradiction for the formal contradiction of a careless proof. Hegel is not affirming a flat conjunction *P ∧ ¬P* and resting in it; he is describing an *unstable* structure — determinations that exclude each other yet require each other — whose very instability **drives** it beyond itself into ground. Formal non-contradiction governs fixed determinacies held apart by the understanding, and within that domain it stands. The dialectical claim is about what happens when such determinacies are thought *through*: they do not sit still to be conjoined; they move. Contradiction here is not a proposition to be believed but a *process* to be followed to its resolution. Hegel himself diagnoses why the charge feels compelling: in reflective thinking, "if something has been defined as positive and one moves forward from this basis, then straightway the positive has secretly turned into a negative, and conversely," and "unfamiliarity with their nature imagines this confusion to be an error that ought not to happen, and ascribes it to a subjective mistake" — whereas in truth "this transition also… remains a mere confusion when there is no awareness of the necessity of the transformation" (*SL* §948). The incoherence-reading is precisely that unawareness, made into a principle.

**The second is the empty-tautology misreading**, in two opposite forms. One says: identity is *A = A*, a barren tautology, so the "laws of thought" are trivial and thought must look elsewhere for content. The other says the reverse: that *A = A*, abstract self-sameness, is the supreme and sufficient law of all correct thinking. Both freeze identity as the understanding's inert sameness. The chapter's finding is that identity, taken strictly, is *neither* trivial *nor* sufficient: its own form already performs a difference, and pursued honestly it *yields* difference, opposition, and contradiction. Identity is a real moment — but a moment, whose truth is the concrete whole that includes its other. Hegel's own formulation of the target is unambiguous: "truth is complete only in the unity of identity with difference," so that it "hence consists only in this unity" (*SL* §876).

**The third is the everything-is-relative misreading**: that if all determinations contradict themselves and pass into their opposites, then nothing is fixed, any claim is as good as its negation, and dialectic is a license for slippage. This inverts the result. Contradiction does not dissolve into an indifferent flux where "anything goes"; it resolves **determinately** — *this* opposition goes to *this* ground. The movement is not the loss of all footing but the disclosure of a *deeper* footing, the ground that the fixed determinations presupposed. Dialectic ends one-sided fixities not to abolish truth but to drive it inward, from the immediate to the grounded. And the relatedness in question is the opposite of an anything-goes: it is the strictest constraint there is, since "their truth consists only in their relation to one another, that therefore each in its very Notion contains the other" — a point Hegel rates so highly that "without this knowledge, not a single step can really be taken in philosophy" (*SL* §951).

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **the "laws of thought" have been derived rather than assumed.** What the old logic posited as self-evident axioms — identity, non-contradiction, excluded middle — the *Logic* has located and derived from the **determinations of reflection** — identity, difference, contradiction — each generated from the last, each true only as a one-sided moment of a movement that does not stop at any of them. Hegel's complaint against the tradition is exactly that it never asks after that movement: "the thoughtless consideration of them enumerates them one after the other" (*SL* §868), as though a list were a derivation. This is the promissory note of §20 (VIII) redeemed: the categories the tradition *collected* are now *deduced*, and their notorious insufficiency — that no actual thought ever rests in bare *A = A* — is explained by their place in the sequence rather than papered over.

The second observation is that **contradiction has been rehabilitated as the engine, not the enemy, of thought.** §20 rescued *Schein* from the charge of mere illusion; here the same move rescues *Widerspruch* from the charge of mere error. The understanding treats contradiction as the one thing to be avoided at all costs; the *Logic* shows it to be the one thing that *moves* — the inner unrest without which nothing would pass beyond itself. The pattern, now twice enacted, is the signature of the whole sphere: the determination the understanding throws away as a defect is, read speculatively, the source of life. Hegel demonstrates the same inversion on the stock example of a "fixed opposition": light, "reckoned as the pure positive," in fact "essentially possesses in its infinite expansion and in its power to promote growth and to animate, the nature of absolute negativity," while darkness, "as the non-self-differentiating womb of generation, is the simply self-identical, the positive" — and virtue, far from being merely positive, "is not without conflict; rather is it the supreme, finished conflict… but is *in its own self* opposition and conflict," while it is *innocence*, "being neither good nor evil," that is "neither positive nor negative" (*SL* §950). The supposedly pure positive turns out to be the negative at work; what is genuinely free of opposition turns out to be not virtue but the untested.

The third observation is that **each determination of reflection is double, and that is why it drives.** Every one of them — identity, diversity, positive, negative — is at once a **positedness** (made by reflection, existing only in its relation to its other) and a **reflection-into-self** (standing, claiming to be self-subsistent). This is exactly the hybrid §20 identified in determining reflection. Because each is *both*, none can rest: what is only-through-its-other cannot also be self-standing, and the friction of that doubleness is what carries identity to difference, difference to opposition, opposition to contradiction, and contradiction to ground. The instability is not incidental; it is built into the form of a reflected determinacy as such. Hegel states the mechanism exactly: the sides of the opposition "*destroy themselves* in that they determine themselves as self-identical, yet in this determination are rather the negative, an identity-with-self that is a relation-to-other" (*SL* §941).

## IX. What Comes Next

With the determinations of reflection resolved into their unity, essence has reached its first genuine mediation, and the sphere can develop it:

- **§22 — Ground**: **ground and grounded**, and why each requires the other; the splitting of ground into **form** and **matter**, form and **content**; the **formal**, **real**, and **complete** grounds, and how ground-relation slides toward triviality (the "dormitive virtue" that grounds sleep by a power-to-sleep); the accumulation of **conditions**; and the passage by which, when the conditions are complete, the grounded steps forth immediately as **Existence** (*Existenz*) — essence's first move out of pure inwardness toward **Appearance**.
- **Beyond**: **Appearance** (*Erscheinung*) — the thing and its properties, the world of appearance, appearance and law, the essential relations (whole and parts, force and its expression, inner and outer) — and then **Actuality** (*Wirklichkeit*) — the absolute, the modal categories, substance, causality, reciprocity — where essence and appearance are fully united and the sphere passes, in its turn, into the **Concept**.

## Coda

The old logic wrote its first principles on the wall — *A is A*; nothing both is and is not; either this or not-this — and took them for the unshakable floor of all thinking. We have watched that floor prove to be a stairway. Identity, questioned, could not keep difference out; difference, pursued, sharpened into opposition; opposition, taken at its word, broke into contradiction; and contradiction, far from halting thought, turned out to be the very thing that moves it — and, in moving, to point beneath itself to the ground it had always presupposed. *What the understanding forbids as the death of thought is, for reason, its life; the determinations by which we hold the world apart are the determinations by which the world holds together; and every fixed opposition, followed without fear to its end, does not leave us with rubble but hands us down — zu Grunde — to the ground on which it stood.*

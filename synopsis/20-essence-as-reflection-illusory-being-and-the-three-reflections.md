# Essence as Reflection: Shine and the Three Reflections

*The eleventh installment of Section III — and the first in the **Doctrine of Essence**, read closely. §19 examined Hegel's passage from absolute indifference to **Essence** (*Wesen*): unity and determinations lose their independently immediate standing, while immediacy is retained as a moment of their mediation. This installment develops that intended result, without treating the new chapter as proof of the preceding transition. Essence first seems to confront being as the **essential** confronting the **unessential**; but this restores the immediate opposition essence was to have overcome. Being remains instead as **shine** (*Schein*), a reflected immediacy whose negativity and self-relation are essence's own moments. Their movement is **reflection** (*Reflexion*), not the activity of a hidden substrate or the manufacture of objects by a mind. **Positing** reflection determines immediacy as a moment of its return into itself; in sublating its positing it is also **presupposing**, finding that from which it returns. **External** reflection takes this immediate as independently given. Its own determining and cancellation of that determining, not a reminder of what a prior reflection made, lead to **determining** reflection (*bestimmende Reflexion*). The result is a **determination of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmung*): positedness that also subsists through reflection into itself. This establishes the form of the determinations to be developed, not their completed sequence. **Identity**, **difference**, and **contradiction** remain the work of §21; **ground**, a further determination, the work of §22.*

---

## I. Where We Are: From the Threshold into Essence

The previous installment examined the closing transition of the Doctrine of Being, including the disputed inference from qualitative inseparability to quantitative equilibrium. The result Hegel intends is not another substrate remaining untouched beneath its states. Both the unity and its determinations have their standing through their own mediation; their immediacy is retained as a moment rather than left independently confronting that mediation. Being had already developed mediated relations, but the immediate form of its final unity now becomes the object of negation. **Essence** is this negativity taken explicitly — a negativity "which is not alien to it but is its very own, the infinite movement of being" (*SL* §812): being gone-into-itself, *being that has been* (§19; *SL* §807).

Essence is therefore not a further immediacy lying beyond being, a truer thing reached by leaving being behind. That picture would undo the result under examination. Hegel calls essence "sublated being" (*SL* §819), not to abolish every immediacy but to deny its independently immediate standing. Essence's determining "is neither a becoming nor a transition, nor are the determinations themselves an other as other" (*SL* §813): the distinction is held within the movement that relates essence to itself. The programme is explicit: "at first, essence shines or shows within itself, or is reflection"; only subsequently does it appear and manifest itself (*SL* §816). The present chapter must develop the first of these moments, not import the completed Doctrine of Essence into its beginning.

## II. The Essential and the Unessential — and Why the Distinction Fails

At first essence seems to confront immediate being as the **essential** (*das Wesentliche*) confronting the **unessential** (*das Unwesentliche*). Essence is to be the abiding core; being, the dispensable outside. But taken as one immediate against another, essence is "a specific determinate being confronted by another such" (*SL* §817). The supposed advance beyond being has relapsed into a distinction between beings.

The distinction is also **external and relative**: an outside standpoint selects what shall count as essential, and the same content can change sides with that standpoint (*SL* §821). This does not make every distinction of relevance arbitrary or every scientific isolation of essential factors worthless. It shows why an external selection, as such, cannot establish the essence whose determination is at issue. Even a better criterion of selection would not yet overcome the deeper defect: essence would remain one side of an immediate opposition. Hegel distinguishes this "first negation" from the "absolute negativity of being" (*SL* §822). The first leaves an independently standing other; the second negates that form of independence itself. On the basis of the preceding transition, being cannot now confront essence as another self-subsistent domain. What remains is not an unessential being beside essence, but being with its independent standing sublated: **shine**.

## III. Shine (*Schein*) — Reflected Immediacy

Miller renders *Schein* as *illusory being*; our own prose uses **shine**, keeping it distinct from the later **appearance** (*Erscheinung*). The category does not name a psychological error to be removed. Its being "consists solely in the sublatedness of being, in its nothingness" (*SL* §823). This is not the assertion that nothing exists. It is the determination of immediacy as having no subsistence apart from the negativity through which it is. Hegel calls it "reflected immediacy" — immediacy "which is only by means of its negation" (*SL* §824). Yet shine still seems to have an immediate side independent of essence. Explaining why it does not is the next task.

Hegel places a polemical comparison here. In his assessment, scepticism retains the manifold content of its phenomena as given; Kantian appearance presupposes immediate affections of the subject; Leibniz's monad develops representations from itself without being the power that generates and binds them together. Even where Fichte's obstacle *may* have no underlying thing-in-itself, Hegel argues, its determination retains an immediate limitation over against the ego (*SL* §825). The common objection concerns the standing of determinate content: denying it an independent thing-in-itself has not, by itself, exhibited its mediation. It is not simply an objection to receiving material from outside — Leibniz's representations are already said to arise within the monad.

This is Hegel's criticism, not a sufficient adjudication of all four positions. Leibniz supplies a particularly sharp test. The *Monadology* expressly makes natural changes proceed from an internal principle, identifies appetition as the activity effecting the passage between perceptions, and makes each present state a consequence of its predecessor. It also distinguishes the principle of change from the particular series of changes that constitutes each monad's specific nature ([*Monadology*, §§11–15, 22, Latta translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Monadology_(Leibniz,_tr._Latta))). This distinction gives Hegel's demand a definite target: an internal source of change is not yet an account of why its content is thus determined. Yet his description of representations arising "like bubbles", "indifferent and immediate over against one another" (*SL* §825), threatens to obscure the consequential connection Leibniz does affirm. Our conclusion is bounded on both sides. Leibniz's connection cannot be dismissed as an unconnected succession, but neither does naming its internal principle derive the particular series. Whether Leibniz accounts adequately for that determination, and whether Hegel's objection preserves the connection while establishing its limit, require more than either formulation alone.

## IV. Why the Shine Is Essence's Own

Hegel does not begin this step by proving the whole passage from Being again. He expressly presupposes that being in its totality has withdrawn into essence. What remains to be shown is that shine's apparently distinguishing determinations belong to essence itself, and that their distinction from essence is itself sublated (*SL* §826). The argument therefore depends on the result whose warrant §19 examined; it cannot retrospectively discharge that examination.

Shine has two moments: **negativity** and **reflected immediacy**. Its non-being is essence's negativity, not a second negative domain outside essence. Its immediacy is the self-equality of that negativity: in relating to itself, negativity has the simple self-relation that seemed to belong to being alone. Thus being is preserved, but as a moment; neither the negativity nor the immediacy supplies an independent remainder confronting essence (*SL* §§827–828).

Nor can these moments be fixed apart within essence. The negativity that relates to itself is thereby immediate; yet that immediacy is the self-relation of the negative, not a resting base exempt from negation. Each determination refers back to the movement from which it was distinguished. This is how Hegel sublates the apparent independence of shine without erasing immediacy: shine is essence in a determinateness that is itself only a moment of essence (*SL* §§829–831). His conclusion is that "essence in this its self-movement is reflection" (*SL* §832).

**Reflection** (*Reflexion*) is not another entity added to shine. Shine is "reflection as immediate" (*SL* §833); reflection makes explicit the movement contained in that immediacy. There is no first substrate which then undergoes the movement: immediacy is the equality with itself of self-related negation (*SL* §834). This distinguishes reflection from passing-over without importing a physical model of a ray, a source, and a mirror. The movement must now be differentiated into **positing**, **external**, and **determining** reflection (*SL* §836).

## V. Positing Reflection — and Its Presupposing

Take self-related negativity first as the movement that returns into itself. Its coincidence with itself is simple equality-with-self, and therefore immediacy. But this is the equality of the negative with itself: it does not leave negativity behind and become a new affirmative base. The immediacy is itself self-negating, only as the return from a negative (*SL* §§838–840). Hegel calls this **positedness**: "immediacy purely and simply as determinateness or as self-reflecting" (*SL* §840). **Positing reflection** (*setzende Reflexion*) determines immediacy in this dependent standing; it does not manufacture an object that subsequently happens to look given.

But positing cannot remain simply the affirmation that everything immediate is posited. The return is the sublating of the negative; its own immediacy, being self-negating, is itself what is sublated. Reflection thus negates its own positing and relates to itself as that from which it returns. That is **presupposing**: "since in its positing it sublates its positing, it is a presupposing" (*SL* §841). Reflection does not acquire an outside starting point to rescue an otherwise empty movement. The starting point belongs to the movement's own self-repulsion, its distinguishing from itself that which it sublates.

The finding is consequently genuine, not a mistake later corrected by remembering an earlier act of production: "reflection therefore finds before it an immediate which it transcends and from which it is the return." Yet "what is thus found only comes to be through being left behind" (*SL* §842). Positing and presupposing are distinguishable moments of one movement, not successive events. Reflection returns from an immediate whose standing as its presupposition is established in that return; Hegel calls this "an absolute recoil upon itself" (*SL* §843). Positing reflection presupposes only shine, only positedness (*SL* §846). Its positing has no presupposition in the sense that its determination is not put in the place of an independently immediate other; it is therefore not yet completed determining reflection (*SL* §853). The contrast concerns the standing of the presupposition, not the absence of presupposing from the movement.

The transition to external reflection follows from the distinction just made. The presupposed immediate is a moment of reflection; but it is also determined as reflection's negative, as an other immediately opposed to it. Taken on this side, reflection starts from the immediate as its other (*SL* §845). The new form does not arise because reflection has forgotten a fact about its past. It arises because its own presupposing has given immediacy this opposed standing.

## VI. External Reflection and Determining Reflection

**External reflection** (*äußere Reflexion*) takes the immediate not merely as positedness, but as self-relation, something from which reflection starts and to which its determining is external. It finds an immediate presupposition; the fact that this is posited does not concern it (*SL* §§846–847). Immediacy belongs to one side, reflective determination to the other, and the determinate immediate connects them without yet overcoming their separation (*SL* §848).

The advance must be made through **external reflection's own activity**, not through our reminder of what positing reflection has already shown. External reflection determines the immediate as its negative or other. But it also presupposes the immediate as prior to this determining; it therefore sublates its own positing. In negating that determining, however, reflection is not simply absent: it is itself establishing the immediate's standing over against it. Its cancellation of its own determination is again a positing, a relation to its own negative. Hegel's conclusion is that the immediate and reflection are not merely identical *for us*: their identity is now **posited** through this movement itself. The externality is sublated; reflection proves to be immanent to the immediacy from which it began (*SL* §849). What is established is not the temporal priority of a producer, but the mediation of the two sides that were supposed to be independently immediate and merely external.

The Remark gives this argument a more familiar setting. Hegel takes Kant's reflecting judgment — the search for a universal when the particular is given — as an example of external reflection. But he immediately adds that external reflection implicitly contains the notion of absolute reflection: the universal, rule, or law sought counts as the immediate's own essence, not as an indifferent addition to it (*SL* §§850–851). The positive point must be retained alongside the limitation. A merely formal activity receiving all its content from outside cannot account for that immanent standing by calling itself reflection; neither does its external form make every result worthless. Hegel reports with distance the fashion of blaming reflection for everything, but concedes that intellectual reflection, insofar as it is external, does begin from a given alien to it. His further concern is the resistance of reflected determinations which appear independently fixed even while presupposing one another (*SL* §852).

**Determining reflection** (*bestimmende Reflexion*) is "the unity of positing and external reflection" (*SL* §853). Positedness alone is not yet its result. As mere positedness, a determination has its standing only in the return into essence; it is not itself reflection-into-self. United with the presupposing moment of external reflection, it is also reflected into itself: the determination is not only sublated in essence but has its own self-relation (*SL* §§853–855). This is a **determination of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmung*).

Its subsistence differs from that of a quality. It persists "not through being but through its equality with itself" (*SL* §856): the negative is maintained through reflected self-relation, not supported by an immediate being opposed to its negation. Hegel distinguishes its two sides — **positedness** and **reflection-into-self** — and joins them in the result: "the determinateness of reflection is the relation to its otherness within itself" (*SL* §§858–859). Its relation to other is internal to what it is.

This is a definite result, but not yet the derivation of identity, difference, and contradiction in their particular forms. Hegel's next chapter announces their development through diversity and opposition to contradiction and ground (*SL* §§860–861). Naming the two sides of reflected determinacy does not perform that development. In particular, mediated self-subsistence is not by itself the specific self-exclusion which §21 must establish as contradiction.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is the sham-misreading**: shine is a subjective error to be cleared away so that a true being behind it can be seen. This reinstates the independent immediacy whose sublation the chapter presupposes. Shine is essence's reflected immediacy, not a veil added to it. That logical claim neither denies the existence of empirical error nor certifies the truth of whatever appears to us; it concerns the standing of the immediate in relation to essence (*SL* §§823–831).

**The second is the psychological misreading**: reflection is an act of a mind performed upon a given, or a mind's production of that given. Hegel explicitly distinguishes his subject from reflection at the level of consciousness or understanding: he is considering "reflection generally" (*SL* §851). The claim concerns the objective significance of the determinations, not merely our procedure for arranging representations (§06). Nor does *posited* mean inferior because merely subjective. Hegel distinguishes two comparisons: positedness is superior to bare determinate being because it makes the latter's negative, mediated standing explicit; relative to essence, however, it is still *only* positedness, not the accomplished return into itself (*SL* §854). The word has no warrant for meaning either arbitrary invention or completed truth.

**The third is the hidden-thing misreading**: essence is an independently resting thing that has reflection as its activity. Hegel's argument is instead that essence **is** this self-related movement, not a substrate surviving untouched underneath it (*SL* §§832–835). The later categories of thing, force, and substance must still be developed and their claims to independence examined (§§23–26). They are not disposed of in advance. The task is to exhibit what their forms establish, what remains external, and how their determinations are preserved in the passage beyond them.

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **the new mode of movement has been articulated, not merely renamed**. Essential and unessential fail because their immediate opposition contradicts the result with which the chapter begins. Shine's two moments prove to be essence's own; positing proves to presuppose; external reflection's own activity sublates its externality. Each advance has its particular work to do. Mediation and positedness were already present in the Doctrine of Being (§19); what is different here is their explicit form as reflection, the relation to other held within self-relation.

The second observation is that **a critique of givenness must distinguish its tasks**. Hegel's comparison with scepticism and idealism asks whether determinate content has been mediated, not merely relocated. His own argument must meet the same demand: the words *internal principle*, *positing*, or *essence* cannot stand in for the connection they name. Our Leibniz comparison retains both the connection the polemical image threatens to lose and the distinction between a principle of change and its particular series, which gives Hegel's demand its target. For scientific inquiry, our methodological inference is correspondingly limited: finding and classifying material are achievements, but the claim that a discovered universal expresses the object's own determination requires its particular warrant. The logical passage through external reflection supplies neither that warrant for every classification nor the concrete law and its constants.

The third observation is that **the form of the determinations of reflection has been developed; their sequence remains to be developed**. Reflected determinations can appear self-contained precisely because their self-relation gives them a standing beyond mere transience. Hegel describes them as "free essentialities floating in the void without attracting or repelling one another" (*SL* §857). But their positedness and relation to other remain within that self-relation. Following how this structure is determined as identity, difference, diversity, opposition, and contradiction is the next task. The old laws of thought have not already received their derivation merely because we can announce the categories to which Hegel will relate them.

## IX. What Comes Next

- **§21 — the Determinations of Reflection**: identity and its relation to difference; difference developed through diversity and opposition; contradiction and its resolution into ground. Hegel's announcement of this order (*SL* §861) must be distinguished from the arguments that establish it.
- **§22 — Ground**: the result of that development, articulated through form, essence, matter, and content; determinate ground and condition; the emergence into Existence. Ground is not a name we can substitute for essence at the outset.
- **Beyond**: thing and properties, appearance and law, the essential relation, and actuality (§§23–26). These give determinate content to the programme in which essence first reflects within itself, then appears, then manifests itself (*SL* §816).

## Coda

We began with essence apparently standing opposite being, as a privileged core opposite an inessential remainder. That division could not sustain the result it was meant to express. What seemed left over as shine proved to have essence's own negativity and reflected immediacy; their movement distinguished positing from presupposing and overcame their external opposition without erasing either moment. The result is neither an untouched thing behind its display nor a display made by a spectator. It is the determination whose standing is a self-relation containing its relation to other. *Reflection has made this form explicit. It has not relieved us of the work of following each determination through its own necessity.*

# Essence as Reflection: Illusory Being and the Three Reflections

*The eleventh installment of Section III — and the first in the **Doctrine of Essence**, read closely. §19 brought Being to its own dissolution: its determinations proved to be **posited**, not immediate, and Being withdrew into its **ground** — into **Essence** (*Wesen*), being that *has been* (*gewesen*). This installment takes the first step inside. Essence is at first only the **negativity** that drove all of Being's passing-over, now taken for itself; and its first move is to set the whole realm of being over against itself as the **unessential** — a distinction that, made externally, at once collapses. What is left of being within essence is not a second world but a mere **illusory being** (*Schein*): an immediacy that *is* only as **sublated**, a being whose whole being is its own negatedness. Yet this shine is not a foreign sham smuggled in from outside; it is **essence's own shining within itself** — and that shining, the movement of the negative relating to nothing but itself, is **reflection** (*Reflexion*). Reflection unfolds in three moments: **positing** reflection, essence's self-movement that posits its own immediacy; **external** reflection, which presupposes a given immediate and relates to it as something *found* (the reflection of the understanding); and **determining** reflection, their unity, in which reflection posits its own presupposition and the determinacies it seemed to find prove to be its own — the threshold of the **determinations of reflection** (identity, difference, contradiction) that §21 will develop. Here the new logic shows its full face: not *passing-over* (*Übergehen*) but *shining-into* (*Scheinen*); not a sequence of immediacies but a self-relating movement that stays with itself in its other.*

---

## I. Where We Are: From the Threshold into Essence

The previous installment closed the Doctrine of Being by sublating it whole. Each determination of Being — quality, quantity, measure — had counted as something *immediately given*; pressed, each undid its own immediacy and disclosed that it was only **posited**, having being solely through its own negation. What did not vanish in all that passing-over was the **negativity** that drove it — the inner mediation always at work behind the surface of immediacy — a negativity "which is not alien to it but is its very own, the infinite movement of being" (*SL* §812). **Essence** is that negativity taken now as a determination in its own right: being gone-into-itself, *being that has been* (§19; *SL* §807).

That gives essence its peculiar starting position, and the whole difficulty of this sphere with it. Essence "stands between *being* and *Notion*"; it is their mean, and its movement is the transition from the one into the other (*SL* §815). Essence is not a *further* immediacy lying beyond being, a new and truer thing one reaches by leaving the world of being behind — that two-worlds picture §19 already forbade, and the path into essence "is the movement of being itself" (*SL* §809). Essence *is* the negation of immediacy: it is "sublated being" (*SL* §819), having being only as the sublatedness of being. So essence cannot simply *be*, the way a quality or a quantum was; it can only **relate** — to the being it has negated, to itself as that negating. The categories of this sphere will therefore never stand alone as Being's did. Essence's determining, Hegel warns, "is neither a becoming nor a transition, nor are the determinations themselves an *other* as other" (*SL* §813): each is what it is only through its other, into which it **shines** rather than passes. The task of the present installment is to watch essence take its very first step on this ground: to see how the being it has negated returns as mere **shine**, and how the movement of that shine is the **reflection** that will generate everything to follow — for "at first, essence *shines* or *shows within itself*, or is reflection," and only "secondly, it appears; thirdly, it manifests itself" (*SL* §816).

## II. The Essential and the Unessential — and Why the Distinction Fails

Essence's first gesture is the natural one, the one ordinary thought makes whenever it reaches for "the essential." Having negated being, essence sets itself over against it: essence is the true, the abiding, the **essential** (*das Wesentliche*); the immediate world of being, by contrast, is the **unessential** (*das Unwesentliche*) — the inessential surface, the mere outside, behind which the essence is supposed to lie. Essence taken this way is essence taken "at first as an immediacy": "a specific determinate being confronted by another such," and so "only *essential,* as opposed to *unessential,* determinate being" (*SL* §817). This is the standpoint of every search for a hidden core: strip away the inessential accidents, and the essence will be left.

But the distinction, made this way, does not hold, and its failure is instructive. To divide a field into the essential and the unessential, *something* must do the dividing — must mark *this* as core and *that* as husk. And that sorting is done from **outside**: the distinction "is *externally* posited… a division which has its origin in a *third*. Such a division does not settle what is essential and what is unessential. It originates in some external standpoint and consideration and the same content can therefore be regarded now as essential and again as unessential" (*SL* §821). Nothing in the material itself says which side is which: the "unessential" is unessential only *relative to* an essence picked out by someone's interest; shift the interest and the sorting shifts. So the essential/unessential distinction is **external and relative** — it falls outside the essence it claims to isolate, and therefore fails to be a distinction *of essence at all*. Worse, it costs essence its standing: the distinction "has caused essence to relapse into the sphere of *determinate being*" (*SL* §821). Essence, if it is to be the inner truth and not one more externally-imposed label, cannot stand merely *opposed* to the unessential as one side against another; for so opposed, "essence is not strictly essence but only a differently determined being, the *essential*" (*SL* §820) — a relative term, just another being. The first move overshoots and collapses — and the decisive reason is not that the sorting was arbitrary, for arbitrariness alone would license nothing worse than a demand for a *better*, internal criterion. The reason is that this whole way of taking essence measures it too small. Characterised as essential only relatively to an unessential, essence "is only the *first negation,* or the negation which is a *determinateness*"; but "essence is the **absolute negativity** of being" (*SL* §822). A first negation leaves its other standing *as* an other, with a being of its own — which is exactly what "unessential" says. Absolute negativity leaves no such other standing. So what essence negates is not an unessential *other* beside it, but its own negated being — and that negated being, held within essence, is not the *unessential* but "the immediate that is *in and for itself* a nullity… only a *non-essence, illusory* being" — the **shine** (*Schein*) (*SL* §822).

## III. Illusory Being (*Schein*) — the Being That Remains Within Essence

Here is the chapter's pivotal and most easily misread category. When essence negates being, being is not simply annihilated; an immediacy *remains* — but an immediacy that now *is* only as negated, only as a moment held within essence. That remainder is **illusory being**, *Schein*. Its whole being "consists solely in the sublatedness of being, in its nothingness" (*SL* §823): it is, precisely, a non-being that yet shows; an immediacy that has no subsistence of its own but only the borrowed standing of something already negated. Hegel's formula is exact and worth slowing over: *Schein* is "*reflected* immediacy" (*SL* §824) — immediacy which "*is* only by means of its negation" and which, set against its mediation, "is nothing but the empty determination of the immediacy of negated determinate being." It shines; it does not stand.

The word must be rescued from the very meaning it first suggests. *Schein* — "shine," "show," "semblance," "illusory being" — sounds like *sham*, mere subjective illusion, error to be cleared away; and philosophy has long handled it at arm's length. Ancient **skepticism** reduced the world to bare appearances and *suspended* assent about anything beyond them: it "did not permit itself to say 'It is'," yet it "permits the content of its illusory being to be *given* to it" — keeping the shine, but as an immediate simply received, never as anything's own act (*SL* §825). **Leibniz** has the monad evolve its representations out of itself, and yet, Hegel objects, "it is not the power that generates and binds them together, rather do they arise in the monad like bubbles; they are indifferent and immediate over against one another" (*SL* §825) — a content the monad *finds* in itself rather than posits. **Kant** would not let the thing-in-itself enter knowledge, so that "the Kantian Appearance is a *given* content of perception; it presupposes affections, determinations of the subject" — immediacy over again, now lodged in the subject (*SL* §825). (Kant's own *transcendental* illusion, the *Schein* native to reason and to be exposed but never dissolved, is a different and narrower thing; it is not the charge Hegel presses here.) Even **Fichte** — and Hegel keeps the concession conditional, "it *may well be* that the infinite obstacle of Fichte's idealism has no underlying thing-in-itself" — leaves the ego's obstacle as "at the same time *immediate*, a *limitation* of the ego," which it may transcend but not derive (*SL* §825). Hegel gathers them for a single verdict: "the various forms of idealism, Leibnizian, Kantian, Fichtean, and others, have not advanced beyond being as determinateness, have not advanced beyond this immediacy, any more than scepticism did" (*SL* §825). In each the shine is still held *apart* from essence — its content received rather than posited — never as essence's own self-showing. He denies the apartness. The shine is not a deceptive skin over a hidden true thing; *there is no hidden thing behind it*. The immediacy that essence negated and yet retains is essence's *own* immediacy, the only immediacy it has. **The shine belongs to essence itself.**

## IV. The Shine Is Essence's Own — and Its Movement Is Reflection

Grant that the shine is essence's own, and the whole picture turns inside out. If *Schein* were the surface of some inner thing, then essence would be that thing and the shine its outer display — and we would be back in the two worlds. Hegel sets the task precisely: "all that has to be shown is that the determinations which distinguish it from essence are determinations of essence itself" (*SL* §826). But essence is precisely *not* a thing; it is the negating, the sublatedness, the pure self-relating negativity. So the shine is not essence's *outside*; it is essence *showing within itself*: "the illusory being in essence is not the illusory being of an other, but is illusory being *per se*, the illusory being of essence itself" (*SL* §828). What shines, shines *in* essence and *as* essence — essence distinguishing itself from itself only to find, in the distinguished, nothing but itself, for "illusory being is essence itself in the determinateness of being" (*SL* §829).

That self-showing of the negative within itself has a name: **reflection** (*Reflexion*) — "essence in this its self-movement is *reflection*" (*SL* §832). Shine and reflection are not two things: "illusory being is the same thing as *reflection*; but it is reflection as *immediate*" (*SL* §833). That qualifier is a verdict, not a synonym. Shine is reflection *not yet grasped as movement* — and the deficiency is what drives the advance, since the shine's whole being consists in its own nothingness and therefore cannot be a resting state at all, but only a self-negating. The word's metaphor is optical, and Hegel marks it as borrowed: for illusory being withdrawn into itself "we have the **foreign word** *reflection*" (*SL* §833). Taken with that warning it still helps. Light *reflected* does not pass on into another medium and vanish, as Being's determinations passed over; it is turned back, bent into itself. But the analogy fails at the precise point where optics needs a lamp and a mirror — a source that emits and a surface that returns. Essence has neither, and Hegel forecloses both: this immediacy "is consequently **not a first from which the beginning was made**… nor is it an affirmatively present **substrate** that moves through reflection; on the contrary, immediacy is only this movement itself" (*SL* §834). Discard the source and the surface, keep the bending-back. So with essence: its movement is not transition into an other that it becomes and is lost in, but "the movement of becoming and transition that remains internal to it" (*SL* §834) — a **bending-back**, indeed "an *absolute recoil* upon itself" (*SL* §843), in which it remains with itself throughout. Reflection is the *Schein* in motion — the shine grasped not as a static remainder but as the very activity of essence reflecting itself. And because it is a *movement*, it has moments; reflection is not one act but a structure of three — positing, external, determining (*SL* §836) — and getting that structure right is the whole technical labour of the chapter.

## V. Positing Reflection — Essence's Self-Movement

Take reflection first in its purest form, as essence's own self-movement, before anything at all is treated as given to it. Essence is absolute negativity: the negative that negates *itself*. But a negation that negates itself does not thereby fall into blank nothing; in negating its own negating, it relates the negative to the negative and so returns — it is "the *movement of nothing to nothing, and so back to itself*" (*SL* §835). What this self-negating movement *produces* is an immediacy — but an immediacy that is sheerly **posited** (*gesetzt*): "*posited being or positedness,* immediacy purely and simply as *determinateness* or as self-reflecting" (*SL* §840), a being that is only its own non-being, a *Schein*. This is **positing reflection** (*setzende Reflexion*): essence positing the shine as its own, the negative bending back and, in the bending, giving itself the look of an immediate.

The decisive point — and the seed of everything that follows — is that the immediacy here is *not found but made*. There is no given starting point that reflection then works upon: "this immediacy, instead of being able to form the starting point is, on the contrary, immediacy only as the return or as reflection itself" (*SL* §840). The "immediate" is reflection's *own* deposit, a positedness through and through. Positing reflection is reflection that starts "from nothing," in which "the positing has no presupposition" (*SL* §853): it is purely the self-moving negativity, and what looks in it like an immediate given is only the turning-back caught at the moment it appears to come to rest. Were this the whole of reflection, essence would be a closed self-relation with no purchase on anything. But positing reflection does not stay pure, and it is Hegel, not a later objection, who breaks it open. Reflection is a sublating of the negative, and "reflection, as sublating the negative, is a sublating of *its other*, of immediacy"; so in sublating its own positing it *presupposes* — "it therefore sublates its positing, and since in its positing it sublates its positing, **it is a presupposing**" (*SL* §841). Hence "reflection therefore **finds before it** an immediate which it transcends and from which it is the return"; and yet "what is thus found only comes to be through being left behind" (*SL* §842). This is why the movement must be read as "an *absolute recoil* upon itself" — one that "comes from itself in so far as positing reflection is **presupposing**, but, as presupposing reflection, is simply **positing** reflection" (*SL* §843). Positing and presupposing are one act told from two ends; "the return of essence is its **self-repulsion**" (*SL* §841). The "no presupposition" of §853 is therefore a *comparative* remark, true of positing reflection only as set beside external reflection; taken by itself, positing reflection is already its own presupposing. What comes next is that self-repulsion stepping forward under its own name. The very immediacy reflection posits carries a second aspect — it is "determined as negative, as immediately *opposed* to something, therefore to an other" — and by that aspect reflection "starts from the immediate as its other" and so "is *external reflection*" (*SL* §845).

## VI. External Reflection, and Its Collapse into Determining Reflection

For the posited immediacy is genuinely *found* — reflection finds it before it in the strong sense §V has just established, not merely seems to; what deceives is never the finding but the supposition that what is found was not also posited. Reflection taken at *this* face is **external reflection** (*äußere Reflexion*): reflection that **presupposes** a being and "therefore *finds* this before it as something from which it starts" (*SL* §847), relating to it from outside — comparing it, subsuming it under universals, drawing out its determinations as though they were waiting to be read off. Hegel's own example is Kant's *reflective* judgement, which, "if only the particular is given *for which the universal is to be found*," goes in search of that universal; and the verdict is flat — it "is clearly also only *external* reflection, which is related to the immediate as to something given" (*SL* §§850–851). This is the reflection of ordinary understanding, "usually taken in a *subjective* sense as the movement of the faculty of judgement" (*SL* §850), the everyday work of *reflecting on* a given: the datum is assumed, and thought plays over it externally, referring it to laws, genera, grounds it does not take to be its own doing. External reflection is indispensable and ubiquitous — it is what "reflection" usually means, and Hegel expressly declines to join the fashion that "ascribed everything bad to reflection generally" (*SL* §852) — and it is *one-sided*, because it forgets where its "given" came from.

And here the two collapse into one. External reflection presupposes an immediate as found; but that immediate, we have just seen, is nothing but what **positing reflection laid down**. The "given" external reflection begins from is its *own* presupposition — posited by reflection and then taken as if found. So external reflection, followed through, sublates its own externality: "the fact is, therefore, that external reflection is not external, but is no less the immanent reflection of immediacy itself… Reflection is thus *determining reflection*" (*SL* §849). Its presupposing *is* a positing, and the immediate it leans on is reflection's own product met coming back. The truth of the two together is **determining reflection** (*bestimmende Reflexion*), "in general the unity of *positing* and *external* reflection" (*SL* §853): reflection that **posits its own presupposition** — in which the determinacy it finds is the very determinacy it has posited, and the determinacy it posits subsists, over against it, with the standing of something found. The product of determining reflection is therefore a peculiar hybrid: a **positedness that is at the same time a reflection-into-self**, a shine that has stiffened into a *fixed* determinacy holding firm on its own. Such a determinacy — a positedness that nonetheless stands like a being — is a **determination of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmung*): "positedness is, as such, negation; but, as presupposed, it is also reflected into itself" (*SL* §855). Unlike a quality, which is "unequal within itself and hence a transitory moment vanishing in the other," it "persists not through *being* but through its equality with itself" (*SL* §856). And this hybrid is not a curiosity to be noted and passed; it is a demand, and it is already legible. "In the determination of reflection… there are **two sides** which at first are distinguished from one another. First, the determination is **positedness**, negation as such; secondly, it is **reflection-into-self**" (*SL* §858). Take the second side by itself and you have simple self-relation — **identity**. Take the first by itself and you have negation, being-other — **difference**. But neither can be taken by itself, for they are two sides of *one* determination; and so "the determinateness of reflection is **the relation to its otherness within itself**" (*SL* §859). That is precisely what divides it from quality, which "through its relation, passes over into an other," whereas the determination of reflection "has taken its otherness back into itself" and keeps its own negation at home. A determinacy whose subsistence consists in holding its own negation *is* a **contradiction**. So what the chapter hands to the next is not a list but a compulsion: identity, difference, contradiction (*SL* §861) — the determinations in which reflection's self-positing shine first congeals into the categories the old logic called the laws of thought.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is the sham-misreading**: that *Schein* means mere illusion — a subjective error, a false appearance to be cleared away so the truth behind it can be seen. This is the reading the word invites and the one the chapter is built to defeat. The shine is not the false *over against* a truth lying elsewhere; *there is no elsewhere*. "The immediacy of the determinateness in illusory being over against essence is consequently nothing other than essence's own immediacy" (*SL* §827) — the only immediacy essence has. To treat *Schein* as removable illusion is a further form of the **apartness** §III diagnosed — holding the shine off from essence as something merely given or referred elsewhere (*SL* §825) — and so keeping the two worlds the whole sphere exists to abolish.

**The second is the psychological misreading**: that "reflection" names an act of *ours* — a thinker turning thought back on a topic, a mental operation performed by a subject on an object. That is **external** reflection mistaken for the whole, and lifted out of the Logic into psychology besides. Hegel forecloses it in one line: "what is under discussion here is neither reflection at the level of consciousness, nor the more specific reflection of the understanding… but of reflection generally" (*SL* §851). The reflection at issue is not something a mind does to a given; it is the self-movement of essence itself — the negative relating to itself, "the showing of the illusory being of essence within essence itself" (*SL* §860). Note the exact shape of Hegel's correction. He does not set an *objective* reflection over against a *subjective* one; he sets **reflection generally** above two of its narrower forms — his subject is "neither reflection at the level of consciousness, nor the more specific reflection of the understanding… but of *reflection generally*" (*SL* §851). Our subjective reflecting is accordingly not demoted to an afterthought: external reflection is a moment the chapter *derives*, and §VI has just shown it indispensable. What is refused is the identification of the whole with that one moment. Hegel administers the same correction to the term that most invites it: when a determination is called "only a positedness," this is commonly "ascribed to external reflection, to the subjective side. **But in fact positedness is the superior**" (*SL* §854). Hegel is describing the structure of the thing, not the habits of the thinker.

**The third is the hidden-thing misreading**: that behind the shine sits an essence-substance, a true *Ding* of which the shine is the display — so that essence after all is a being, merely a deeper one. Hegel concedes the appearance that invites it — illusory being "seems still to have an immediate side that is independent of essence and to be simply an other of essence" (*SL* §824) — and then removes it. The misreading collapses essence back into the very immediacy it is the negation of. Essence is **not** a thing that shines; it is the *shining itself*, the self-relating negativity: "illusory being, therefore, is essence itself, but essence in a determinateness… and *essence* is the reflection of itself within itself" (*SL* §831). The category that will keep tempting us to re-install a hidden substrate — first as *thing*, later as *force*, later as *substance* — is exactly what the Doctrine of Essence will, step by step, dissolve.

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **the new mode of the dialectic is now fully in force.** §19 announced the change — *passing-over* gives way to *shining-into* — but announced it from the far shore of Being. Here it is enacted. Nothing in this installment *passed over* into something else and vanished; the essential/unessential, the shine, the three reflections all **bent back** into essence, each the other's own moment — essence's determining being "neither a becoming nor a transition" but a movement "that remains internal to it" (*SL* §§813, 834). Reflection is that bending-back made the explicit law of movement. One can feel the difference in the very awkwardness of the prose: Being's categories could be lined up; Essence's must be said twice, each through its other, because neither *is* apart from the relating.

The second observation is that **a discredited category has been rehabilitated — and a method of rehabilitation shown.** *Schein* entered as the name for sham. What had to be overturned, though, was not a wish to be rid of the shine — the philosophers of §III all *kept* it, and the sceptic's kept it with "the entire manifold wealth of the world" inside — but the habit of leaving it *immediate*, a content held off from essence and referred elsewhere (*SL* §825). The chapter does not defend illusion; it shows that what looked like mere illusion, followed honestly, is essence's own self-showing (*SL* §§822, 828) — that the negative, taken as *belonging* rather than as foreign, is productive. This is the same move that will, in §21, rescue **contradiction**, which the old logic ranked "a contingency, a kind of abnormality and a passing paroxysm or sickness" (*SL* §957), and which Hegel makes "the root of all movement and vitality" (*SL* §956). The pattern is worth naming: the determinations the understanding throws away as defects are, read dialectically, the engine.

The third observation is that **the "laws of thought" have been given a derivation, not a list.** Identity, difference, contradiction were handed down by the old logic as fixed axioms, externally collected. We can now see where they come from: they are **determinations of reflection** — the points at which essence's self-positing shine stiffens into a fixed determinacy, appearing thereafter "as free essentialities floating in the void without attracting or repelling one another" (*SL* §857). That they are *posited* (made by reflection) and yet *subsist* (stand like beings) is the whole secret of their later instability: having "the form of being-in-and-for-self," they "count as *essential*, and instead of passing over into their opposites they appear rather as absolute, free, and indifferent towards each other" (*SL* §852). The next installment cashes this out.

## IX. What Comes Next

With reflection in hand, essence can begin to generate its determinate content:

- **§21 — the Determinations of Reflection**: **identity** — "simple self-relation, pure *identity*," which, Hegel at once adds, "as such it is rather the absence of any determination" (*SL* §861) — and why "A = A," taken alone, is "nothing more than the expression of an empty *tautology*," a law of thought that "has *no content* and leads no further" (*SL* §875), so that what it really shows is "that this identity is nothing, that it is *negativity*, the absolute difference from itself" (*SL* §881); **difference** (through *diversity*, whose law "*all things are different*" is opposed to the law of identity in declaring "A is *distinctive*, therefore A is also *not A*," *SL* §903 — and *opposition*, driving into **contradiction**), and the rehabilitation of contradiction as the inner pulse of essence rather than a fault to be avoided — the determinations driving themselves toward their **ground**.
- **§22 — Ground**: contradiction as that in which "the opposition is reflected into itself and withdrawn into its *ground*" (*SL* §861); ground and grounded; the splitting of ground into form, matter, and content; the accumulation of **conditions**; and the passage by which, when the conditions are complete, the grounded steps forth immediately as **Existence** (*SL* §§1033–1035) — essence's first move out of pure inwardness toward **Appearance**.
- **Beyond**: **Appearance** (*Erscheinung*) — the thing and its properties (*SL* §§1036–1039), appearance and law (*SL* §1063), the essential relations (*SL* §§1085–1087) — and then **Actuality** (*Wirklichkeit*) — the absolute, the modal categories, substance, causality, reciprocity — where essence and appearance are fully united and the sphere passes, in its turn, into the **Concept**: the three steps announced at the outset, in which essence *shines*, then *appears*, then *manifests* itself (*SL* §816).

## Coda

Being had let us stand on a surface and watch each foothold give way to the next; Essence sends us beneath the surface, only to teach us that there is no second floor — that the "depth" is the surface bent back on itself, knowing itself as posited. We came looking for the essential behind the inessential and found that the division could not be drawn from outside; we came looking for the thing behind its shine and found that the shine was essence's own, and the looking-behind was essence reflecting itself — essence "contains the illusory being within itself as the infinite immanent movement that determines its immediacy as negativity and its negativity as immediacy, and is thus the reflection of itself within itself" (*SL* §832). *What the Doctrine of Being met as immediacy, the Doctrine of Essence meets again as posited — and the whole art of this sphere is to hold what merely shines and what truly grounds in one thought, never letting the shine harden into a hidden thing, nor the ground evaporate into a mere show; for here, for the first time, nothing simply is — everything is only through its other, shining within it, and turning back.*

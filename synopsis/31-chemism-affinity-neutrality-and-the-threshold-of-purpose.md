# Chemism: Affinity, Neutrality, and the Threshold of Purpose

*The twenty-second installment of Section III — the fifth in the **Doctrine of the Concept**, and the second in **Objectivity**. §30 reached a law immanent in the system, while the objects' specific natures still appeared indifferent to the relation sustaining them. **Chemism** (*Chemismus*) makes that relation their own determinateness. Each object is a totality, yet exists as only one side of the total relation: its **affinity** towards its other is therefore grounded in a tension with itself. The **chemical process** first resolves this tension in a **neutral product**. But the unity achieved is not yet the principle of its own differentiation; renewed separation has an external condition. The development does not end with this alternation. Separation reaches indifferent elementary bases, and their further determination makes the process **posit its own presupposition**. Hegel articulates the whole through **three syllogisms**, whose still-separated operations disclose the demand for a single self-mediating principle. This is the threshold of **end** (purpose), not yet the accomplished unity of Concept and objectivity, and not the discovery of a conscious intention inside a reagent. A bounded acid–base comparison exhibits specifically relational determination while preventing two confusions: a medium need not be chemically inactive, and a stable equilibrium is not the cessation of reaction. The chapter follows the categorial argument without making its transitions a chronology of nature.*

## I. From Immanent Law to Specific Relation

The result of §30 was not an aggregate waiting for an observer to connect it. **Free mechanism** already had law, centrality, and self-movement. Its remaining inadequacy concerned the standing of the objects themselves: their independence was mediated by a relation whose specific differences had not yet become their own determinate natures.

Chemism begins when that separation can no longer be maintained. The object does not merely participate in a common system; **what it is specifically involves its relation to a specifically different other**. The centre's negative unity is no longer confined to an inward law over against externally existing objects. Its difference has become objective opposition (*SL* §§1576–1579).

This is not the introduction of a new connection from outside. The relation already shown to sustain the objects must enter their own determination. Neither is it a claim that every feature of a concrete body is exhausted by one relation. The logical question concerns the form in which its specific determinateness is understood, not an empirical inventory of all its properties.

Hegel accordingly calls chemism the **moment of judgment within objectivity** (*SL* §1578). The unity is differentiated into totalities whose specific standing relates them to one another. Judgment here does not mean a scientist's sentence about a reaction, nor does it mean that opposition or contradiction has only now appeared in the Logic. It names the objective differentiation of the unity reached in mechanism.

The primary sequence is *SL* §§1578–1592: **the chemical object**, **the chemical process**, and **the transition beyond chemism**. The shorter account in *Enc.* §§200–203 foregrounds neutralization and differentiation; the larger work develops three syllogisms and must not be reduced to those two operations alone. Source consultation for this installment is of the supplied Miller and Wallace text exports, not a fresh collation of printed editions.

## II. The Chemical Object — Why Affinity Rests on Self-Tension

At first the chemical object seems to have two incompatible descriptions. It is a **self-standing totality**, not a detached property. Yet its determinateness is essentially a **particularization** of a universal nature shared with its other. That universal is not an observer's class label: it is the principle specifying both opposed determinations and their relation (*SL* §1579).

The distinction is therefore between **the whole relation implicit in the object** and **the one-sided existence in which it immediately stands**. Its other is not just something else that happens to obstruct it. The relation to that other belongs to what this object itself is. To maintain its immediate one-sidedness as complete would be to exclude the totality its own determinateness involves.

This is why the tension is first a tension **within each object**. Hegel does not begin with two peaceful identities and subsequently add a conflict between them. Each has in itself the difference between its determinate existence and its universal nature. Its striving towards its other is at the same time a striving to overcome its own one-sidedness (*SL* §§1581–1582).

But that inner totality must not be confused with an already accomplished unity. The object can reflect into itself and appear an indifferent base, self-related apart from its outward connection. As long as that self-relation merely retracts the connection, however, it is abstract: the determinate relation still stands outside it, distributed between distinct objects. The object is not made adequate by ignoring the very difference constituting it (*SL* §1581).

**Affinity** names this specific reciprocal orientation. It is not a second force added to otherwise unrelated objects, and it is not an unexplained preference for company. Nor need we supply a human observer who first judges the isolation defective. On Hegel's account, the contradiction lies in the object's own determination, and its activity begins from that contradiction.

The positive achievement must be kept in view. Mechanism had shown self-subsistence through relation; chemism makes the **kind of relation** internal to the object's specific character. Yet the whole is still divided between immediately standing objects. A constitutive relation is not, merely by being constitutive, already a self-sustaining unity.

## III. Communication and the Neutral Product

The first process brings the objects' implicit unity into existence. Because each strives to overcome its own one-sided determination, its activity also bears upon the other's complementary one-sidedness. Their integration is not the victory of one unchanged extreme over the other. It produces a reality containing both moments in a new unity (*SL* §1582).

What mediates them? Hegel gives the middle two connected aspects. It is their **common nature**, the whole Concept containing both. But because the objects still confront one another as distinct existences, that unity also has an external form: an **element of communication** in which they can enter into relation. He calls this first middle **abstract neutrality** (*SL* §1583).

The adjective matters. This neutrality is not yet the completed product. It is the common element in which the opposed determinations can meet, while the real difference remains in the extremes. Hegel illustrates its function with water in material processes and with signs, more particularly language, wherever the spiritual analogue holds. Water's role as medium must be distinguished from chemical inactivity (§VII).

Communication here also differs from the formal mechanical communication of §30. It does not merely distribute a determination among objects whose particular natures remain indifferent to its reception. **The objects' specifically opposed determinations are transformed in their integration.** Their tension is weakened, and the striving reaches neutrality (*SL* §1584).

The result is **real neutrality**: not just a shared medium but the **product** in which the opposed ingredients no longer stand as independent chemical objects in the former tension. Hegel expressly retains their capacity for renewed self-subsistence and tension. Sublation does not mean that nothing of their difference remains; it means that the difference no longer has its previous independently opposed form (*SL* §1585).

Why is the result nevertheless only a **formal unity**? Because the process began with the difference as a presupposition. It has overcome that difference immediately, in the product, without yet making differentiation the product's own activity. The unity is real as a result, formal with respect to its self-mediation. Those descriptions answer different questions and do not cancel one another.

The first contradiction is thus resolved, but the resolution exposes a new limit. **The unity has been produced; the unity does not yet itself produce the differentiation through which it can be active.** §09 compresses this new limit into the process's stopping. What makes the stop a contradiction is not rest as such, but the separation between achieved unity and its principle of renewed activity.

## IV. Separation — More Than Undoing the Combination

The differentiating activity has not simply vanished from the total account. It was essential to the process and has acquired existence through it. But it now stands **outside the neutral product**. Hegel describes an active negative unity confronting the product, whose quiescent unity contains the possibility of renewed opposition (*SL* §1586).

The middle has therefore changed. In the first syllogism, the opposed objects met through a formally neutral element. In the second, **the concrete neutral product itself is the middle**: the differentiating activity acts upon the unity the first process achieved. The result of one movement becomes the mediation of the next.

It would be tempting to stop here and describe the second movement as simply recovering the original opposed objects. Hegel explicitly refuses that compression. Renewed chemical opposition belongs to the differentiating principle's immediate action upon the product; it is a determinateness of the middle itself, alongside its universal nature, not the other extreme of this syllogism (*SL* §1587).

The analysis goes further, into **abstract indifferent moments**: the base and its activating principle, with the latter also taking the form of indifferent objectivity when separated from the base. Their relation is no longer the specifically tensed opposition with which the first process began. Hegel calls the whole a **disjunctive syllogism**: one objective totality is displayed as active negative unity, as concrete neutral unity, and as resolved elementary moments (*SL* §§1587–1588).

These **elementary objects** are logical determinations of this analysis, not an identification of atoms, chemical elements in the periodic table, or a historically established decomposition technique. Hegel's argument concerns what remains of the object's determination when its concrete chemical relation is resolved into indifferent bases. It supplies no laboratory procedure for performing that resolution.

The achievement is consequential. What the initial chemical relation took for granted — an immediately given basis of its determinateness — has now itself become **a result of the process**. But the achieved elementary objects still carry their determinateness as an immediate character. Their abstract self-subsistence must therefore face the same question in a changed form: can a determinate nature be indifferent to the relation in which it is determinate?

## V. Positing the Presupposition

The third movement begins from that last discrepancy. The elementary object stands as simply self-related, yet its inner determinateness is an impulse to differentiate itself, establish tension, and enter a relation in which that determinateness can acquire reality. The process thus returns to the opposed objects and their neutralization, but no longer with precisely the same logical standing (*SL* §1589).

**What was presupposed at the beginning is now posited through the movement.** This is not the assertion that every material condition has been manufactured from nothing. It is the development of the determination that made the process possible: rather than receiving its difference as an unexplained given, the object differentiates itself so as to establish the relation its realization requires.

Hegel supplies a physical illustration at this point. He describes a body redistributing oxidation between parts of its mass, so that a part in the resulting lower degree can enter a combination for which its first determination was unsuitable (*SL* §1590). The important feature is not combination alone but **an alteration that prepares the condition of a further combination**.

The passage names no definite reagents, balanced reaction, or experimental conditions. It therefore cannot serve here as a verified chemical demonstration of the logical transition. Its function in Hegel's argument is nevertheless clear: the object is not merely carried into a relation according to an unchanged immediate character; its activity mediates the character through which that relation becomes possible.

This also shows why **self-mediation is not mere repetition**. A sequence can return to an earlier-looking state while leaving every transition dependent on conditions supplied elsewhere. The stronger claim is that the movement produces the differentiated terms and mediation through which it relates itself to itself. Repeating a cycle does not establish that claim; the work done by the return must be exhibited.

The three syllogisms can now be distinguished without turning them into three isolated chemical reactions (*SL* §1591):

| Movement | Mediation | Achievement and remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutralization** | The specifically opposed objects meet through **formal neutrality**. | Their unity becomes a real product, but active differentiation is not sustained within that product. |
| **Separation and resolution** | **Real neutrality**, the first product, mediates the differentiating activity and the indifferent elementary result. | The initially given basis is produced, but activity and basis still confront one another externally. |
| **Self-realization** | The Concept **posits the presupposition** conditioning its realization; the universal is the essence of the syllogism. | Differentiation and reunion are disclosed as moments of one mediation, while chemical objectivity still presents them as separately conditioned processes. |

The third row must be read with its qualification intact. Hegel does **not** say that chemism, simply as chemism, is already the fully explicit totality of self-determination. He says the opposite: this totality emerges from chemism, which is sublated in it. The operations still fall apart under the immediacy and externality belonging to chemical objectivity (*SL* §§1591–1592).

## VI. Why the Result Is End — and Not Yet the Idea

The remaining externality is more specific than an assertion that something somewhere comes from outside. Neutralization, renewed differentiation, and reduction depend upon conditions that their isolated forms do not themselves posit. Even where combining and separating occur in one physical process, Hegel states that the objects separated are different from those combined; their subsequent neutralization is then another process. Co-occurrence alone is not the required self-mediation (*SL* §1591).

The positive argument proceeds through the successive negations of this externality. First, the opposed objects lose their independent standing in the neutral unity. Second, that unity loses its standing as a merely quiescent result when it becomes the middle of differentiation. Finally, the indifferent bases lose their claim to be ultimate givens when their own determinateness initiates the relation. Each form is retained, but none remains an independently fixed starting point (*SL* §1592).

What, then, retains itself through the differences? Not one unchanged chemical ingredient. It is **the determining unity whose differentiation and reunion constitute the mediation**. Hegel's conclusion is that the Concept is no longer submerged in the objects' immediately given characters. It is explicit as the principle that determines the relation and maintains itself through that mediation. This is **end**.

The inferential step is not *chemistry needs an extra push, therefore a designer exists*. The criterion comes from the objects' own claim to totality: their universal nature cannot have its entire reality in one-sided existences whose integration leaves its activity outside the result. The development successively makes the apparently independent terms moments of that nature's mediation. **Our reconstruction** locates the warrant for the transition here, in the changing standing of the same determinations, not in an analogy with conscious planning.

The sequence meets this criterion on the premises developed here: each new mediation is required by the preceding result's own shortfall. The product must become the middle of differentiation, and the elementary bases must realize in relation the determinateness that their indifferent subsistence cannot express. This is the necessity Hegel invokes in recapitulating the stages (*SL* §1592). Their unity is developed through the inadequacies of its own forms, not selected after the event from co-occurring reactions. Yet within chemical objectivity the operations still fall apart; their determining unity becomes explicit by no longer remaining submerged in them. **What has been earned is the independent standing of the Concept as end, not already its adequate objective realization.**

A new difficulty appears immediately. The Concept, liberated from its submergence in objective immediacy, now stands **over against objectivity** as end. Its liberation is therefore initially one-sided: it confronts material and conditions for its realization. Hegel explicitly retains this externality at the opening of teleology (*SL* §1600; *Enc.* §§204–205).

There is no contradiction in saying that the previous form of external determination has been overcome while a new opposition remains. What has changed is the location of the determining principle. It is no longer merely the latent unity of externally related processes; it has an explicit standing of its own. But that standing must still overcome its opposition to the objectivity through which it is realized.

Consequently the result is **not yet Life or the Idea**, and it does not discard mechanism or chemism. Their processes become the means through which an end can realize itself. Whether and how this initially external relation develops into an immanent unity is the next installment's task.

## VII. A Bounded Chemical Comparison

The logical word **chemism** and the science **chemistry** do not name interchangeable domains. To compare them responsibly, we need an actual chemical determination rather than an assurance that substances somehow seek their opposites.

Take **Brønsted–Lowry acid–base reactions in water**. An acid donates a proton; a base accepts it. These are complementary roles in a proton-transfer relation, not labels attached to two otherwise unspecified substances. In the forward reaction of hydrogen fluoride with water, water accepts a proton; in the forward reaction of ammonia with water, water donates one:

*HF(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + F⁻(aq)*

*NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)*

The equations and roles belong to modern acid–base theory, not to Hegel's deduction; see [OpenStax, *Chemistry 2e*, §14.1](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-1-bronsted-lowry-acids-and-bases). They illustrate a precise gain of relational determination: **the same substance can occupy different roles in different specific relations**. They do not imply that its molecular constitution is created by our choice of description, that every partner is suitable, or that a logical category determines the equilibrium composition. The products themselves include an acid and a base; these equations illustrate relational roles, not an exhaustive loss of acid–base character in a neutral product.

This also restricts the water illustration in *SL* §1583. Water can be the medium and a participant in the reaction. Its role as medium does not entail chemical inactivity. To turn Hegel's **abstract neutrality** into a universal empirical assertion of an inert solvent would therefore be false; to read it as the middle's logical role leaves the concrete chemistry still to be specified. The distinction corrects that possible application without silently supplying Hegel with a modern theory.

The neutral product requires an equally important distinction. **Chemical equilibrium is dynamic.** In a reversible reaction at equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal; constant concentrations do not mean that both rates have become zero. This is the distinction explained in [*Chemistry 2e*, §13.1](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/13-1-chemical-equilibria). Even pure liquid water establishes the autoionization equilibrium described in *Chemistry 2e* §14.1:

*2H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)*

In the reverse direction, hydronium and hydroxide combine to form water: the equation also shows water as the product of their neutralization. But these molecular events are not, by that fact, the renewed macroscopic separation into independently existing tensed objects whose external conditioning Hegel describes. At equilibrium under unchanged conditions there is no net compositional change. This is compatible with a quiescent product, even though chemical reaction continues.

**The correction is to a reading of extinction as the absence of reaction**, not to Hegel's description of quiescent neutrality (*SL* §§1584–1586). Modern chemistry explains stable composition **through continuing opposed reactions**, not despite them. The account in *SL* §§1578–1592 does not supply this kinetic distinction; the comparison identifies both an agreement and a limit without silently modernizing his explanation.

But continuing reaction is not, by itself, the stronger self-mediation demanded in §VI. An equilibrium account can explain its composition and response under specified conditions without claiming that the system posits those conditions or determines its activity as an end. That limitation does not invalidate the explanation. Conversely, naming Hegel's further requirement does not establish that a given chemical account is incapable of answering it in a more developed form.

The comparison therefore has two distinct results. Relational acid–base roles make a feature of chemism intelligible; dynamic equilibrium distinguishes quiescence in composition from inactivity. Any stronger application must identify the concrete unity, its differentiation, and the work by which it sustains or produces its conditions, rather than merely find the word *self* in a description.

## VIII. Beyond Chemistry — Without Reducing Everything to It

Hegel expressly extends the schema beyond chemistry narrowly understood. He names meteorological relations, the sexual relation in living beings, and a formal basis of love and friendship (*SL* §1580). The point of citing these extensions is not that every detail of them has thereby been explained. It is that **chemism is a logical determination of relation**, not a second name for a particular department of natural science.

The restriction **formal basis** is important. A relation in which the participants' determinate standing involves one another need not be exhausted by that structure. Hegel's own extension therefore gives no warrant for reducing a friendship to neutralization or deriving its norms from chemical affinity. Likewise the language example in §III identifies a mediating function, not a complete account of language or its users.

For scientific method, our corresponding question is more exact than *is everything connected?* We must distinguish a relation that changes what happens to an object from one that enters the determination under investigation, and then ask what produces and sustains that relation. The acid–base example supplied a bounded instance of relational determination. The further question concerns what produces the process's conditions, not just its output.

This does not require every useful inquiry to explain all its own presuppositions before it can begin. A specified preparation and model can support a warranted result. The further investigation asks which presuppositions can themselves be explained as results of processes, and whether those processes belong to one determining unity or merely shift the unexplained condition elsewhere. That is a methodological task drawn from the chapter, not a guarantee that any particular research programme will complete it.

## IX. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**“Chemism is the first discovery that objects affect one another.”** No. Mechanism already developed objective mediation and immanent law. Chemism makes the specifically opposed relation belong to the objects' own determinate natures. Mere interaction does not establish that further determination.

**“A neutral product destroys difference, so the only possible next step is to add difference again from outside.”** No. The capacity for renewed differentiation is retained, and the subsequent process develops the basis first presupposed. External conditioning is the limit to be overcome, not Hegel's permanent solution. The third syllogism would disappear if chemism were reduced to combining and externally undoing the combination.

**“A reaction that continues or renews its conditions is already purpose, and therefore alive.”** No. Persistence alone does not establish the determining unity argued for in §VI. Nor is the first end already the Idea: it initially stands opposed to objectivity. The adequacy of an empirical example and the further logical development must each be shown.

## X. What the Method Did Here — Observations

**It made relation enter the determination of the relata.** The beginning was not an empirical addition to mechanism. It developed what mechanism's own mediated independence required: the relation could no longer be essential to the system yet indifferent to the objects' specific natures.

**It located the opposition within each side.** Each object was a totality existing one-sidedly. Its relation to the other was therefore also a relation to its own inadequacy. Affinity was not a foreign force inserted between complete identities.

**It changed the work of the middle.** The common element became a real product; that product became the middle of differentiation; the determining unity then became explicit through its own mediation. Repeating the word *unity* would have hidden these changes rather than explained them.

**It distinguished producing a result from producing its presupposition.** Neutralization achieved the first. The further process made the initially given basis a result and developed the activity through which a relation becomes possible. The return changed the logical standing of the beginning.

**It exposed a limit through an achievement.** The product really overcame the first tension. Its inadequacy was that this unity did not yet sustain the activity through which it was unity. The argument did not have to deny the result in order to go beyond it.

**It made preservation concrete.** The transition does not eliminate chemical processes or the warranted science of them. It changes their standing within a more comprehensive mediation. Our equilibrium comparison likewise retains chemistry's explanatory achievement while testing, rather than immunizing, the proposed philosophical application.

## Coda

Chemism begins with objects whose relation is not an accidental addition to what they are. Yet this gain does not complete the account. Their unity first exists as a product in which the activity that produced it is no longer its own explicit principle.

The further movement asks more of unity. It must not merely receive differentiated terms and leave a result behind; it must account for the differentiation and mediation through which it is real. **The decisive return is therefore not to the same product, but to the presupposition with its standing changed.**

That is the threshold of purpose. What was only implicit in the related objects emerges as the determining principle of their relation. Its new opposition to objectivity is not an excuse to stop: it is the precise difficulty with which the next development begins.

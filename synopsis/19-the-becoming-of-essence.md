# The Becoming of Essence: Absolute Indifference and the Passage into Essence

*The tenth installment of Section III and the close of the **Doctrine of Being**. The substrate disclosed by the measureless (§18) becomes **absolute indifference** (*absolute Indifferenz*): a mediated unity whose determinate states still appear external to it. Hegel develops its difference as the **inverse ratio of its factors**, distinguishing two sides, each containing both qualities, rather than simply adding two independent qualities together. The argument exposes the external origin and determination of differences within a supposedly self-contained whole; its further inference from qualitative inseparability to quantitative equilibrium requires separate scrutiny. The Remark on **centripetal and centrifugal force** makes both logical and physical claims, whose warrants must not be conflated. The intended result is **Essence** (*Wesen*): the unity and its determinations lose their independent immediacy and are grasped through their own **negative self-relation**. **Passing-over** (*Übergehen*) gives way to **shining-into** (*Scheinen*) and **reflection** (*Reflexion*), without abolishing immediacy or already accomplishing the development of the new sphere.*

---

## I. Where We Are: the Substrate Disclosed by the Measureless

The previous installment followed real measure through the nodal line into the **measureless** (*das Maßlose*). A new measure is measureless relative to the one exceeded, yet itself another determinate relation; their alternation with merely quantitative relations gives the progress to infinity. Its resolution did not follow from bare succession. Qualitative measures lost their isolated independence through quantitative alteration, while quantitative continuation lost its indifference through the emergence of qualitative relations. Hegel takes this mutual sublation as the specifying unity's continuation into itself — "the truly persisting, self-subsistent material substance or thing" (*SL* §780). The **substrate** thereby has "directly in its own self the determination of affirmative infinity" (*SL* §781); the formerly self-subsistent measures become its states.

That substrate is the result the present installment must develop. It is not one more measure alongside the others, but neither has all immediacy vanished. Its differentiation still depends on an external quantitative determination; its achieved mediation is not yet posited as its own self-differentiation (*SL* §§784, 788). The task is to show why this unity cannot remain indifferent to the states that constitute its difference. Only the further sublation of indifference will carry Being into **Essence**. To call the substrate already Essence would claim the coming chapter's result before its argument.

## II. Absolute Indifference — a Mediated Unity in Immediate Form

Hegel distinguishes **absolute indifference** from two earlier forms. Pure Being is indeterminate because no determinateness has yet been posited in it; pure quantity admits determinations that remain external to its character. Absolute indifference, by contrast, is the *result* of the negation of quality, quantity, and their immediate unity in measure. It is "a process of self-mediation resulting in a simple unity" (*SL* §785). Its simplicity is therefore not the emptiness of the beginning. What it lacks is an adequate form for the mediation it contains.

Its differences first count as **states** (*Zustand*): "Any determinateness it still possesses is only a state, i.e. something qualitative and external which has the indifference for a substrate" (*SL* §785). Yet a quality reduced to something external to the being it determines has lost its independent qualitative standing. Its vanishing is not an event outside the unity; it is the mediation through which that unity became indifference. Hence the supposedly external state is the unity's own differentiation: "it is precisely externality and its vanishing which make the unity of being into indifference and they are therefore within this indifference" (*SL* §786). The result is **concrete**, not a blank sameness produced by an observer's abstraction. The contradiction concerns its form: what has become through its differences still stands over against their determination as an indifferent substrate.

## III. The Inverse Ratio — Two Sides, Each Containing Both Qualities

The question is now how this determination is posited **within indifference itself** (*SL* §787). The reduced measure-relations continue into one another through a common substrate, which Hegel calls an indivisible, self-subsistent measure, wholly present in its differentiations. But its mediation is still only implicit; the result stands as a substrate, and its differences initially have the quantitative form appropriate to that indifference (*SL* §788).

Hegel first presents **two quanta of the same substrate**, bounded by a fixed sum: the greater one is, the smaller the other. As a notation for this initial quantitative aspect, we can write *a + b = W*. Hegel calls the terms "inversely proportional" (*SL* §789), but this is not the fixed-product relation *a · b = k* examined in §17. Nor is the absolute indifference simply another quantum comparable with other sums: the quantitative form belongs to its difference, while the unity claims to contain that difference within itself. Hegel expressly distinguishes the present relation from the earlier formal inverse ratio because "here the whole is a real substrate and each of the two sides is posited as having to be itself in principle this whole" (*SL* §789).

The **qualities** must now be distinguished from the **sides**. The difference has two qualitative determinations, each negating the other, yet inseparable in the unity they constitute. Accordingly, each side contains **both qualities**: one predominates on the first side, the other on the second. The sides differ by a more of one quality and a less of the other, not by assigning one exclusive quality to each side. Each side is therefore itself an inverse relation, and the same qualities continue through both, only in different magnitudes (*SL* §§790–791).

This is why the sides are more than two amounts taken from a stock. Each contains the qualitative whole; the indifference remains immanent in each. Yet their quantitative difference also gives them the appearance of self-subsistence relative to one another. The unity is implicit, while its determinate realization stands apart from it: the moments have not yet been determined as sublating themselves into unity through themselves and one another (*SL* §792). The fixed-sum notation expresses one moment of this construction; it is not a complete model of it.

## IV. The Contradiction — and the Warrant of Its Quantitative Form

Hegel identifies three connected difficulties (*SL* §793). **First**, the determinations simply emerge in a unity which is supposed to contain them all: their emergence is not exhibited as its own self-determination. **Second**, their quantitative fluctuation is likewise determined from outside; indifference supplies neither the difference nor the principle of its variation. **Third**, the sides and qualities have a standing inconsistent with being merely independent shares. Each side contains the whole qualitative unity; each quality continues through both sides and is not confined by the quantitative boundary between them. The whole is present in its determinations, yet their particular determination is left outside its mediation.

The third difficulty leads to the chapter's most demanding inference. If the qualities were independently subsisting things, their supposed unity would fall apart. But they have been determined as inseparable, each having meaning and reality only in its relation to the other. Hegel concludes that "each reaches only as far as the other" and therefore that "they are in equilibrium" (*SL* §794). Their increase or decrease would have to proceed together in the same proportion, rather than through the unequal predominance required by the inverse relation.

On this reasoning, an excess of one quality over the other would either lack a basis in their relation or cease to be the asserted excess. If they are equal, however, the inequality by which they were supposed to be distinguished disappears. Hegel also considers the quantitative representation of one overpowering the other: the diminished factor loses its support, leaving not the required pair but the one whole (*SL* §795). His conclusion is not that a sum cannot be divided; it is that this unity, determined as indifferent to its own determining process, contradicts itself and must be grasped as an **immanently negative unity** (*SL* §796).

**Our reservation concerns the inference to equilibrium.** Qualitative dependence does not by itself determine equal magnitudes. The strongest reading of Hegel's step takes the quantum to express nothing beyond the common extent of the qualitative relation: on that premise an independently subsisting excess has already been excluded. But the premise needs its own warrant; inseparability alone does not establish that this is the only possible quantitative determination of the moments. As in §17, the consequences of a restriction must be distinguished from the argument requiring it. We therefore do not treat §§794–795 as a general proof that qualitatively opposed moments cannot be quantified unequally.

The first two difficulties retain a definite force. A unity presented as the whole of its determinations cannot explain their emergence by leaving their source outside itself. A fixed relation can constrain variation without supplying the principle of the particular variation. Neither point makes such a relation mathematically false or scientifically useless. The failure is relative to **the stronger claim of self-mediation** made for absolute indifference. What remains to be shown is the positive determination that overcomes this externality, not merely the inadequacy of a diagram of two factors.

## V. The Remark — Mechanics and the Historical Comparison

Hegel applies the inverse relation to **centripetal and centrifugal force**, as invoked to explain elliptical motion. At first, he notes, this gives only two opposed qualities, not the full construction of two sides each containing both. He accepts the observed variation in orbital speed — increasing toward perihelion, decreasing toward aphelion — and its quantitative law: the fact "rests on a firm empirical foundation" (*SL* §797). He goes further: reducing the fact to its law has accomplished "all that can properly be required of a theory" (*SL* §798). His target is the proposed explanation by two factors whose strengths alternately increase and decrease against one another.

The objection is not exclusively categorial. Hegel argues that once the inward-directed factor gains the upper hand, the weakening outward-directed factor could not restore the separation; the body would continue toward the centre. Conversely, the weaker inward factor could not regain dominance at aphelion. An "alien force" would be needed to reverse their relative strengths (*SL* §§798–799). He then reconstructs each half of the orbit as a side containing both factors, and repeats the dilemma: either they are independently given and externally combined, or their qualitative inseparability forbids the independent excess assigned to one. The argument depends on the inference examined above, but it also makes claims about **what can explain actual motion**. Those claims cannot be insulated from mechanics by calling the example logical.

**A scientific check must use the actual dynamical relation.** For a particle of mass *m* in the fixed Newtonian gravitational field of a central mass *M*, conservation of angular momentum *L* yields the radial equation *m · d²r/dt² = L²/(m · r³) − G · M · m/r²*, with *r* the distance from the centre and *G* the gravitational constant. The outward term expresses angular motion in the reduced radial equation; it is not a second physical interaction acting outward in the inertial frame. As *r* decreases, both terms' magnitudes increase, but at different rates. They are not shares of a fixed sum. For bound, non-radial motion the equations permit an elliptical orbit, with closest and furthest approach, without an added force that switches dominance by fiat (MIT's *Classical Mechanics*, [chapter 25, §§25.3–25.4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf)).

This does not derive mechanics from the *Logic*, nor does a mechanical calculation prove the logical transition to Essence. It establishes a more limited point against the physical application: quantitative dynamics can explain the alternation without the independent, constant-sum factors Hegel attacks. The explanation uses a force law, conservation, and initial conditions; it does not merely rename the observed changes. Whether it fulfils Hegel's further demand for conceptual self-determination is another question. That further demand does not cancel the explanation's achievement, and the defect of the assumed factor-model is not a refutation of Newtonian orbital mechanics.

Hegel extends the same criticism to explanations of density by attraction and repulsion, and of organic differences by sensibility and irritability. He reports the physiological formalism as discredited while charging physical astronomy with continuing to use it (*SL* §800). These are his assessments of the applications, not further proofs that every quantitative treatment of qualitatively distinct factors must fail. The categorial task and the concrete science remain connected precisely because the proposed connection has to be established.

The Remark ends with **Spinoza**, not with a proof that every philosophy of identity is simply the same system. Hegel compares absolute indifference with substance insofar as determinations of being are posited as vanished; he then distinguishes the explicit problem of determination raised by indifference from what he diagnoses as the empirical introduction of attributes and modes in Spinoza. His objection is that difference is not grasped as the unity's own differentiation, so that substance is not determined as subject (*SL* §§801–802). This is Hegel's local comparison and criticism. Its function here is to sharpen the question of **whose determining the difference is**, not to establish a complete assessment of Spinoza by attaching a categorical label.

## VI. The Passage into Essence — the Unity Must Negate Its Own Indifference

"Absolute indifference is the final determination of being before it becomes essence; but it does not attain to essence" (*SL* §803). Merely collecting the differences into an underlying unity would leave the decisive movement to the external reflection of the thinker. Hegel requires the differences themselves to sublate their independent standing; the unity must prove indifferent not only to otherness but to **its own indifference**.

The remaining contradiction is between what the unity is **implicitly** and how it is **posited**. It claims to contain the whole mediation through which quality, quantity, and measure have lost their independence, yet presents itself as an immediate substrate to which differences happen. Conversely, the differences are supposed to have their whole standing in that unity, yet to receive their determination from outside it. The denial of independent difference thus leaves an independent *form of determining* untouched. Hegel's further step negates this separation: determining is no longer the emergence of an alien content in an unchanged bearer, but the unity's own negative relation to itself (*SL* §804).

Our reconstruction of this step emphasizes what must change on **both sides**. If the unity really contains the mediation constituting its differences, it cannot stand as a complete, indifferent being before that mediation; its self-identity is the result of relating to itself through difference. If the differences have no independent standing outside the unity, their determination must be grasped through their relation to it and to one another, not as a second set of beings alongside it. This identifies the intended positive result — **self-related negativity** — without making the disputed equilibrium inference an unquestionable proof of the passage. The reconstruction is conditional on Hegel's claim to a self-contained totality; it is not a requirement that every finite scientific model derive its own external conditions.

Hegel names the changed standing of the determinations **positedness**: each is related to its unity, to its other, and to negation (*SL* §805). The unity, too, has no untouched immediacy outside this relation. Its apparently original self-subsistence is a result, and the presupposed immediate whole is itself only a moment of the movement (*SL* §806). Neither a hidden carrier nor an empty negation remains. "Being, in its determining, has thus determined itself to essence, a being which, through the sublating of being, is a simple being-with-itself" (*SL* §806). This is the result Hegel claims; exhibiting its determinate content and identifying the limits of its argument are both required.

## VII. From Passing-Over to Reflection — a New Sphere, Not a Finished One

"The truth of being is essence" (*SL* §807). **Essence** (*Wesen*) is not something newly discovered behind Being, but Being's return into itself through its own negation. Hegel connects the word with *gewesen*, being that has been: "essence is past — but timelessly past — being" (*SL* §807). This is a logical past, not the chronological extinction of the world. Nor is inwardization merely an operation performed by a knowing subject upon an otherwise unchanged object: Hegel expressly claims it as Being's own movement (*SL* §§808–809).

The distinction from external abstraction remains decisive. Stripping determinations away would only leave them elsewhere and produce an empty essence confronting the content removed from it. Essence as Hegel intends it is instead the negativity through which Being has sublated its own independent otherness (*SL* §§810–812). **Immediacy is retained as mediated**, not forbidden. The result does not mean that mediation or positedness appeared nowhere earlier: being-for-self already held a mediated self-relation in the form of simple being (§14). What is now challenged is the immediate standing of the **whole sphere**, including the substrate supposed to persist beneath its changes.

The mode of movement accordingly changes. In **passing-over** (*Übergehen*), a determination loses its independent standing in another which retains it as a moment; it is not erased without remainder (§06). In Essence, determining remains within the unity: the determinations are not independent others externally related, but subsist through their association with one another. "The negativity of essence is reflection; and the determinations are reflected, posited by essence itself and remaining in essence as sublated" (*SL* §814). This is **shining-into** (*Scheinen*) and **reflection** (*Reflexion*), not the motion of a permanent thing behind changing appearances.

But the result is **at first indeterminate Essence**. Its determinations have still to be explicitly developed within the new sphere (*SL* §§813–815). The change in their general standing does not already give identity, difference, ground, or causality their articulated forms. **Ground** (*Grund*) will be a further determination, not a substitute name for Essence from the outset. Hegel's announcement that Essence first reflects within itself, then appears, then manifests itself sets the programme; it does not discharge it (*SL* §§816–817). Even the initial appearance of Essence as an immediate opposite of Being has to be worked through, which is where §20 begins.

## VIII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is the two-worlds misreading.** Essence would relapse into determinate being if it were simply another, superior being confronting an inferior one. Its differences are to be its own **shine**, not independent things screened off by a hidden bearer. Hegel states this as the task of the opening chapter (*SL* §§817–818); the successive forms of the essential, the unessential, and shine still have to be examined.

**The second is the final-indifference misreading.** The substrate is not the conclusion in which differences are swallowed and the investigation stops. Absolute indifference is itself the last inadequate form of Being. Its own difference requires the sublation of its merely implicit unity; the result is negative self-relation, not a deeper layer of featureless permanence (*SL* §§803–806).

**The third is the deduction-of-science misreading.** The logical demand for immanent determination neither supplies an orbital law nor rules out unequal quantities merely because their meanings are relational. Conversely, a successful orbit calculation does not prove Hegel's passage to Essence. The physical application and the quantitative mediation must each answer to their own premises; neither can serve as an automatic verdict on the whole argument.

## IX. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **the form of a result can contradict its content**. Absolute indifference contains a mediation achieved through measure, yet holds itself apart from the determination of its own differences. The advance does not add a more profound substrate. It changes the standing of both unity and difference: their self-subsistence has to be understood through their relation, rather than left behind that relation as its unexplained support.

The second observation is that **the positive result must be distinguished from the success of every proposed proof of it**. Hegel's account of external determination poses a genuine problem for a unity claiming to mediate itself. His inference to quantitative equilibrium carries a further burden. To exhibit the proposed successor is not to settle that burden, just as rejecting the inference would not erase the determinate problem that led to it. The disputes over the inverse ratio in §17 and its new form here are connected, not interchangeable.

The third observation is that **a change in the mode of mediation preserves the preceding achievement**. Quantity, measure, and their scientific uses are not discarded when reflection becomes explicit. Nor does the *Logic* exchange one arbitrary method for another: passing-over and shining-into name different forms of the same immanent investigation (§06). The new sphere must show how its determinations relate, rather than simply pronounce the earlier sphere deficient. Its own incompleteness is part of the result.

## X. What Comes Next

The **Doctrine of Essence** opens with a task, not with a completed inventory (*SL* §§816–817):

- **Reflection within itself** — the essential and the unessential, **shine**, and the forms of **reflection** (§20); then **identity**, **difference**, **diversity**, **opposition**, and **contradiction** (§21), and **ground** with its conditions (§22).
- **Appearance** (*Erscheinung*) — existence, the thing and its properties, appearance and law, and the essential relations of whole and parts, force and its expression, inner and outer.
- **Actuality** (*Wirklichkeit*) — the unity of Essence and its appearing, developed through the absolute, modality, and the relations of substance, causality, and reciprocity, toward the **Concept** (*Begriff*).

This outline must not turn reflection into the Concept's already accomplished freedom. Essence has emerged from Being and must still posit its determinations within itself; Hegel distinguishes that posited determinate being from the Concept's being in and for itself (*SL* §815). The next installment begins with the difficulty nearest at hand: why Essence cannot remain an immediate essential being over against an unessential one.

## Coda

The measureless left a substrate persisting through the loss of particular measures; the present chapter asks how its differences belong to it. A unity cannot explain its own determining by keeping that determining external, nor can a change of vocabulary make a disputed quantitative inference necessary. Hegel's intended advance is nevertheless determinate: the unity loses its standing as an untouched bearer, and its differences lose their standing as independent states whose connection is elsewhere. Immediacy returns as a moment of their mediation. *Being does not leave a silent thing behind its vanished forms. It gives way to the question made explicit in Essence: how the unity is itself through its differences, and the differences through the unity to which they belong.*

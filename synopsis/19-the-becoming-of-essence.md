# The Becoming of Essence: Absolute Indifference and the Passage into Essence

*The tenth installment of Section III and the close of the **Doctrine of Being**. The measureless (§18) disclosed a **substrate** (*Substrat*) that persists through the endless succession of measures; taken as indifferent to every determinate measure that arises and passes upon it, this substrate is **absolute indifference** (*absolute Indifferenz*) — the first figure of a ground that *underlies* its determinations rather than passing over into them. But indifference cannot rest: the very differences it was to be indifferent toward reassert themselves as its own moments, and indifference is determined as the **inverse ratio of its factors** — the §17 inverse ratio returning, now between two qualitative moments whose sum is the constant whole. This is the categorial site of Hegel's critique of a physics that would dissolve a qualitative opposition into the quantitative trade-off of centripetal and centrifugal force — with the **categorial-not-empirical** guardrail held firm. The determination collapses: the factors cannot be held as merely quantitative shares within a whole that is supposed to be their indifferent unity, and the contradiction sublates the entire sphere — Being shows that its determinations were never immediate but **posited**, having being only as **sublated**. This is the **becoming of Essence** (*Wesen*): being that has gone into itself, that **is** only as the negation of its own immediacy — being that *has been* (*gewesen*). With it the mode of the dialectic changes: **passing-over** (*Übergehen*), proper to Being, gives way to **shining-into** (*Scheinen*) and **reflection** (*Reflexion*), proper to Essence — the connections that operated covertly throughout Being now becoming the explicit theme. Being has spoken its last word; the language of the ground begins.*

---

## I. Where We Are: the Substrate Disclosed by the Measureless

The previous installment brought Measure to its own dissolution. Each determinate measure proved to be a station and not a terminus: at its nodes it gave way to another measure, and that one to another, in an **endless succession** — the **measureless** (*das Maßlose*), the last return of the spurious infinite within the Doctrine of Being (*SL* §780). And we saw that this succession is not chaos but a *determinate passage* from each measure to the next, and that the bare fact of passage points beyond the measures to something that **persists** through them: a **substrate** that takes on this measure and then that, surrendering one and acquiring the next while itself enduring — "the truly persisting, self-subsistent material substance or thing" (*SL* §780), which "has directly in its own self the determination of affirmative infinity" (*SL* §781).

That substrate is the result the present installment must develop. It is no longer anything *immediate*: it is not one more measure, not a qualitative quantum found alongside the others, but the **enduring ground** of all of them — the affirmative whole that holds the endless succession of measures as its own inner moment. The task now is to take this ground exactly as the measureless delivered it and let it determine itself. In doing so the Doctrine of Being will reach its end, for the ground here disclosed is the first form of a category that is no longer *being* at all, but the **truth** of being: **Essence** — for "the truth of *being* is *essence*" (*SL* §807).

## II. Absolute Indifference — the Substrate as Indifferent to Its Measures

Take the substrate as the measureless itself first presents it: as that which *remains the same* through all the changes of measure. Measures arise upon it and pass away; it abides. Considered in this way — as the persisting unity to which every determinate measure is something that merely comes and goes — the substrate is **indifferent** to all of them. It is not *this* measure rather than *that*; it is the one continuing being beneath the whole nodal line, to which each measure is an external **state** (*Zustand*) it passes through without being any of them essentially: "Any determinateness it still possesses is only a *state,* i.e. something qualitative and external which has the indifference for a *substrate*" (*SL* §785).

This is **absolute indifference** (*absolute Indifferenz*): being as the wholly indeterminate unity that underlies all determinate measures and is exhausted by none — the indifference that is such "through the *negation* of every determinateness of being, i.e., of quality, quantity, and their at first immediate unity, measure," and is thereby "a process of *self-mediation* resulting in a simple unity" (*SL* §785). It is the first, still abstract figure of an **absolute** — the substrate raised to a totality, the one ground of which all determinacies are mere modifications. A philosophy of identity has reached for exactly this figure: the **Absolute as the point of indifference**, the featureless identity in which all distinctions are levelled and from which they are supposed to issue. Hegel's own Remark here, however, names not Schelling but **Spinoza**: absolute indifference "may seem to be the fundamental determination of Spinoza's *substance*," and "this is indeed the case in so far as in both every determination of being … is posited as vanished" (*SL* §801) — the fault lying exactly where the whole sequel will press it, that "substance is not determined as self-differentiating, not as subject" (*SL* §802). Here the category is reached not by assertion but by the dialectic of measure itself. And precisely because it is reached that way, it cannot be the resting place it presents itself as. For an indifference won by abstracting *from* the differences still has those differences as the very thing it is indifferent toward; they have not vanished, only been declared external. The next step is to watch them return.

## III. Indifference Cannot Rest — the Return of the Difference

The indifference was supposed to be the **whole** — the entire content of the nodal line, gathered into one enduring unity. But a whole that contains its determinations only as a flat sameness, indifferent to which of them obtains, has not thereby been rid of them; it has them *within itself* as its own moments. The substrate is indifferent to *its* measures — and that little word betrays it. The differences are not foreign intrusions upon a unity that could be without them; they are the unity's own articulation, the moments whose succession the substrate *is*. Indifference toward them is therefore indifference toward what it itself, taken concretely, consists in. Hegel's own formulation: "it is precisely externality and its vanishing which make the unity of being into indifference and they are therefore *within* this indifference, which therewith ceases to be only a substrate and in its own self only abstract" (*SL* §786).

So the difference reasserts itself, no longer as an external state befalling an indifferent ground, but as a determination *of* the indifference — something the indifferent unity has *in itself* and must hold within itself if it is to be the whole at all. The two qualitative moments that every real measure (§18) bound together — the two sides whose unity a measure was — re-emerge here as the two **factors** of the one indifferent whole. Indifference is thereby **determined**: it is no longer the empty sameness it pretended to be, but a unity that contains its difference as a relation of two moments. The task is therefore "to see how this determination of indifference is posited within the indifference itself" (*SL* §787). And the only form in which it can at first hold that relation — holding the difference while still claiming to be indifferent to it — is *quantitative*: "at first it is essentially the merely quantitative external difference which is present in it" (*SL* §789), though what is so differentiated is "*two qualities,* one of which is sublated by the other" (*SL* §790).

## IV. Indifference as the Inverse Ratio of Its Factors

If the indifferent unity is to remain the same whole while its two moments differ within it, the moments must vary in such a way that the whole is always restored. The one can be greater only insofar as the other is less; their sum must be the constant total that *is* the indifferent unity. The two moments are made to stand, that is, in an **inverse ratio of factors**: each is present in the whole as a magnitude, and the increase of either is the exact decrement of the other, so that *a + b = W* holds fast through every redistribution: "Bounded however by the fixed limit of their sum, they are related to each other not externally but negatively, and this now is the qualitative determination of their relationship. They are consequently *inversely proportional*" (*SL* §789). The **inverse ratio** of §17 returns in name — and Hegel himself marks the difference: "This relation is distinguished from the earlier formal inverted relation or inverse ratio by the fact that here the whole is a real substrate and each of the two sides is posited as having to be itself in principle this whole" (*SL* §789). Where there it bound two indifferent quanta beneath a fixed *product* (whose asymptote made it the third spurious infinite), here the constant is a *sum* apportioned between two *qualitative* moments beneath a fixed qualitative whole; the factors stay bounded, and no asymptote — no fresh bad infinite — recurs.

Hegel finds this form in the science of his day, and it is here that his sharpest categorial polemic falls. The relationship of a whole "which is supposed to have its determinateness in the quantitative difference of two factors determined qualitatively against each other," he writes, "is applied to the elliptical motion of the celestial bodies" (*SL* §797): a representational mechanics **represents** orbital motion through two opposed forces — **centripetal** and **centrifugal** — taken to stand in inverse proportion, the one counting for more as the other counts for less, their balance supposed to hold the body in its path. And the same mould is stamped well beyond astronomy: **attraction** and **repulsion** are made the two factors explaining the differing **densities** of bodies, and **sensibility** and **irritability** the two factors from whose inequality the *Naturphilosophie* of the day proposed to derive health, disease and "the variety of living species" — a formalism that collapsed in physiology into "the confused hotchpotch of nonsense in which it became entangled," yet "is practised without restraint in the other sciences, particularly in physical astronomy" (*SL* §800). Hegel's objection is not astronomical or physiological but **categorial**: a *qualitative* opposition — the directedness toward the centre and the directedness away from it — has been cast in the **quantitative** form of an inverse ratio, as though two genuinely distinct determinations could be treated as two shares of a single magnitude that merely trade places. This "is contradicted by the essentially qualitative relation between their respective determinatenesses which makes their separation from each other completely out of the question. Each has meaning only with reference to the other" (*SL* §798). The factors are supposed to be qualitatively different, each contributing its *own* determination; yet the inverse-ratio form treats them as homogeneous quantities summing to a constant — which is to deny the very difference it was invoked to express.

The guardrail of §17 and §18 must be repeated here in its strongest form, because the temptation runs the other way this time. The Logic is **not** refuting Newton, and nothing in this critique stands or falls with the later fate of "centrifugal force" — or of any such force-decomposition — in mechanics. Hegel draws that line himself, and sharply. The inverse relation of velocities in an elliptical orbit "rests on a firm empirical foundation" (*SL* §797); its quantitative side "has been accurately ascertained by the untiring diligence of observation, and further, it has been reduced to its simple law and formula. Hence **all that can properly be required of a theory has been accomplished**; but to reflective understanding this did not appear sufficient" (*SL* §§797–798). The fact and the law stand; what is put on trial is the *explanatory apparatus* superadded to them — the pair of forces assumed "for the express purpose of explaining this difference," from which, Hegel shows, the alternation "*cannot be ascertained* or, as it is said, *explained*" at all (*SL* §799). The claim is categorial: *the inverse ratio of magnitudes is an inadequate logical form for a unity of qualitative moments that are each meant to be the whole.* Whether or not any given physics ever cast a real opposition in that form, the **form itself** is what the dialectic here puts on trial — and finds wanting. The science supplies an illustration; the inadequacy is the category's own.

## V. The Collapse of Indifference and the Self-Sublation of Being

The inverse-ratio determination cannot hold, and its breakdown is the hinge of the whole transition. The two factors were required to be at once *qualitatively distinct* — each a moment in its own right, contributing what the other cannot — and *merely quantitative shares* of a constant sum, differing only in how much of the one whole each commands. These demands are incompatible. As mere magnitudes summing to a total, the factors have no qualitative distinctness at all; the relation collapses into a single quantum indifferently divided, and the difference vanishes. But pressed the other way — as genuinely distinct qualities — each factor is not a *part* of the whole but the **whole itself** under one of its determinations ("each side is in its own self the totality of the indifference," *SL* §793), and then the two do not sum to the indifferent unity but each *is* it, so that the inverse ratio has nothing to measure. Hegel's own demonstration turns on precisely this: "because their quantitativity is simply and solely of this qualitative nature, each reaches only as far as the other," from which "it follows that they are in *equilibrium*" (*SL* §794) — and then the excess by which either might exceed the other "would only be a baseless determination, or else this *more* would only be the other itself again; but in this their equality, both would have vanished" (*SL* §795). Either way the form destroys itself: indifference cannot contain its difference as an inverse ratio of factors, because the difference is not quantitative and the unity is not a sum.

What is the upshot? That the indifferent unity, in trying to hold its difference *outside* itself as an external quantitative variation, is forced to take that difference *into* itself as its own **negativity**. The whole that was to be indifferent shows itself to be, on the contrary, the active **excluding** of its own moments — it *posits* the difference as belonging to it and at the same time *negates* it as a merely posited, non-self-subsistent difference. Indifference has become **self-related negativity**: a unity that is what it is only by negating its own determinations and holding them as sublated. It "is a contradiction in every respect" and must therefore be "*posited as* sublating this its contradictory nature," having "for its result and truth not the unity which is merely indifferent, but that immanently negative and absolute unity which is called essence" (*SL* §796) — "posited as what it in fact is, namely a simple and infinite, negative relation-to-self, its inherent incompatibility with itself, a repelling of itself from itself" (*SL* §804). And with that, the immediacy that defined the entire sphere is gone. Quality, quantity, measure — every determination of Being had counted as something *immediately given*, a determinacy that simply *is* what it is. Here, for the first time, **immediacy itself** is revealed as **posited**: "The being of the determinations is no longer simply *affirmative* as in the entire sphere of being, but is now a sheer *positedness*" (*SL* §805) — being shown to have its truth only in and through its own negation, only as **sublated** (*aufgehoben*). Being sublates itself; and in sublating itself it does not pass into yet another mode of being, but into the **negation of immediacy as such**.

## VI. The Becoming of Essence — Übergehen Gives Way to Scheinen

This negation of immediacy is **Essence** (*Wesen*). "Essence is *sublated being*" (*SL* §819) — *aufgehobenes Sein* — being that has gone into itself, that no longer *is* immediately but *is* only as the inwardizing negation of its own givenness; the sphere's own last sentence puts it exactly: "Being, in its determining, has thus determined itself to essence, a being which, through the sublating of being, is a simple *being-with-itself*" (*SL* §806). The whole Doctrine of Being is now seen to have been the self-sublating of immediacy: each of its determinations, taken as immediate, drove itself beyond itself, until being as a whole proved to have its truth not in any immediacy but in the **ground** into which it has withdrawn. "The truth of *being* is *essence*" (*SL* §807) — not a second thing discovered behind being, but being's own demonstrated result: that what *is*, examined, shows itself to be only as **posited**, as reflected out of immediacy into a self-maintaining ground.

Language itself, Hegel notes, has preserved the point: "The German language has preserved essence in the past participle *gewesen* of the verb *to be*; for essence is past — but timelessly past — being" (*SL* §807). Essence is being that **has been**: not abolished, but *past as immediate*, sublated and retained, immediacy gone-into-itself. Nor is this an operation performed on being from outside: "Not until knowing *inwardises, recollects* itself out of immediate being, does it through this mediation find essence" (*SL* §807). The result is timelessly past being — being recollected inward as its own ground.

And with this the very **mode of the dialectic changes** — the single most important architectural fact of the transition. Throughout the Doctrine of Being the movement was **passing-over** (*Übergehen*): one determination *became* another and was *lost* in it — being into nothing, the finite into the infinite, the quantum into its beyond, each measure into the next. The determinations were mutually external, and the truth of each lay *outside* it, in the other it turned into. In Essence this is no longer so. Because essence is the ground that has taken its determinations *into* itself as sublated, its moments do not pass away into an outside; they **shine** — *Scheinen* — *within* essence itself: essence's "determining remains within this unity and is neither a becoming nor a transition, nor are the determinations themselves an *other* as other, or relations *to other*" (*SL* §813). The movement is no longer transition into another but **reflection** (*Reflexion*) — "The negativity of essence is *reflection*; and the determinations are *reflected,* posited by essence itself and remaining in essence as sublated" (*SL* §814) — essence's staying-with-itself in its own distinctions, the differences appearing as essence's own semblance rather than as independent beings it becomes. Mediation no longer means perishing into the other; it means the inward shining of the ground in its determinations: "At first, essence *shines* or *shows within itself,* or is reflection; secondly, it *appears*; thirdly, it *manifests* itself" (*SL* §816). The connections that operated *covertly* all through the Doctrine of Being — substrate, ground, the persisting-through-change — now step forward as the **explicit theme**.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is the two-worlds misreading**: that "essence behind appearance" erects a second, hidden world — a realm of true being lying *behind* the world of measures, accessible only by leaving the latter behind. This is the picture of essence as a *Ding-an-sich*, a beyond. But the transition just made forbids it. Essence is not reached by stepping behind being to a different thing; it is being's *own* result, being driven inward. The ground does not lie behind its determinations as another being; it **shines in them** — the illusory being of essence "is not something external to or other than essence; on the contrary, it is essence's own illusory being. The showing of this illusory being within essence itself is *reflection*" (*SL* §818). The whole subsequent Doctrine of Essence will turn on this: that the supersensible is not a second world but *appearance as appearance*, essence showing itself in what it grounds. It is exactly the failure to see this that Hegel charges against "the various forms of idealism, Leibnizian, Kantian, Fichtean," which "have not advanced beyond being as determinateness, have not advanced beyond this immediacy" (*SL* §825). To make essence a hidden beyond is to relapse into exactly the immediacy the transition sublated.

**The second is the night-of-indifference misreading**: that the absolute reached here — the indifferent substrate — is the final word, the featureless identity in which all determinations are extinguished. This is the **Absolute as the point of indifference** — the featureless identity Hegel elsewhere mocked as "the night in which all cows are black" (*Phenomenology of Spirit*, Preface). The entire movement of §§III–V is the refutation of that picture *from within*, and Hegel states the verdict flatly: "Absolute indifference is the final determination of *being* before it becomes essence; **but it does not attain to essence**" (*SL* §803). Indifference **cannot remain indifferent**: "in the development of its positedness, this determination has shown itself to be from every aspect a contradiction" (*SL* §804) — its difference is its own moment and reasserts itself, and the attempt to hold that difference quantitatively destroys the indifference and converts it into self-related **negativity**. The truth of the absolute is therefore, at this turning, not indifference but **reflection** — not a static identity beneath all difference, but a ground that posits, distinguishes, and shines. The featureless absolute is precisely the figure the dialectic here leaves behind.

**The third is the imposed-transition misreading**: that the passage from Being to Essence is a leap of system-building — a new "Book" begun by fiat, or a mystical deepening read into a Logic that had merely run out of quantitative tricks. It is the opposite of imposed — and Hegel raises the objection himself before answering it. Pictured "as the path of knowing," he grants, "this beginning with being, and the development that sublates it, reaching essence as a mediated result, appears to be an activity of knowing external to being, and irrelevant to being's own nature" (*SL* §808). The answer is one sentence: "But this path is the movement of being itself. It was seen that being inwardises itself through its own nature, and through this movement into itself becomes essence" (*SL* §809). No external category was fetched in; Essence was nowhere appealed to until being had, *of itself*, shown its determinations to be posited. The measureless undid the fixity of measure; the substrate it disclosed could not hold its own difference as an immediacy; and in that failure being sublated *itself*. The transition is the most rigorously **immanent** in the whole sphere — and the deepest, because it does not advance one category but takes an entire Doctrine back into its ground.

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **an entire Doctrine, not merely a category, has been sublated.** At every earlier node the dialectic carried one determination into its successor *within* a sphere — quality into quantity, quantum into measure. Here the result is of a different order: Measure, brought to the measureless and then to absolute indifference, dissolves the **whole Doctrine of Being** into its ground — "being in general and the being or immediacy of the distinct determinatenesses, no less than the *implicit being,* has vanished" (*SL* §806). The reader who watched single categories pass over now watches a *sphere* pass over — and sees that the same figure that drove each local transition (the immediate undoing itself and disclosing what it presupposed) governs the architecture at its largest scale.

The second observation is that **the mode of mediation has itself been transformed.** This is the rarest kind of result: not a new content but a new *kind of movement*. Being's logic was the logic of **passing-over**, of determinations external to one another, each true only in the other it perishes into. Essence's logic will be the logic of **reflection**, of **shining-into**, of a ground at home with itself in its distinctions — for "the determining then is of a different nature from the determining in the sphere of being, and the determinations of essence have a different character from the determinatenesses of being" (*SL* §813); "Essence stands between *being* and *Notion*; it constitutes their mean, and its movement is the *transition* from being into the Notion" (*SL* §815). (And beyond it, the Concept's logic will be **development** — the free self-particularizing of a universal that remains itself in all its determinations.) The method has not only generated a new category; it has, by exhausting one of its own modes, *changed gears*.

The third observation is that **the spurious infinite has at last been closed for good within the Doctrine of Being.** Four times within the sphere the bad infinite appeared — at the finite (§13), at the quantum and its series (§16), at the inverse ratio (§17), at the measureless (§18) — and four times it was sublated into an affirmative whole. Its final appearance, the endless succession of measures (*SL* §780), is not merely capped by one more affirmative whole *within* being; it is taken back into the **ground** that the whole of being turns out to be (*SL* §803). The figure that opened the deep work of the sphere closes it — and, in closing it, carries us out of it.

## IX. What Comes Next

With Being sublated into its ground, the **Doctrine of Essence** opens — the second of the *Logic*'s three books, and the one in which the covert connections of Being become the explicit theme. Hegel announces its course in advance, and the three divisions follow the deepening of the shine itself (*SL* §§816–817):

- **The determinations of reflection** — **identity**, **difference**, **diversity**, **opposition**, and **contradiction** — no longer immediate determinacies but the self-relating distinctions of a ground that shines within itself; and **ground** itself (*Grund*) as the first explicit form of essential mediation, the relation of **ground and grounded**.
- **Appearance** (*Erscheinung*) — **existence**, the **thing** and its properties, **appearance and law**, the **essential relation** of whole and parts, force and its expression, inner and outer — essence as it *shows* itself, the supersensible as appearance qua appearance.
- **Actuality** (*Wirklichkeit*) — the **absolute** and its **exposition**; the **modal** categories of **possibility**, **contingency**, and **necessity**; and then **substance**, **causality**, and **reciprocity**, the categories of necessary connection — where Essence presses to *its* limit and passes, in turn, into the **Concept** (*Begriff*), the third and final book, in which the truth that was *substrate* in Being and *ground* in Essence becomes free **subject**.

The whole that the Doctrine of Being could only point toward — the affirmative, self-relating totality that holds its determinations as its own — is what the rest of the *Logic* will unfold. Being gave us the immediate; Essence will give us the mediated and reflected; the Concept will give us the immediacy *and* the mediation as one self-determining whole.

---

## Coda

We began at the bedrock of pure Being, the least and emptiest of immediacies, and we have watched that immediacy, pressed at every turn, refuse to be the final word. Quality perished at its limit; quantity grew indifferent to what it determined; measure bound the two and then broke itself open at its nodes, beyond which lay the measureless and, beneath the measureless, a substrate that abides through all change. And now even that substrate has surrendered its immediacy: asked to be indifferent to its own difference, it could not, and in failing it turned being inside-out — from what merely *is* to what *has been*, gathered inward as its own ground. *The truth of Being was never any immediate being. The immediate, followed without flinching to the end, sublates itself into the ground it had all along presupposed; and Being, having spoken its last word in the language of what is, falls silent before the language of what lies within — where nothing any longer merely passes away, but everything shines.*

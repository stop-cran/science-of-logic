# The Quantitative Ratio and the Threshold of Measure

*The eighth installment of Section III — the third and final moment of Quantity, and Hegel's passage into **Measure** (*Maß*). **Quantitative ratio** (*das quantitative Verhältnis*) makes explicit the quantum's determination through its other; its **exponent** (*Exponent*) determines the relation rather than naming only a modern power-index. In the **direct ratio** (*direktes Verhältnis*) it is a fixed quotient; in the **inverse ratio** (*umgekehrtes Verhältnis*), a fixed product whose factors vary reciprocally. Hegel interprets the latter through the spurious and affirmative infinite; his finite-maximum argument requires a part–whole restriction whose warrant for the product's factors must itself be examined. The **ratio of powers** (*Potenzenverhältnis*) then presents unit and amount as determined through the same quantum. Its necessity must be tested through the surviving argument for mediated unity, not dismissed with the disputed maximum or certified merely by its intelligible form. The **double transition** from quality to quantity and back supplies Hegel's argument for their unity in Measure. The installment reconstructs that argument without certifying its disputed mediation, distinguishes self-determination of a relation from fixation of its numerical values, and retains Hegel's own warning against turning powers into a universal philosophical symbolism.*

---

## I. Where We Are: the Quantum's Determination in Its Other

The previous installment followed quantum through number, degree, and the quantitative infinite. An indifferent limit sent it beyond itself; the beyond proved to be quantitative in turn. The result was not an unlimited magnitude but a unity in which the related quanta were moments: Hegel names this **quantitative ratio** (*SL* §537). The new chapter begins there. The infinite quantum, uniting quantitative and qualitative determination, “**is in the first instance a ratio**” (*SL* §669). The mathematical Remarks examined in §16 came after the logical derivation; they do not replace the development of ratio which now follows.

The decisive determination is not merely that one magnitude can be compared with another. In the ratio, what each is *as a member of this relation* is constituted through the other. Its beyond is no longer a place in which its determination is endlessly sought and never found: “**what each is, it is in the other**” (*SL* §670). Hegel accordingly treats the ratio itself as a single quantum containing its difference within itself, a “**self-enclosed totality**” (*SL* §671). The two magnitudes have not disappeared; their independence from the relation has been negated.

The chapter develops three forms of this unity — direct ratio, inverse ratio, and ratio of powers — before passing into Measure (*SL* §672). Its problem is how a determination which belongs to quantity's externality can become the quantum's own qualitative determination. Hegel intends an immanent development, not an inventory of useful equations. Our task is to exhibit the steps and test their warrant, not to infer their necessity from their assigned place in the system.

## II. Unit, Amount, and the Exponent

The governing term is **exponent** (*Exponent*). Here it means the determinant of the ratio, not simply the raised numeral in *x²*. In direct ratio the exponent is a quotient; in inverse ratio it is a product; in the ratio of powers Hegel calls it a qualitative determination rather than an immediate quantum (*SL* §§674–689). Confusing these uses with the modern power-index would conceal precisely the change the chapter seeks to establish.

The distinction between **unit** and **amount**, which constituted number in §16, returns in the ratio. Each moment now appears as a quantum on its own (*SL* §675). Consider *a/b = c*: *b* can serve as unit and *c* gives the amount of such units in *a*. When *b* is numerically one, *a* equals the exponent; when *b* changes, *a* changes with it while the amount *c* remains the same. Thus 2 : 3 and 4 : 6 have the same quotient, 2/3, although their terms are different numbers. Two does not cease to be two; its particular magnitude is not what constitutes the ratio's identity.

This is already a qualitative moment: changing the quotient changes *which relation* holds, whereas changing both terms proportionally does not. Yet an invariant is not, solely by being invariant, an immanently derived determination. The value *c* is still taken as given. Hegel's question is how the exponent can be the unity of the moments it determines, rather than an immediate quantum whose determining role is simply assigned to it.

## III. The Direct Ratio

In the **direct ratio** (*direktes Verhältnis*), the determinateness of either side lies reciprocally in the other, and their common determination is itself a quantum, the exponent (*SL* §674). For positive magnitudes, *a/b = c* permits both sides to increase or decrease by the same factor. Fixing *c* determines either side once the other is supplied; it does not determine an individual pair without that further datum.

That freedom is not by itself Hegel's argument against the direct ratio. It remains present in the later forms too: a fixed product allows many pairs of factors, and a fixed power-relation allows many values of its variable. The more specific difficulty concerns **unit and amount**. As members of the direct ratio, the sides have only these partial roles; they are “**not complete quanta**” in the determination of their Concept (*SL* §677). This does not deny that they are numbers. It means that each, in this relation, counts as only one of the moments whose unity constitutes quantum.

The exponent is supposed to gather both moments, but the quotient can itself count as either. In Hegel's example, *B/A = C*: take *A* as unit, and *C* is the amount of such units in *B*; take *A* as amount, and *C* is the unit required to yield *B*. The quotient therefore does not yet express the demanded unity as a unity of both moments; it occupies one or the other role. Hegel concludes that the sides must be posited with their negation as incomplete quanta, so that the exponent has “**the significance of the product of the sides**” (*SL* §678).

This is his passage to inverse ratio. The product expresses unit and amount together through its factors, instead of leaving the exponent in either partial role. The passage is not an algebraic consequence of *a/b = c*: preserving that quotient while varying its terms does not preserve their product. It changes which determination is held fixed. Hegel's justification is the demand that the unity be explicitly realized in its moments; whether the succeeding form fulfils that demand must be shown in its own development.

## IV. The Inverse Ratio — Its Achievement and Its Difficulty

In the **inverse ratio** (*umgekehrtes Verhältnis*), *a · b = k*, the exponent *k* counts as a product, the unity of unit and amount (*SL* §§679–682). Take positive magnitudes and a fixed positive *k*. Increasing one factor by a given factor requires decreasing the other by the same factor. Unlike the direct ratio, the quotient between the sides now varies. The invariant is maintained through their opposed alteration, not through proportional enlargement of both.

This is a genuine determination. Neither factor's value is independent of the other, and the product constrains their variation without fixing either one separately. Hegel describes each as continuing itself *negatively* into the other: “**one of them becomes as many times smaller as the other becomes greater**” (*SL* §683). The negative relation is reciprocal dependence, not subtraction of equal amounts from a common stock.

Hegel then makes a stronger claim. He treats each side as taking a part of their common whole and as a moment only insofar as it limits the other and is simultaneously limited by it. On that basis, each has its maximum in the exponent; in becoming equal to that whole it would lose its character as a moment, while the other magnitude becomes zero (*SL* §684). The exponent is consequently an unattainable beyond to which the sides approximate — the **spurious infinite** — and at the same time a present affirmative unity holding their variation within itself (*SL* §685). His conclusion is that the whole, as the limit of their reciprocal limiting, is “**negation of the negation**” and hence an affirmative relation to itself (*SL* §687). The form recalls §§13–16, but the particular inference needs scrutiny.

**The fixed product alone does not establish this part–whole restriction.** If *a · b = 6*, then *a = 6* gives *b = 1*, not zero; *a = 12* and *b = 1/2* remain a pair of factors. As *b* tends to zero through positive values, *a* grows without bound rather than approaching six. A constant sum, *a + b = 6*, would make one term vanish when the other reached six, but that is a different relation. These calculations test the product without the further restriction; they do not yet answer Hegel's criterion for what counts as a moment.

The Russian edition's explanatory note 125, attached to this passage, recognizes the arithmetic distinction and proposes that zero be understood figuratively: the side ceases to count as a side of the relation (Stolpner, vol. I, p. 494). That preserves the distinction between a magnitude's numerical value and its logical role. A stronger reading can connect this to Hegel's own limiting criterion: a factor of one no longer diminishes the other relative to the whole, and so no longer counts as its negative limiter. If both factors of six must exceed one, each is strictly less than six, and *a* approaches six as *b* approaches one from above. The approximation now has a definite mathematical sense; allowing factors outside that domain does not refute it.

The remaining question is why this restrictive sense of *limiting* belongs to the product's factors. A factor below one still determines the other reciprocally; what it fails to do is keep that other below the product. Hegel's part–whole premise supplies that demand, but the passage does not establish why it is required by the multiplicative unity of unit and amount. Taking parts from a stock is apt for a sum; applying that model to factors needs its own justification. The dispute therefore concerns the warrant for the domain restriction, not a refusal to acknowledge its consequences or a claim that Hegel argued from the bare equation alone.

The intended positive result contains more than the contested approximation. In *SL* §687 Hegel argues that each factor, having its value in the other, is implicitly the whole exponent, while no particular factor-value supplies a fixed immanent limit. He takes the negation of those particular values to negate the exponent's own self-externality: the exponent preserves and unites itself in their alteration and therefore determines its own otherness. This is his inference, not merely a demand for a successor. The next paragraph reads that self-identity in otherness as being-for-self; unit and amount are its moments, and amount is now determined by the unit alone (*SL* §688). The infinite beyond still figures in the argument, but reciprocal dependence and the absence of a uniquely fixed factor-value do not require a finite maximum. Those determinations give its claim to mediated unity a content that survives the objection to the approximation.

**Our judgment is bounded:** the objection to the maximum does not refute this surviving argument for mediated unity. Hegel names the result **ratio of powers** at the end of §687. The distinct question concerns his inference from negating the factors' particular values to negating the exponent's self-externality. Does the former also remove the immediacy of the third quantum that governs their variation — an immediacy which Hegel still ascribes to the inverse ratio's exponent in *SL* §689? The product is maintained through the factors' reciprocal determination, but that maintenance can be described without making either factor determine the amount by itself. To say that each is implicitly the whole is not yet to identify its role with that of the unit which supplies its own amount. Whether Hegel's mediation warrants that further identification remains open here. Neither the disputed maximum nor the intelligibility of the proposed successor settles it.

## V. The Ratio of Powers — What Is Determined Through Itself?

In the **ratio of powers** (*Potenzenverhältnis*), Hegel makes the unit itself determine the amount (*SL* §688). The elementary case is the square: *y = x²*. In three threes, the same number serves as unit and amount; in four fours, both change together because their equality belongs to the form. This is not a third fixed magnitude allotted between two factors. The quantum *x* itself supplies both factors of its square.

The square in §16 provides an arithmetic comparison, not the missing logical derivation. Hegel expressly distinguished his ordering of arithmetic operations from an immanent development of the Concept. Here the point is the determination realized in the operation: the other quantum is produced through the original quantum's relation to itself. In that sense Hegel says that, insofar as the alteration is a raising to a power, its otherness “**is limited purely by itself**” (*SL* §688).

Its **exponent** is accordingly “**no longer an immediate quantum**”; Hegel calls it “**of a wholly qualitative nature**” and specifies the content: “**the amount is the unit itself**” (*SL* §689). Neither a fixed quotient nor a fixed product captures this determination. As positive *x* varies, *x²/x* and *x · x²* vary too. What is maintained is the power-relation. The word *qualitative* therefore has a determinate task here: it names the form of self-relation, not a mysterious property of a particular number.

But *x* remains variable. The square does not select three rather than four, nor does the bare existence of the operation show that any given object must be governed by it. The raised “2” specifies the square in our notation; calling the relation self-determining does not derive that index or every other possible power-law from the preceding fixed product. Hegel's claim concerns quantum's form of determination, not complete determination of every numerical value or unrestricted self-production of a concrete thing.

He anticipates the objection that raising to a power is merely an operation externally performed on any quantum. His answer is that the operation expresses what quantum already is: a limit which continues into its otherness and remains itself there. The power-relation is, for him, “**the display of what quantum is in itself**” (*SL* §690), the explicit realization of quantity's own determination rather than an alien procedure imposed upon it. This answers why the form is relevant to the concept of quantum. Whether the preceding inference from the factors' alterability to the exponent's determination of its own otherness establishes the necessity of this form remains the distinct question raised above; rejecting the finite-maximum argument does not answer it.

The chapter's closing Remark prevents a further overreach. Powers belong to the determinate sphere of quantum; numbering the Concept's moments as first, second, and third powers does not explain their philosophical content (*SL* §§695–697). Hegel concedes a symbolic use only to renew his objection to symbolism: philosophy needs no such help, and determinations adequate to a subordinate sphere are untrue when imposed on higher relations. If a philosophical meaning is claimed, that meaning must first be demonstrated; once it is, the numerical designation adds no justification (*SL* §698). A self-related quantum is not already the developed Concept, and a successful mathematical form is not a universal key to every higher sphere.

## VI. The Double Transition and the Threshold of Measure

Hegel's conclusion is that quantum, fully exhibiting its own determination, has passed into another determination: **quality** (*SL* §692). The point is not that a number has become a colour or that quantity has vanished. Externality, which characterized quantity, is now taken as mediated through the quantum itself, as a moment of its self-relation. The qualitative return retains the quantitative moment it transforms.

The relation to the earlier movement matters. Quality had passed into quantity: determinateness ceased to coincide immediately with what the Something was and became indifferent magnitude. But that result, left by itself, was one-sided; quality was contained in quantity without quantity being explicitly grasped as a moment of quality. The return is required to establish the unity in both directions. Hegel calls this the **double transition** and attaches to it importance for the whole scientific method (*SL* §693). It is not a licence to announce a unity after two arbitrary changes of category. Each direction must develop the determinations whose unity is claimed.

Hegel names the result **Measure** (*Maß*): quantum no longer merely external or indifferent, but that by virtue of which something is what it is (*SL* §694). His introductory account of Measure repeats the structure: externality related to itself is also sublated externality, containing quantitative and qualitative moments (*SL* §§699–700). This is the positive content of the transition. Our reconstruction preserves it alongside the distinct questions about the inverse ratio's domain restriction and the passage from mediated unity to the unit's determination of amount. The statement of the result is not retroactive proof of either step.

Nor is the result already the developed sphere of Measure. Its unity is initially immediate; Hegel says that it is Essence only *in itself*, not yet as a posited result (*SL* §707). A finite interval of permissible variation, a node where quality changes, and the sustaining unity of different measures must still be developed. None follows just by substituting numbers into *y = x²*. To call the present result the *threshold* of Measure is to distinguish the announced unity from that further work, not to treat all its consequences as already secured.

The comparison with **physical quantization** deferred in §§15–16 remains open for the same reason. Discrete energy levels, radiation frequencies associated with transitions between them, and Planck's action constant are distinct determinations. A comparison with Measure must identify the physical system, its relevant quality and quantity, and the relation between their changes. Neither the common word *quantum* nor the refusal to deduce physical constants establishes the application. §18 develops Measure and examines the demands on such comparisons; §19 addresses the further passage into Essence.

## VII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

**The first is that “qualitative” merely dignifies an ordinary numerical invariant.** In the direct ratio a given quotient distinguishes one proportion from another; in the inverse ratio a product fixes reciprocal variation; in the power-relation the determination is no longer either of those fixed quanta. The word must be explained through these different roles, not used to erase their difference. Conversely, finding an invariant does not prove that its form or value has been immanently derived. That is precisely why the movement between the forms needs argument.

**The second is that Measure means nothing more than measurement.** Comparing a magnitude with a chosen unit can remain an external operation; Hegel explicitly distinguishes conventional standards from an immanent measure (*SL* §714). The category concerns how quantitative determination belongs to what a thing is, not simply how an observer assigns it a number. But this distinction supplies no universal proof that every act of measurement already exhibits a threshold of qualitative transformation. Establishing a particular connection between magnitude and quality remains a task of the object investigated.

**The third is that Hegel's transition is either an empirical generalization or beyond criticism because it is logical.** He does not infer Measure by collecting physical changes and abstracting their common pattern. He intends to derive it from quantity's own development. That intention commits the argument to showing its mediations; it cannot insulate a claim about products and their factors from the consequences of that very relation. Hegel likewise reserves the specific proof connecting a concrete object's qualitative and quantitative aspects to the science of that object (*SL* §709). Logical derivation, mathematical correctness, and concrete application are connected obligations, not substitutes for one another.

## VIII. What the Method Did Here — Observations

The first observation is that **the relation changes what counts as a complete determination.** A number taken separately can be fixed as *this much*; within a ratio it counts through its role in a unity. Hegel does not add a mysterious ingredient to arithmetic. He asks what becomes explicit when unit and amount, first internal moments of number, confront one another as related quanta. The direct ratio's partial roles and the inverse ratio's reciprocal dependence are definite achievements of that investigation.

The second observation is that **a recurrent dialectical figure does not certify its own recurrence.** The spurious infinite and affirmative self-relation established the questions in §§13–16. Hegel invokes them again in the inverse ratio, but the claimed numerical beyond must be established from this relation, not borrowed from the earlier movement. An asymptote is not inherently a failed ought, as §16 already stressed. The restriction suggested by Hegel's part–whole criterion can give the approximation a mathematical sense; why that restriction belongs to the relation remains the substantive question. The comparison is useful only if it preserves both the conditional achievement and its outstanding warrant.

The third observation is that **the demand for immanent development also measures the exposition itself.** In *SL* §687 Hegel presents the next determination as what has emerged through the relation's own moments. Our criticism applies that criterion to the supplied mediation: the domain restriction needs justification, while the inference from negated particular factor-values to the exponent's self-determination poses a separate question about the removal of the given third quantum. These are distinct burdens, not one arithmetic objection repeated twice. The double transition then gives a further methodological demand — both sides must show themselves as moments of the unity — rather than a formula that relieves us of proving either passage.

## IX. What Comes Next

Hegel's development has reached Measure; the next installments follow its own determinations rather than treating the disputed step as settled by what comes after it:

- **Specific quantity**, immediate measure, and the **specifying measure**: how a qualitative quantum is distinguished from an external standard, and how the quality specifies an imposed amount.
- The relation of qualitatively determined magnitudes, **real measure**, and a **system of measures**: mathematical laws and concrete scientific interpretations whose warrants must be distinguished.
- The **nodal line of measure-relations** (*Knotenlinie der Maßverhältnisse*) and the **measureless** (*das Maßlose*): qualitative transformations, the progress beyond each measure, and the unity that persists through it, with the proposed comparison to physical quantization still requiring its own mediation.
- The passage of **Being into Essence**: why the persisting substrate cannot remain merely indifferent, and how *passing-over* (*Übergehen*) gives way to *shining-into* (*Scheinen*). That crossing is §19's task, not an accomplished result of the ratio of powers.

## Coda

The movement from quantum to ratio has made determination through another explicit; the movement through the ratios asks whether that otherness can be determined through the quantum itself. The power-relation gives this demand a definite expression, and the double transition states what a unity of quality and quantity must contain. But the method loses its force if a desired result is allowed to stand in for its mediation. **To preserve an achievement is neither to repeat its verdict uncritically nor to discard it with its disputed inference.** The threshold of Measure is therefore also a test of the reading: to follow Hegel's passage, retain its positive content, and distinguish what has been established from what remains at issue in its mediation. *The how-much begins to disclose its connection with what the thing is; neither the elegance of the formula nor the promise of the next category can take the place of that connection's proof.*

# The Doctrine of Essence — Reflection, Appearance, Actuality

*The second sphere of the Logic, traversed at the level of its three "bigger" sub-spheres: from essence as pure self-reflection, through essence appearing in its other, to essence and appearance fully united in actuality — and through reciprocity's contradiction to the threshold of the Concept.*

---

## I. Orientation: The Sphere of Essence

Where the Doctrine of Being was the sphere of immediacy — its categories standing seemingly self-contained, vanishing into one another by *passing-over* — the Doctrine of Essence is the sphere of **mediation**. Here it has become explicit that no determination stands in its own right; each is what it is only through its relation to its other. The mode of transition is *shining-into* (*Scheinen*): each category does not pass over and disappear, but **shines** into its other and is reflected back. The two are no longer adjacent; they constitute each other reciprocally.

The German *Schein* is the master-word of this sphere. It means at once "shine," "show," "semblance," and "illusory being." A *Schein* is not a sham (though it can be); it is essence's own **shining** — a being that has no subsistence of its own but *is* only as sublated, essence reflecting within itself. The sun *scheint* — it shines — and at this stage what shines is not yet an outer that manifests an inner beyond it, but essence showing *within itself*; the play of *Schein* is the rhythm of every category in this sphere. (The richer structure of an inner that appears in an outer — *Erscheinung* — is a later determination; here we have only essence's immediate self-showing.)

The Doctrine of Essence divides into three sub-spheres:

- **Essence as Reflection in itself** — essence in the moment of pure inwardness, generating the determinations of reflection (identity, difference, contradiction) and resolving them in the category of *ground*.
- **Appearance** — essence stepping forth into existence, manifesting itself in the thing with its properties, in the world as appearance, and in the essential relations (whole/parts, force and its expression, inner and outer).
- **Actuality** — essence and appearance fully united; the Absolute and its exposition, modal articulation (possibility, contingency, necessity), and the absolute relation (substance, causality, reciprocity).

The arc is: from essence taken purely in itself, through essence appearing in its other, to the unity of the two as actuality — at whose terminus reciprocity opens onto the Concept through the self-sublation of its sides' substantial independence.

## II. Essence as Reflection in Itself

### The starting point

Essence begins from Being's own last result. Measure's indifferent unity was forced to take difference into itself as **self-related negativity**; immediacy is now grasped as *posited*, and Being sublates itself (§07; §19). Hegel calls essence "past — but timelessly past — being" (*SL* §807): being returned into itself through its own negation. What seemed self-subsistent in immediacy now has its standing within this movement; the task is to develop the determinations of that reflection (§20).

Essence initially seems to confront being as the essential confronting the unessential. But that opposition would leave both as immediate beings. Since Being has sublated itself, what remains is **shine** (*Schein*), without independent standing; its reflected immediacy and negativity are essence's own determinations. Shine is thus **essence's own shine**, and its movement within essence is **reflection** — essence distinguishing itself from itself (§20).

### The determinations of reflection: identity, difference, contradiction

Once essence is recognized as self-reflecting, its determinations must be followed through their reciprocal mediation. These are the **determinations of reflection** (*Reflexionsbestimmungen*) — the categories underlying the traditional "laws of thought," now to be examined in their own movement.

- **Identity.** Essence's self-relation is constituted through negation, not exhausted by the formula A = A. Hegel reads the formula's propositional movement as distinguishing and return, and faults its claim to be a sufficient expression of truth. That is not a demonstration that the identity statement is formally false. The categorial transition develops identity as a moment of the negative self-relation it initially names as a whole (§21).
- **Difference.** Difference unfolds as *absolute difference*, *diversity*, and *opposition*. The first is difference inherent in identity, not a separation first introduced by comparing independent things. In diversity, the sides appear indifferent to one another; in opposition, each is what it is by excluding its other and yet requiring that other for its own determination — as positive and negative (§21).
- **Contradiction.** For Hegel, contradiction is not simply a symptom of confused thinking; it is the form in which a determination shows that it cannot rest in itself. Each opposed side claims self-subsistence through its relation to the other, yet excludes that other. It thereby excludes what its own independence consists in and contradicts itself (§21).

The crucial Hegelian claim: **everything finite is internally contradictory**, and this is not a defect to be patched but the engine of movement and life. The contradictions of essence do not stop thought; they drive it. The next step is not to discard the opposed determinations, but to follow their own self-sublation into **ground**.

### Ground

The self-exclusion of developed opposition is also a negation of its own positedness. Each side excludes the other through whose inclusion it constitutes itself, and this exclusion therefore reaches its own self-subsistence. The positive result is not merely their disappearance: self-subsistence returns into itself through the negation of that positedness, preserving opposition as its determination. This is **Ground** (*Grund*), not a third thing inferred from their incompatibility (*SL* §§939–945; §21).

Ground develops the relation in which a determination is shown as **posited**, rather than accepted as an immediate fact. That demand is not yet a sufficient explanation. A *formal* ground presents the same content as ground and grounded; a *real* ground offers different determinations but can leave their connection presupposed; *complete ground* mediates that connection through another relation which it still presupposes (§22). Its completeness is not finished explanation. The progression tests what an explanation supplies instead of treating the word "ground" as its guarantee.

Through form and essence, form and matter, form and content, ground develops the unity it has reached. Its mediation nevertheless presupposes an immediate **condition**. Ground and condition are distinct, yet each is what it is through the other; their unity is the **fact itself** (*die Sache selbst*), the same complete content as reflected in ground and as immediately present in the totality of conditions. Ground and conditions therefore cease to stand over against the fact as independent premises. Their mediation sublates itself into **Existence** — an immediacy that contains its mediation as sublated (*SL* §§1029–1035; §22).

This is the move from essence-as-pure-reflection into appearance. What exists is not a product with its essence left somewhere behind it: it is essence's own mediated immediacy.

## III. Appearance

### Existence and the thing

The first form of essence's stepping-forth is *Existenz* — what concretely is, the existing thing. The thing has **properties**, the determinate ways in which it shows itself: hardness, color, weight, electrical charge. The thing seems first to be what underlies the properties (a substrate that *has* the properties), and the properties to be its accidents.

But the thing maintains itself through its determinate relations — its properties. As these become the essential continuity between things, the thing apart from them becomes an indifferent collecting-point. Properties taken as self-subsistent are **matters** (*Materien*), a form Hegel also examines in the caloric and electrical-matter theories of his period (*SL* §§1064–1069). Each matter is to subsist independently, yet interpenetrates the others within the thing's negative unity. It is this contradiction of independent subsistence and reciprocal negation, not the mere repeatability of a property across many things, that dissolves the thing's self-standing character (§23).

### Appearance and the law of appearance

The thing, having dissolved into properties and matters that are themselves not self-standing, is exposed as **appearance** (*Erscheinung*). What we encounter is not an immediate substrate but a manifestation; the world we live in is the world of appearance.

Behind the unstable surface of appearance, however, runs a **law** — a stable, recurring, lawful structure that the surface manifests. The "supersensible world" of laws (Newton's mechanics, Maxwell's equations, the conservation laws) appears to be the inner truth of the world of phenomena, the essence behind the appearance.

But Hegel resists the simple division of two worlds. The "law" is not a separate realm hidden behind the world we encounter; it is the **inner** of that world, present *in* its appearances. The supersensible behind the sensible is itself just one moment of appearance, taken in abstraction. There is one world, articulated into the inner stability of law and the outer flow of phenomena, with each *of* and *for* the other.

### Essential relation: whole and parts, force and expression, inner and outer

The relation of essence to appearance, of inner to outer, comes to explicit thematic treatment in the **essential relations**. Three of them, in ascending concretion:

- **Whole and parts.** The whole consists of its parts; the parts make up the whole. Neither is independent: a whole that has lost its parts is no whole; parts of nothing are not parts. They are **mutually constitutive** moments of one structure. Yet the relation remains external: the whole is taken as their sum, while the parts retain independent existence. It does not suffice for organic life, whose members are what they are through the living unity; this limits the relation without invalidating analysis (*Enc.* §135; §24).
- **Force and its expression.** Force is not a hidden inner *thing* over and above its expressions; its content must be exhibited in them. But expression is conditioned: a force requires solicitation, and the soliciting force is itself solicited. This mediation advances beyond the external relation of whole and parts without making force self-determining. Nor does merely naming a force explain the content and law of its action: a renamed phenomenon is a tautology. The sciences' discovery of laws supplies content that the bare name cannot (*Enc.* §136; §24).
- **Inner and outer.** The fully developed essential relation. Hegel's striking claim is that the **inner is exhausted by the outer**, and the outer is the inner manifested. There is no inner essence "left over" once we have grasped the entirety of how a thing acts and shows itself; conversely, every outer manifestation is the inner of the thing made visible. This claim is sometimes misread as a kind of behaviorism. It is not. It is the considered metaphysical view that essence is not a thing tucked away behind appearance, but the **principle of self-articulation** that *is* its appearance — taken not as bare phenomena but as the manifestation of an inner determining structure.

When this is fully grasped — when essence is no longer pictured as "behind" appearance but is recognized as the inner that is its own outer — we have crossed into Actuality.

## IV. Actuality

*Wirklichkeit* — actuality — is the unity of essence and existence. What was distinguished as inner and outer is now one content and its manifestation; essence does not remain behind what exists. In the greater *Logic*, this sphere first examines **the Absolute**, its attribute and mode: exposition must belong to the absolute itself, rather than merely be our account of an inaccessible beyond. Actuality then develops its modal forms and the absolute relation (§25; §26).

This is also where a famous sentence must be read carefully. Hegel restates the Preface to the *Philosophy of Right*: "**What is reasonable is actual and What is actual is reasonable**" (*Enc.* §6 Remark; Wallace renders *vernünftig* as "reasonable" where the familiar English has "rational"). He distinguishes actuality from mere existence: a contingent existence does not deserve the **emphatic** name of actuality. This prevents the sentence from sanctifying whatever happens to exist. Yet contingency is itself a determination of actuality in the *Logic*, and an actual thing can be **really necessary** under conditions it did not posit (§25). Nor does the distinction simply license an external standard of what ought to be: in the same Remark, Hegel rejects confronting the actual Idea with an abstract "ought." A judgment on particular circumstances may be right without thereby being philosophical science. The critical use we draw from the distinction therefore requires a further argument about the specific object and its principle; the distinction alone neither vindicates nor condemns a given institution.

### Modality: possibility, contingency, necessity

Actuality articulates itself through the modal categories. Hegel works through three levels:

- **Formal** modality — bare logical possibility (whatever is non-self-contradictory), formal necessity, formal contingency. The empty modality of the abstract Understanding.
- **Real** modality — possibility as the **totality of the conditions**, contingency as the actual whose ground lies elsewhere, and a necessity that is **relative**: necessary *given* a circle of conditions it did not itself posit, and therefore in itself also contingency (§25).
- **Absolute** modality — the necessity whose presupposition proves to be its own positing. Contingency is not eliminated: the apparently independent actualities express a unity that is still **blind**, not yet manifest in their relation. This is the threshold of substance (§25).

The advance is from bare formal possibility through necessity relative to given conditions to a necessity whose presupposing belongs to its own movement. The limit of this last form is not that it still awaits one more external condition, but that its unity has not yet become explicit in its apparently independent actualities (*SL* §§1225–1231).

### Substance, causality, reciprocity

Actuality, developed as **substance**, is the unity and power of its accidents, rather than a bearer left over when they are removed (§26). Spinoza's *Ethics* exhibits the one infinite substance — *Deus sive natura* — of which everything finite is a mode. Hegel takes this philosophy as a decisive achievement and a position to be overcome on its own ground (§25; §26).

Spinoza already understands God as an immanent cause, acting from the necessity of his own nature (*Ethics* I, propositions 16–18). Hegel's further objection is that the exposition does not derive the differentiation and return of attributes and modes through substance's own **self-negating form**; it takes up determinations and refers them back to substance (*SL* §§1179–1183; §25). The sequence through causality and reciprocity is Hegel's attempt to exhibit that development from substance itself (*SL* §§1287–1288), not merely to add causal activity that Spinoza already affirmed.

- **Causality.** Substance acts. But the relation is not, at bottom, one *between two* independently constituted actualities: it is the **self-differentiation of one power**, whose two sides have the same content, the cause being actual only in its effect (§26). Taken as a one-way relation running from one substance to another, causality runs off into the endless progress and does not yet grasp what it is itself doing.
- **Reciprocity** (*Wechselwirkung*). Pressed, the causal relation **bends round**: the acted-upon substance reacts against the first cause, whose causal standing is sublated together with the effect in which alone it was actual, and the progress that ran off to infinity returns into itself. What results is a mutual causality of **two** substances, each active and passive toward the other — not a network in which everything is cause and effect of everything else (§26).

Reciprocity is the truth of causality and the highest relation of Essence. Its achievement also exposes its limit: the sides' substantial independence is no longer separable from the single reflection constituting their relation.

### From substance to the Concept: the most subtle transition

Here lies one of the genuinely difficult moves of the entire *Logic*, and an honest IOU is in order: a fully careful treatment requires the close work that Section III will provide. At the level of this overview, the move is the following.

The move is *not* that each substance turns out to depend on all the others and a fuller category arrives alongside that network. Hegel locates a contradiction in necessity and causality: their identity of connection coexists with the substantial independence of the sides. Reciprocity develops the resolution by making each side's becoming the other its own reflection into itself. Their independent substantial standing is thus **sublated**, and necessity unveils itself as **self-determination** (*SL* §§1274–1276). §26 follows the further articulation into universal, singular, and particular rather than treating the name **Concept** as the proof of the transition.

The freedom of the Concept is born from **blind necessity** unveiled as **self-determination**. Necessity was blind because its unity did not show itself in the independence of its sides; now that independence is posited as their own identical mediation. Necessity does not disappear: its form is transformed, rather than merely becoming better known to an observer (§26).

This is the conceptual basis for Hegel's claim that he has gone beyond Spinoza: not by rejecting substance but by exhibiting it as the implicit form of the Concept. Substance is not abolished; it is *aufgehoben* — preserved as a moment of a richer category that grasps the same content as self-determination rather than as blind necessity.

## V. From Essence to the Concept: A First Look

The move from Essence to the Concept involves the third and final shift in the mode of transition. In Being, categories *passed over* into one another and disappeared. In Essence, categories *shone* into one another, each constituting its other reciprocally. In the Concept, categories will **develop themselves out of themselves** (*Entwicklung*) — each moment will be the explicit articulation of what was implicit in the preceding. The earlier determinations are retained as sublated moments, not carried forward with their former independence unchanged.

What this means concretely will be the work of the next installment. For now, the orienting thought: the entire sphere of Essence, with its inner grounds and outer appearances, its substances and their reciprocity, was **already** the implicit self-articulation of a self-determining whole. The Concept is not a new content brought in to crown the system; it is what was always at work in essence, now grasped explicitly as such.

## VI. The Doctrine of Essence in Scientific Practice

A brief observation, in the spirit of the synopsis: the categories of Essence name forms at work in inquiry alongside those of Being, not a separate residence for **theoretical science**. Empirical sciences already investigate laws and explanatory connections; the question is what forms their explanations employ and what those forms can establish (§07).

- **Ground / grounded** — asking whether a proposed reason does more than repeat the fact under a new name, and how the connection between different determinations is established. A chemical or genetic explanation must supply that connection through its own investigation; the category cannot substitute for it.
- **Inner / outer, force / expression** — connecting manifestations through a determinate law and specifying the conditions of their occurrence. Such inquiry supplies more than the assertion that an unknown force produces them; it need not treat a power not presently manifested as nonexistent.
- **Whole / parts, essential relation** — investigating composition and the relations of components. The achievement of analysis is retained, while the organization of an organism cannot be exhausted by treating its members as independently constituted parts (*Enc.* §135).
- **Substance / accident, causality** — distinguishing persistence from change and investigating causal dependence. Hegel's finite causal form requires identity of content between cause and effect; he rejects its application where a living or spiritual recipient transforms what acts on it (*SL* §1256). Whether a modern account uses that form or a different one must be established, not decided by its biological or social subject matter alone (§26).
- **Reciprocity** — our comparison with the elementary Lotka–Volterra predator–prey model has a limited warrant: each population's rate of change depends on the other, through specified feeding and mortality relations. The equations yield conditional conclusions about coexistence and population cycles; that is an explanatory achievement, not merely the name of a loop. It does not establish Hegel's deduction of reciprocity or its passage into the Concept. Hegel himself uses organic and political examples of reciprocity, granting a degree of correctness while warning that stopping there leaves the subject "utterly incomprehensible" (*Enc.* §156). His further demand concerns comprehension of the related sides as moments of a whole, not the denial that a model can establish determinate results (§26).

These comparisons do not assign whole sciences to logical spheres or derive their results from the *Logic*. They distinguish contributions within explanatory work: measured determinations, grounds and conditions, laws, and reciprocal dependences. The philosophical question is how such forms sustain and limit one another, while the warrant for a particular scientific account remains in its own evidence and argument.

## VII. Coda: Into the Concept

The second sphere of the *Logic* has been traversed. We began with essence as pure self-reflection (identity, difference, contradiction, ground); moved through essence stepping forth into appearance (existence, the world of phenomena and its laws, the essential relations of whole/parts, force/expression, inner/outer); arrived at actuality as the unity of essence and appearance; and followed its articulation through the Absolute, modality and the absolute relation. Reciprocity's contradiction sublates the sides' substantial independence: necessity unveils itself as freedom, and substance is retained as a moment of the Concept.

The next installment opens the **Doctrine of the Concept**, the third and final sphere of the *Logic*. We will follow the Concept through its three sub-spheres: the **Subjective Concept** (concept proper, judgment, syllogism), **Objectivity** (mechanism, chemism, teleology), and the **Idea** (life, cognition, the Absolute Idea) — and witness, at the closing of the sphere, the closing of the entire circle of the *Logic*.

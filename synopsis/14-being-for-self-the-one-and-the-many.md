# Being-for-self, the One, and the Many

*The fifth installment of Section III — a close reading of the true infinite held in the form of simple being: **being-for-self** (*Fürsichsein*), being that relates to itself through its ideal otherness; its moment of **being-for-one** (*Sein-für-Eines*), without a fixed bearer opposed to ideal contents; the collapse into the immediate, exclusive **One** (*das Eins*); the **One and the Void**, and Hegel's assessment of atomism and Kant's construction of matter; the dialectic of **Repulsion** and **Attraction**, in which the Many lose their claimed independence without being abstractly erased; and the self-sublation of the qualitative One that carries Quality over into **Quantity**, initially as continuity.*

---

## I. Where We Are: from the True Infinite to Being-for-self

The previous installment closed with the **true infinite** — not a *unity of finite and infinite* as two standing terms, but the *becoming*, the double negation in which each side, passing into the other, returns to itself through it, neither persisting as an independent term (§13; *SL* §301). We named its result but did not develop it: being that no longer points beyond itself to an Other that bounds it, but is *with itself*. That being is **being-for-self** (*Fürsichsein*), and unfolding it is the work of this installment.

It is worth being precise about what kind of step this is. Something, Limit, and finitude developed the dependence on an Other and showed that this otherness was the finite's own determination (§§11–13). The true infinite overcame the opposition of independently standing terms through their **self-return**, not through an unchanged oscillation. That result is not won a second time here. What is now to be unfolded is the **form** in which it is held: the mediated self-relation is taken in the form of **simple being** (*SL* §§318–321). Its mediation has not ceased to belong to it, but its immediate form does not yet exhibit the distinction and movement of its moments. This is the source of the apparent hardening to come: being-for-self becomes the exclusive One, and the moments of its ideality appear once more as independent beings. Their own relation must undo that independence. Through this development, rather than through the mere announcement of self-return, Quality completes itself and passes over into Quantity.

## II. Being-for-self and Ideality

Recall the determinations distinguished within Quality: **Reality** (*Realität*), Quality taken affirmatively as being, and **Negation**, non-being as belonging to determinate being (§11). That negative side did not first become inward here. The Limit had already become the Something's own immanent determination (§12), and finitude had made non-being the thing's own nature — "something with its immanent limit posited as the contradiction of itself, through which it is directed and forced out of and beyond itself, is the *finite*" (*SL* §247). What the true infinite achieved, and what being-for-self now holds in the form of simple being, is the negation of *that* negation: otherness is no longer an independent limit standing over against the affirmative, but a sublated moment of the being's own self-relation. This sublated status of the otherwise-external is what Hegel calls **ideality** (*Idealität*).

Being-for-self is therefore the category in which **ideality takes the form of simple being**. We met the *word* in the previous installment, in the proposition that the finite is *ideal*; but there it named the status the finite acquires *inside* the true infinite. Now we have the determination whose very content *is* that ideality: being that is affirmatively itself precisely because it has reduced its otherness to a moment. But Reality and Ideality are not therefore two opposed qualitative sides — a real bearer on one hand, its merely ideal contents on the other. Hegel raises that distinction only to withdraw it: the whole and its moments are alike ideal, and neither holds an independent reality over against the other. The distinction actually to be unfolded is that of **being-for-self** and **being-for-one**, which are "not different meanings of ideality, but are essential, inseparable moments of it" (*SL* §§321–324). This is the consummation of the qualitative: a being that is fully determinate and yet, in its determinacy, *related only to itself*.

The temptation, here as everywhere, is to picture this as a *thing* that has swallowed its environment. Resist it. Being-for-self is not yet a thing, not an object, not a self in any psychological sense. It is the bare logical form of *self-relation that has its otherness within as ideal*. Hegel illustrates it through consciousness and self-consciousness and discusses it in atomism and Leibniz's monads (*SL* §§320, 326, 337, 348). Those more concrete shapes are not thereby deduced from this minimal determination.

## III. Being-for-one — the Other Turned Inward

How does the internalized otherness show itself within being-for-self? As the moment Hegel names, with deliberate awkwardness, **being-for-one** (*Sein-für-Eines*).

The phrase is built to be heard against its predecessor. *Being-for-other* (§12) expressed the Something's relation to an Other. Here determinate being has not vanished: it remains as a moment, but its relation to an independent Other has been bent back into the infinite self-relation. As this ideal moment, it is **for one** (*SL* §321). There is not yet a fixed One that bears ideal contents. What is ideal, and that for which it is ideal, are moments of the same ideality; being-for-self is itself also being-for-one, and the one for which it is is its own self (*SL* §322). The relation does not join an independently real bearer to merely dependent contents.

Hegel illustrates the point through the German idiom *"Was für ein Ding ist das?"* — "what kind of thing is this?", word for word, "*what for a* thing is this?" This is a question about **quality**, not about the thing's purpose or use. Hegel hears in the expression a "for" that returns into the thing whose character is being asked about, rather than referring it to another bearer (*SL* §323). This is his speculative reading of the idiom, not a deduction from the English question about what something is *for*. The categorial argument must already carry the conclusion that the linguistic observation illustrates.

This is the transformed standing in which the Other survives (§13): not as an independent term over against being-for-self, but as the ideal moment of its self-relation. Neither that moment nor the self-relation has an independent reality outside the other (*SL* §324). The next move must therefore explain why this articulated ideality takes the form of an exclusive One; simply calling otherness ideal does not yet explain the hardening.

## IV. The One

Press being-for-self for the form it has here. Its moments are inseparable, and their unity is held in the **immediacy of being**. In this form their distinction has collapsed: the negative self-relation stands as a simple being that excludes otherness, the **One** (*das Eins*) (*SL* §§328–331). It is not ideality as such that abolishes internal articulation — Hegel's discussion of the monad expressly allows an ideal manifold (*SL* §326). The collapse belongs to this immediate form of qualitative being-for-self. What was the ideal unity of moments now stands as a fixed One, and its moments can consequently fall apart as apparently independent beings. Hegel names this reappearance of independence as the difficulty of the development that follows (*SL* §329).

The One is the qualitative determination driven to its extreme of simplicity. It has three features, and they will generate everything that follows. First, it is **simple self-relation** — within it there is no manifold, no parts, no internal difference; it is the very form of indivisible unity. Second, it is **indeterminate** — but not as pure Being was indeterminate (through sheer emptiness); the One's indeterminateness is the *result* of a negation, the determinateness of having excluded all otherness, so it is indeterminate *by way of* being utterly self-enclosed. Third, and decisively, it is **self-relating negation**: the One *is* what it is only by negating, by excluding, by being-not-other. And this third feature is unstable in a precise way — though not because negating has run out of an object and must go looking for one elsewhere. The One is at once the simple immediacy in which all difference has vanished and the self-related negation through which that immediacy is determined. Its very nothing is therefore a *posited* nothing. The next steps will distinguish that nothing *within* the One, and only then set it over against the One's affirmative being: first as the void, then as other Ones.

## V. The One and the Void — Atomism and Its Logical Truth

In the One's simple immediacy all mediation and manifoldness has vanished: there is *nothing* in it. But this nothing is itself **posited** — distinguished from the being-within-self whose abstraction it is. Posited *in the One*, it is the **Void** (*das Leere*), and as such it is first of all the One's own *quality*: "This nothing, then, posited as *in the one*, is the nothing as the *void*. The void is thus the *quality* of the one in its immediacy" (*SL* §334). Only then, "as distinct from the affirmative being of the one", is the void posited *outside* it (*SL* §335). The One and the Void are thus not a fullness first given and an empty room afterwards required; they are the moments of being-for-self fallen apart into determinate being.

The One and the Void: this is the logical skeleton Hegel finds in **atomism**. He regards the atomists' principle as more determinate than Parmenides' Being or Heraclitus' becoming, while criticizing its relapse into the external relation of composition (*SL* §337). His verdict on the early atomists is nevertheless not merely negative. He credits them with grasping the void as the **ground** of movement, not just the unoccupied space into which something can move. In that trivial reading the void would be "only the presupposition or condition of movement, not its *ground*"; the profounder thought is "that in the negative as such there lies the ground of becoming, of the unrest of self-movement" (*SL* §338). This is Hegel's speculative interpretation of their achievement, not an independently established attribution of his developed category of negativity to Leucippus or Democritus. Its force within the present argument is precise: the void cannot remain a second, indifferent find beside the atom if it is to be the ground of the atom's own movement. Hegel's criticism of atomistic externality must preserve this insight, while showing how the One's negative relation goes beyond the juxtaposition of full and empty.

Hegel himself carries the diagnosis into physics, and in the same breath into political theory: "Physics with its molecules and particles suffers from the atom, this principle of extreme externality, which is thus utterly devoid of the Notion, just as much as does that theory of the State which starts from the particular will of individuals" (*SL* §339). His target in both is an account that begins with independent units and adds their relation from outside; the analogy is his, not a refutation here of a particular physical or political theory on its own ground. His assessment of Kant is more differentiated. He esteems the attempt to derive matter from opposed forces rather than from impenetrability alone, but says that "his two fundamental forces within matter remain external to and completely independent of each other" and that "Kant's method is basically analytical, not constructive" (*SL* §§377–380).

Kant's own undertaking matters to the judgment. His *Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science* deliberately starts from the empirical concept of matter and investigates its a priori conditions; analysis is to supply the metaphysical elements needed for mathematical construction ([Preface](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Kant%27s_Prolegomena_and_Metaphysical_Foundations_of_Natural_Science/The_Metaphysical_Foundations_of_Natural_Science/Preface); [Dynamics, Proposition 8, Note 1](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Kant%27s_Prolegomena_and_Metaphysical_Foundations_of_Natural_Science/The_Metaphysical_Foundations_of_Natural_Science/Second_Division), Bax translation). Nor does he merely juxtapose unrelated forces. Repulsion alone would disperse matter without bound; attraction alone would contract it to a point. Both are therefore necessary to matter, although they remain distinct fundamental forces (Dynamics, Propositions 5–7). **Constitutive inseparability is not the same as derivation of each force from the other.** Hegel demands the further logical work in which each determination's mediation through the other proves to be self-mediation. That demand is not answered by Kant's conjunction; but the label *analytical*, or the fact that Kant starts from matter, does not by itself show failure at Kant's declared task. Hegel himself acknowledges the qualification about the elements of construction (*SL* §374). We retain the dynamical account's move beyond absolute impenetrability, without certifying Kant's mathematical construction or rejecting it merely for not being Hegel's logical derivation.

The next logical step does not require a verdict on a physical theory (§03). It requires the One's own negative relation to be unfolded: why does it not merely face an empty other, but posit *other Ones*?

## VI. Repulsion and Attraction

The One is self-relating negation, but now fixed as an **affirmatively present being**. Its negative relation to itself is consequently a relation to an affirmative being; as negative, it distinguishes that being as other, but as self-relation it gives the other the same determination as itself. The other is therefore not indeterminate non-being, the void, but likewise a **One** (*SL* §341). This positing of **many Ones** through the One's negative self-relation is **Repulsion** (*Repulsion*, *Abstoßung*). It is neither a physical object's production of copies nor ordinary becoming in which a Something turns into a qualitatively different Other: the One becomes only One (*SL* §342). Plurality is not assumed as a collection already present before the relation; it is what this immediate negative self-relation posits.

But each resulting One relates only to itself, not to another as the ground of its being. Its positedness thus disappears from its immediate character: the Ones appear **presupposed as not posited**, independently there before their relation (*SL* §§343–345). This explains the transition from repulsion as the positing of many Ones to **mutual repulsion** of Ones apparently already given. Their plurality remains external to each; each has the same qualitative determination, being One, and none acknowledges its being-for-other (*SL* §§346–347). These are not yet numbers or a determinate count. The contradiction is that their supposed independence is itself the result of the relation they exclude from it.

Their self-sameness is, at first, a comparison *we* make. The advance must lie in their own negative relation. Each maintains itself by negating the others' independence, while each equally resists being reduced to a mere being-for-other (*SL* §§349–353). This defeats the claimed self-preservation: the negation through which each was to return into itself is rendered ineffectual by the others' resistance. If its independent being consists in that return, a negation that fails to accomplish it cannot secure that being (*SL* §354). This is not the disappearance of the many; it is the failure of their claim to maintain themselves as independently exclusive Ones.

The positive result is present in exclusion itself. The One relates to other Ones, hence to its own determination: its negative relation proves to be a **going-together-with-self**, **Attraction** (*Attraktion*, *Anziehung*) (*SL* §355). What attraction sublates is the *independence* and mutual externality that repulsion was meant to secure — not the many themselves. It does not draw them into a solitary point: "it does not absorb the attracted ones into itself as into a centre, that is, it does not sublate them abstractly. Since it contains repulsion in its determination, this latter at the same time preserves the ones as many in it" (*SL* §360). Were attraction "conceived as accomplished, the many being brought to the point of the one one, then there would be present only an inert one and no longer any attraction" (*SL* §358). Completed absorption would abolish attraction, not rebound into repulsion; their truth is not an oscillation between total scattering and total collapse.

Yet mutual dependence alone is not the completed argument. Repulsion seems to require already existing Ones; but their plurality is its own positing, and their maintenance as Ones is their repelling. What it presupposes is therefore its own activity, not an independent given (*SL* §367). Attraction seems to require independently repelling Ones whose externality it then overcomes; but their ideality already belongs to them as Ones with the same determination, including the One conceived as attracting. Its unity is not first imposed by a foreign centre (*SL* §368). In this **self-presupposing**, each posits itself as other — the moment of repulsion — while what it presupposes is itself — the moment of attraction. **Each contains the other as a moment within it** (*SL* §369). Their mediation through the other has proved to be self-mediation: not two independent agents subsequently balanced, but the single process in which the One posits and sublates its own otherness.

## VII. The Self-Sublation of the Qualitative One — and the Passage into Quantity

Stand back and see what this single activity has done to the qualitative One. The One was supposed to be the determination of *exclusive*, *self-related* being — the very emblem of qualitative determinacy at its sharpest: *this* one, excluding all else. But the dialectic of Repulsion and Attraction has shown that the One's exclusiveness *undoes itself*: its supposed immediate independence proves to be a mediation, the otherness it posits is its own, and its return out of that otherness is its own self-relation. The One is therefore present throughout the process "only as sublated" (*SL* §370). Its limit no longer cuts it off absolutely from its other; the One **continues itself into that other** (*SL* §369). The qualitative determinacy of the One — its being *this* exclusive unit — has thereby made itself **indifferent**. What is left is not an indifferent heap of look-alikes, but the excluding limit preserved within being as an indifferent moment: "the limit which is no limit, which is present in being but is indifferent to it" (*SL* §372).

And *that* — a determinateness that has become indifferent to the being it determines, "a limit which is just as much no limit" — is no longer Quality. It is **Quantity** (*Quantität*) (*SL* §387). In Quality, the determinacy *was* the being: to alter the quality was to make the thing cease to be *this* and become something else. In Quantity, the determinacy has come loose from the being: a thing can grow or shrink, be one or many, more or less, *without ceasing to be what it is*. The One-and-Many has produced exactly this loosening. But quantity appears first as **pure quantity**, and — this is easy to miss — in the form of *continuity*, not of a countable heap: "Quantity is the unity of these moments of continuity and discreteness, but at first it is so in the form of one of them, *continuity*" (*SL* §398). Attraction is retained here as the moment of continuity, repulsion as that of discreteness (*SL* §§395–397). The unit of counting has its *ground* here, but not yet its development: Number requires the limitation of quantity and the articulation of the Quantum, where amount and unit first become moments (§§15–16; *SL* §§434–441).

This is the first passage from one sub-sphere of the Doctrine of Being to the next, and it has the form proper to the sphere of Being: a **passing-over** (*Übergehen*), distinct from Essence's shining-into and the Concept's developing-out-of (§06). The exclusive One cannot sustain its immediate independence; its excluding limit is sublated, and the One continues itself into its other. **Preservation here has a definite positive form:** attraction is continuity, repulsion is discreteness within that continuity, and the One remains quantity's own moment through which a limit can be posited (§15). This is not a thinning of the earlier content (§13), nor the retention of every determination unchanged. The governing determination has changed: the limit remains, but no longer as immediately identical with the being it determines.

## VIII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

As with "negation" and with "the infinite," three standing misreadings are worth meeting head-on.

The first is the **physicalist misreading**: that the One is an atom and the Void is empty space, so Hegel is doing armchair physics — deducing the structure of matter from his desk. Hegel draws the boundary himself: "matter as it exists for sense perception is no more a subject matter of logic than are space and its determinations. But the forces of attraction and repulsion, in so far as they are regarded as forces of empirical matter, are also based on the pure determinations here considered of the one and the many and their inter-relationships" (*SL* §375). He thus claims a logical ground for the empirical forces, without making them the subject matter of this deduction. The argument traced here exhibits repulsion and attraction as moments of being-for-self; connecting those moments to determinate physical forces requires the further account of space and matter that the passage expressly excludes. The shared names cannot supply that account. Reading a later physics alongside it is a comparison **this synopsis** may draw, not a confirmation the *Logic* earns. Whether a particular theory has overcome the externality criticized here must be established from that theory, not inferred from its vocabulary; §03 admits modern physics as "an illustration, though not a confirmation" and insists that "the direction is not a law".

The second is the **reified-forces misreading**: that Repulsion and Attraction are two forces Hegel claims to derive, each a thing in its own right that bodies "have." The logical argument yields no such independent entities: they are *moments of one self-relating negativity*. To hypostatize either — to make repulsion-plus-void a complete account, or to erect attraction into a self-standing principle — erases the mediation just traced. But distinguishing fundamental forces in a physical account is not already the claim that either could constitute matter alone. Kant's argument for their constitutive inseparability remains an achievement (§V); Hegel's demand for logical self-mediation must engage it, not replace it with an easier target.

The third is the **One-as-Absolute misreading**: treating the One as a final, self-sufficient unity rather than a determination whose own movement carries it beyond itself. Hegel does present atomism as a historical definition of the Absolute through being-for-self, the One and the Many (*Enc.* §98 Remark). That does not make this definition adequate as the final truth. Nor does the shared language of unity identify the qualitative One with every philosophical doctrine of the One: Hegel himself distinguishes Eleatic Being from this more determinate form (*SL* §§325, 337). At the other extreme, the One is not already the arithmetical 1. Number requires the further development of Quantity; reading it back into *das Eins* skips the very transition at issue. The result here is neither a supreme unity beyond development nor a ready-made counting unit, but pure Quantity, from whose articulation Number will come.

## IX. What the Method Did Here — Observations

Two observations on this step, not a substitute for the work of later ones.

The first: **the opposed determinations proved to contain one another, and their mediation proved to be self-mediation.** The decisive advance was not merely the observation that each needs the other. Repulsion's supposedly given Ones proved to be its own positing and self-maintenance; attraction's supposedly foreign unity proved to be the ideality of the Ones themselves (*SL* §§367–369). Each thus found itself in what it had seemed merely to presuppose. This is a more determinate account of the self-return reached in §13, not that result renamed in a new vocabulary. Its warrant lies in the intervening movement: the One's immediate form, the positing of the Many, and the defeat of exclusive self-preservation. The question is not which of these logical moments is fundamental in isolation, but how their claim to independence is sublated. A physical inquiry into distinct forces is not answered simply by transferring that conclusion to it.

The second, and deeper for the synopsis's governing theme: **quantitative indifference has a qualitative genesis.** In Hegel's order, quantity is not a primitive given: its indifference is the result of qualitative exclusiveness sublating itself. This is the logical ground of the repeatable unit, not yet the development of Number; that requires quantity's further limitation (§§15–16), and no theory of measurement has been supplied merely by this transition. The gain for our investigation of method is a question about the grounds and limits of quantification, not a rival foundation for all mathematics. Mathematical reasoning can be adequate to its determinate tasks without deriving the category of quantity; Hegel himself grants mathematics a method appropriate to its objects (*SL* §62; §27). What is challenged is the treatment of quantitative form as an unconditioned starting point or an exhaustive account of its subject matter, not the validity of a proof merely because it proceeds under stated assumptions. Quantity's own development must show where its indifference ceases to suffice. The comparison with nodal points, thresholds, and phase transitions belongs to the further labor of Measure and is **ours to earn there** — a scientific illustration, not a deduction from the *Logic* (§03).

## X. What Comes Next

The next installment (§15) opens the second sub-sphere of the Doctrine of Being with pure Quantity, its moments of continuity and discreteness, and the emergence of the Quantum. The outline below ranges beyond that single installment: §16 develops Number, degree, and the quantitative infinite; §17 the quantitative ratio and the threshold of Measure; §18 Measure itself.

- **Quantity** (*Quantität*) — pure quantity as the sublated being-for-self, the determinacy that has become indifferent to its being; its two moments, **continuity** and **discreteness**, and why each requires the other; the **quantum** as limited quantity, and **number** (amount and unit) as its determinate form.
- **The quantitative infinite** — the endless progress of "ever larger / ever smaller," to be derived from the quantum's own indifferent limit rather than inferred from a resemblance to §13; and the *qualitative* moment that emerges in the quantitative ratio.
- **The passage toward Measure** — how quantity, pursued to its limit, reveals that it is not after all indifferent to quality, and how the two rejoin in **Measure**, the truth of the sphere of Being and the threshold of Essence.

With the move into Quantity, the *Logic* has completed the sphere of **Quality** and begun the second of the three great determinations of Being.

---

## Coda

We have now watched the qualitative sphere close upon itself, and close in a way worth pausing over. It did not end by reaching some final, stable quality; it ended with the exclusive One proving to be only the movement in which it distinguishes itself from itself and continues itself in its other. There is something almost vertiginous in this: the One's attempt to maintain exclusive independence depends on the very relation it would keep external to itself. Following that contradiction, rather than applying a rule that extremes turn into opposites, has yielded the unity whose continuous and discrete moments the next installment will unfold. Quality becomes Quantity — not because qualitative determinacy has vanished, but because the limit that was constitutive of being now persists in it as an indifferent, sublated moment. And the reader who has followed from pure Being to here has seen the first complete sphere of the *Logic* pass beyond itself — not exhausted, but sublated, carried forward into a sphere whose indifference must be developed with the same patience by which its qualitative ground was won. *Knowing where quantity comes from gives us the next problem, not its solution: what does this indifferent determinacy itself require?*

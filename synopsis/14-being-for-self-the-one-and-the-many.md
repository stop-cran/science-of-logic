# Being-for-self, the One, and the Many

*The fifth installment of Section III — a close reading of the determination in which the true infinite first comes to rest: **being-for-self** (*Fürsichsein*), being that has taken its otherness wholly inside itself and relates only to itself; its moment of **being-for-one** (*Sein-für-Eines*), in which the Other survives only as something ideal; the collapse of being-for-self into the **One** (*das Eins*); the **One and the Void**, and the logical truth of atomism; the dialectic of **Repulsion** and **Attraction** by which the One posits the Many and the Many collapse toward the One; and the self-sublation of the qualitative One that carries the whole sphere of Quality over into **Quantity**.*

---

## I. Where We Are: from the True Infinite to Being-for-self

The previous installment closed with the **true infinite** — not a *unity of finite and infinite* as two standing terms, but the *becoming*, the double negation in which each side, passing into the other, returns to itself through it, neither persisting as an independent term (§13; *SL* §301). We named its result but did not develop it: being that no longer points beyond itself to an Other that bounds it, but is *with itself*. That being is **being-for-self** (*Fürsichsein*), and unfolding it is the work of this installment.

It is worth being precise about what kind of step this is. The whole movement from *Dasein* through Something, Limit, and finitude was driven by *the relation to an Other*: at every stage the determinate being was what it was by reference to something it was not. The true infinite already broke that dependence — that was the previous installment's result, and it is not won a second time here. What is new is the **form** in which the result is held. In the true infinite, being-with-itself was a *movement*: a going-out into otherness and a return, the unity legible only in the whole back-and-forth. In being-for-self that movement has **collapsed into simple being** — self-relation is no longer the outcome of a return but the determination's own immediate character. The Other is still present, but only as a moment within, no longer as an outside, and no longer as a passage that must be traversed to reach the unity. This is the qualitative sphere's last great determination; with it, and with the dialectic of the One and the Many that it generates, Quality completes itself and passes over into Quantity.

## II. Being-for-self and Ideality

Recall the two minimal sides of any determinacy, named long ago: **Reality** (*Realität*), the affirmative *being-thus*, and the negative side that, taken strictly, is the determinacy's *not-being-otherwise*. But that negative side did not first become inward here. The Limit had already become the Something's own immanent determination (§12), and finitude had made non-being the thing's own nature — "something with its immanent limit posited as the contradiction of itself, through which it is directed and forced out of and beyond itself, is the *finite*" (*SL* §247). What the true infinite achieved, and what being-for-self now holds in the form of simple being, is the negation of *that* negation: otherness is no longer an independent limit standing over against the affirmative, but a sublated moment of the being's own self-relation. This sublated status of the otherwise-external is what Hegel calls **ideality** (*Idealität*).

Being-for-self is therefore the category in which **ideality takes the form of simple being**. We met the *word* in the previous installment, in the proposition that the finite is *ideal*; but there it named the status the finite acquires *inside* the true infinite. Now we have the determination whose very content *is* that ideality: being that is affirmatively itself precisely because it has reduced its otherness to a moment. But Reality and Ideality are not therefore two opposed qualitative sides — a real bearer on one hand, its merely ideal contents on the other. Hegel raises that distinction only to withdraw it: the whole and its moments are alike ideal, and neither holds an independent reality over against the other. The distinction actually to be unfolded is that of **being-for-self** and **being-for-one**, which are "not different meanings of ideality, but are essential, inseparable moments of it" (*SL* §§321–324). This is the consummation of the qualitative: a being that is fully determinate and yet, in its determinacy, *related only to itself*.

The temptation, here as everywhere, is to picture this as a *thing* that has swallowed its environment. Resist it. Being-for-self is not yet a thing, not an object, not a self in any psychological sense. It is the bare logical form of *self-relation that has its otherness within as ideal* — the form, as we will see, that the I, the atom, the monad, and the living individual will each later instantiate, but which at this level is only that form, stripped to its minimum.

## III. Being-for-one — the Other Turned Inward

How does the internalized otherness show itself within being-for-self? As the moment Hegel names, with deliberate awkwardness, **being-for-one** (*Sein-für-Eines*).

The phrase is built to be heard against its predecessor. *Being-for-other* (§12) was the side on which the Something stood exposed to an Other genuinely outside it. *Being-for-one* is what *being-for-other becomes* once the Other has been internalized: the being is now "for" something, yes — but the something it is for is no longer an external other; it is *itself*. Hegel makes the point through a quirk of German idiom. The ordinary question *"Was für ein Ding ist das?"* — "what kind of thing is this?" — says, word for word, "*what for a* thing is this?" In asking what something is *for*, ordinary speech already half-knows that to ask what a thing is *for* is to ask what it is *in itself*: the "for" and the "what it is" coincide. So in being-for-one, *that for which the being is, and the being itself, are one and the same*. The relation-to-other has become a relation-to-self conducted through an otherness that is now only ideal.

This is exactly the "transformed form" in which the Other survives that the previous installment promised. The Other is not gone — being-for-self would be empty without the otherness it has sublated — but it no longer stands over against the being as an independent term. It persists *as* the being's own being-for-one: as the ideal moment through which the being relates to itself. Hold this carefully, because the next move looks, at first, like a sudden hardening, and it can only be understood as the result of this internalization.

## IV. The One

Press being-for-self for what it now is. It is self-relation; its otherness is wholly internal and ideal; it refers, through that ideal otherness, only to itself. But a self-relation in which the otherness has become *entirely* ideal — utterly without standing of its own — is a self-relation with *no internal distinction left to articulate*. The two sides (the being, and that-for-which-it-is) have collapsed into bare identity. What remains is a simple, immediate, self-related unit that excludes all otherness from itself: the **One** (*das Eins*).

The One is the qualitative determination driven to its extreme of simplicity. It has three features, and they will generate everything that follows. First, it is **simple self-relation** — within it there is no manifold, no parts, no internal difference; it is the very form of indivisible unity. Second, it is **indeterminate** — but not as pure Being was indeterminate (through sheer emptiness); the One's indeterminateness is the *result* of a negation, the determinateness of having excluded all otherness, so it is indeterminate *by way of* being utterly self-enclosed. Third, and decisively, it is **self-relating negation**: the One *is* what it is only by negating, by excluding, by being-not-other. And this third feature is unstable in a precise way — though not because negating has run out of an object and must go looking for one elsewhere. The One is at once the simple immediacy in which all difference has vanished and the self-related negation through which that immediacy is determined. Its very nothing is therefore a *posited* nothing. The next steps will distinguish that nothing *within* the One, and only then set it over against the One's affirmative being: first as the void, then as other Ones.

## V. The One and the Void — Atomism and Its Logical Truth

In the One's simple immediacy all mediation and manifoldness has vanished: there is *nothing* in it. But this nothing is itself **posited** — distinguished from the being-within-self whose abstraction it is. Posited *in the One*, it is the **Void** (*das Leere*), and as such it is first of all the One's own *quality*: "This nothing, then, posited as *in the one*, is the nothing as the *void*. The void is thus the *quality* of the one in its immediacy" (*SL* §334). Only then, "as distinct from the affirmative being of the one", is the void posited *outside* it (*SL* §335). The One and the Void are thus not a fullness first given and an empty room afterwards required; they are the moments of being-for-self fallen apart into determinate being.

The One and the Void: this is the logical skeleton of **atomism**, and Hegel pauses on it deliberately, for atomism is one of the great speculative achievements of ancient thought. Leucippus and Democritus grasped, with extraordinary penetration, that the structure of a world can be built from just two principles — the full (the atom, the One) and the empty (the void) — and Hegel credits *the first* of these thinkers with a further speculative insight: that the void is the **ground** of movement. He himself separates this from the trivial version — that a thing can only move into unoccupied space — in which the void would be "only the presupposition or condition of movement, not its *ground*"; the profounder thought is "that in the negative as such there lies the ground of becoming, of the unrest of self-movement" (*SL* §338). Hegel's verdict is double, and the doubleness is instructive. On one side, he honors atomism as *speculative*: it seized the One and its negation as principles, which is a far deeper start than the sensuous philosophies of stuff that preceded it. On the other side, he diagnoses its one-sidedness: atomism *externalizes* the negativity. It treats the void as something simply *found*, lying outside the atom, when in truth the void is *the One's own negativity*, the One's own self-relating negation displaced outward and given a separate name. The atom and the void are not two independent finds; they are the One and its own immanent nothing, torn apart and set side by side.

Hegel himself carries the diagnosis into physics, and in the same breath into political theory: "Physics with its molecules and particles suffers from the atom, this principle of extreme externality, which is thus utterly devoid of the Notion, just as much as does that theory of the State which starts from the particular will of individuals" (*SL* §339). The defect is one and the same in both — independent units first, their relation added afterwards from outside. He presses the point at length against Kant's construction of matter: Kant deserves credit for deriving matter from two opposed determinations rather than from impenetrability alone, and yet "his two fundamental forces within matter remain external to and completely independent of each other", and a procedure that presupposes matter is offered as its construction — "Kant's method is basically analytical, not constructive" (*SL* §§377–380). How far any *later* physics has overcome that externality is a question to be settled from that physics, theory by theory; the present deduction issues no verdict on it in advance (§03). What the logical form itself *demands*, and what atomism-with-only-the-void cannot supply, is now to be drawn out: the One does not merely face an empty other; it posits *other Ones*.

## VI. Repulsion and Attraction

The One is self-relating negation. To relate to itself *as negation* is to repel: the One, in being the One it is, *repels itself from itself* and thereby posits **many Ones**. This positing of the Many by the One is **Repulsion** (*Repulsion*, *Abstoßung*). It is not that several Ones were lying about and pushed apart; it is that the very negativity constitutive of *one* One generates plurality. Plurality is not imported from outside the One; it is the One's own act.

But now watch the peculiar thing — the hinge on which the whole passage to Quantity turns. The many Ones that Repulsion posits are, each of them, *simply One*. Each is the same bare self-excluding unit; there is, within any of them, nothing to distinguish it from any other. The One repelled *itself*, and what it produced is only *itself over again*, indefinitely. So the plurality that Repulsion generates is a plurality of the *qualitatively indistinguishable*. The Ones differ *numerically* — there are many — but not *qualitatively* — each is just a One. Their distinction is therefore wholly external: it falls not into any One's character but only into the bare "and another, and another."

This self-sameness of the many Ones is, at first, a comparison *we* make. The advance lies in their own negative relation: each maintains itself only by excluding the others, and in excluding what is itself a One, its relation to its other proves to be a relation to itself. This going-together-with-self is **Attraction** (*Attraktion*, *Anziehung*). What attraction sublates is the *independence* and mutual externality that repulsion was meant to secure — not the many themselves. It does not draw them into a solitary point: "it does not absorb the attracted ones into itself as into a centre, that is, it does not sublate them abstractly. Since it contains repulsion in its determination, this latter at the same time preserves the ones as many in it" (*SL* §360).

Repulsion and Attraction are not two independent forces that happen to coexist (the error of taking atomism's repulsion-and-void as complete, or of taking Newtonian attraction as a self-standing principle). **Each contains the other as a moment within it** (*SL* §369). Repulsion is not mere dispersion: even its excluding remains a *relation* to what it excludes, and that moment of relation is already attraction (*SL* §363). Attraction is not a completed absorption: were it "conceived as accomplished, the many being brought to the point of the one one, then there would be present only an inert one and no longer any attraction" (*SL* §358) — accomplished attraction would abolish *itself*, not rebound into repulsion. Their truth is therefore not an oscillation between total scattering and total collapse. Each presupposes the other, and closer examination shows that what each presupposes is in fact its own positing: mediation through the other proves to be self-mediation (*SL* §§365–369). They are two moments of a *single* self-relating negativity. The understanding holds them apart as two forces in balance; the truth is that they are one self-sublating activity.

## VII. The Self-Sublation of the Qualitative One — and the Passage into Quantity

Stand back and see what this single activity has done to the qualitative One. The One was supposed to be the determination of *exclusive*, *self-related* being — the very emblem of qualitative determinacy at its sharpest: *this* one, excluding all else. But the dialectic of Repulsion and Attraction has shown that the One's exclusiveness *undoes itself*: its supposed immediate independence proves to be a mediation, the otherness it posits is its own, and its return out of that otherness is its own self-relation. The One is therefore present throughout the process "only as sublated" (*SL* §370). Its limit no longer cuts it off absolutely from its other; the One **continues itself into that other** (*SL* §369). The qualitative determinacy of the One — its being *this* exclusive unit — has thereby made itself **indifferent**. What is left is not an indifferent heap of look-alikes, but the excluding limit preserved within being as an indifferent moment: "the limit which is no limit, which is present in being but is indifferent to it" (*SL* §372).

And *that* — a determinateness that has become indifferent to the being it determines, "a limit which is just as much no limit" — is no longer Quality. It is **Quantity** (*Quantität*) (*SL* §387). In Quality, the determinacy *was* the being: to alter the quality was to make the thing cease to be *this* and become something else. In Quantity, the determinacy has come loose from the being: a thing can grow or shrink, be one or many, more or less, *without ceasing to be what it is*. The One-and-Many has produced exactly this loosening. But quantity appears first as **pure quantity**, and — this is easy to miss — in the form of *continuity*, not of a countable heap: "Quantity is the unity of these moments of continuity and discreteness, but at first it is so in the form of one of them, *continuity*" (*SL* §398). Attraction is retained here as the moment of continuity, repulsion as that of discreteness (*SL* §§395–397). The unit of counting has its *ground* here, but not yet its development: Number requires the limitation of quantity and the articulation of the Quantum, where amount and unit first become moments (§§15–16; *SL* §§434–441).

This is the first passage from one sub-sphere of the Doctrine of Being to the next, and it has the form proper to the sphere of Being: it is a **passing-over** (*Übergehen*). Quality does not "shine into" Quantity (that relational mode belongs to Essence) nor "develop into" it (that belongs to the Concept); it *passes over* into it, by driving its own central determination — the exclusive One — to the point where the excluding limit is sublated and the One continues itself into its other, so that the qualitative determinant is retained only as the indifferent moment of a unity of continuity and discreteness. Quality is not left behind; it is sublated. (Every quantum will still have, as we will see in the sphere of Quantity, a qualitative moment — a *this-much* that is, after all, a determinacy. The qualitative is preserved, in the thinned, being-sphere way the previous installment described. But the *governing* determination has changed: determinacy and being have come apart.)

## VIII. Three Misreadings, Answered Directly

As with "negation" and with "the infinite," three standing misreadings are worth meeting head-on.

The first is the **physicalist misreading**: that the One is an atom and the Void is empty space, so Hegel is doing armchair physics — deducing the structure of matter from his desk. This inverts the relation, and Hegel draws the boundary himself: "matter as it exists for sense perception is no more a subject matter of logic than are space and its determinations. But the forces of attraction and repulsion, in so far as they are regarded as forces of empirical matter, are also based on the pure determinations here considered of the one and the many and their inter-relationships" (*SL* §375). The connection is therefore real but strictly ordered: the *Logic* does not deduce the physical atom; it exhibits repulsion and attraction as moments of being-for-self, on which the empirical forces in turn rest. Reading a *later* physics alongside it — fields, quanta, forces of kinds Democritus never dreamt — is a comparison **this synopsis** may draw, not a confirmation the *Logic* earns. Whether any particular theory has overcome the externality criticized here must be established from that theory, not inferred from its vocabulary; the corpus has already fixed this limit, admitting modern physics as "an illustration, though not a confirmation" and insisting that "the direction is not a law" (§03).

The second is the **reified-forces misreading**: that Repulsion and Attraction are two forces Hegel claims to derive, each a thing in its own right that bodies "have." This is precisely the error the section diagnoses. Repulsion and Attraction are not independent forces but *moments of one self-relating negativity*; to hypostatize either — to make repulsion-plus-void a complete account, or to erect attraction into a self-standing principle — is to repeat atomism's one-sidedness at the level of dynamics. Hegel's target here is not physics' use of forces but the *metaphysics* that takes forces to be ultimate, externally-related things.

The third is the **One-as-Absolute misreading**: that Hegel's One is the One of Parmenides or of Neoplatonism — the supreme Unity, the Absolute as One — or, at the other extreme, that it is already the arithmetical 1. Both are out of place. The numeral 1 belongs to Quantity, which has not yet opened; to read it back into *das Eins* is to skip the very transition this installment traces. And the One-as-Absolute belongs to a wholly different level of the system: the One here is the *minimal qualitative self-excluding unit*, the thinnest form of being-for-self, not the divine Unity of the whole. The whole interest of the passage is that *this* humble One, pressed, dissolves its own exclusiveness and gives birth not to the Absolute but to pure Quantity, from whose further articulation Number will come.

## IX. What the Method Did Here — Observations

Two observations on the step, each holding at later steps as well.

The first: **the opposed determinations proved to contain one another, and their mediation proved to be self-mediation.** This is by now a familiar signature, but the present case sharpens it. Repulsion contains attraction, because even its excluding remains a relation to what it excludes; attraction contains repulsion, because its unity preserves the many as moments within it. Neither first stands alone and then calls its opposite into being; the deeper point is that each finds, in what it seemed merely to presuppose, its own positing (*SL* §§365–369). This is the same lesson as finitude-and-infinity, now in dynamical dress: the understanding's fixed, mutually-external determinations are, read for their own movement, moments of a single process in which each "continu[es] itself in its other" (*SL* §369). The reader who has internalized this no longer asks "which is fundamental, repulsion or attraction?" — the question itself belongs to the understanding. The fundamental thing is the self-mediating movement whose two faces they are.

The second, and deeper for the synopsis's governing theme: **quantitative indifference has a qualitative genesis.** The "one" that mathematics and measurement presuppose — the bare, repeatable, qualitatively-empty unit that can be added to itself indifferently — is not a primitive given. It has a *logical ground*: what the qualitative One becomes when its exclusiveness has worked itself into indifference. That ground is what this installment secures; Number itself, and with it any theory of measurement, still lies ahead (§§15–16). This matters greatly for the question that animates the whole synopsis — the place of Hegel's method in the scientific paradigm. Quantification, the very medium of exact science, is not the bedrock beneath which one cannot dig. To see this is to see both why mathematics is so powerful (it works in the medium of pure indifferent determinacy, where everything is external and combinable) and why mathematics is not self-sufficient (its indifferent unit is the residue of a qualitative determination it has forgotten). The *Logic* will spend the whole sphere of Quantity, and then Measure, earning back the qualitative that the passage into Quantity set aside. The comparison with nodal points, thresholds, and phase transitions belongs to *that* labor and is **ours to earn there** — a scientific illustration, not a deduction from the *Logic* (§03).

## X. What Comes Next

The next installment (§15) opens the second sub-sphere of the Doctrine of Being with pure Quantity, its moments of continuity and discreteness, and the emergence of the Quantum. The outline below ranges beyond that single installment: §16 develops Number, degree, and the quantitative infinite; §17 the quantitative ratio and the threshold of Measure; §18 Measure itself.

- **Quantity** (*Quantität*) — pure quantity as the sublated being-for-self, the determinacy that has become indifferent to its being; its two moments, **continuity** and **discreteness**, and why each requires the other; the **quantum** as limited quantity, and **number** (amount and unit) as its determinate form.
- **The quantitative infinite** — the endless progress of "ever larger / ever smaller," which we will recognize at once as a *spurious* infinite of the form already diagnosed, and the *qualitative* moment that lurks in the quantitative ratio.
- **The passage toward Measure** — how quantity, pursued to its limit, reveals that it is not after all indifferent to quality, and how the two rejoin in **Measure**, the truth of the sphere of Being and the threshold of Essence.

With the move into Quantity, the *Logic* has completed the sphere of **Quality** and begun the second of the three great determinations of Being.

---

## Coda

We have now watched the qualitative sphere close upon itself, and close in a way worth pausing over. It did not end by reaching some final, stable quality; it ended with the exclusive One proving to be only the movement in which it distinguishes itself from itself and continues itself in its other. There is something almost vertiginous in this: the most rigidly self-asserting of the qualitative categories, the One that excludes everything, is exactly the category whose exclusiveness sublates itself. The lesson is one the *Logic* will repeat in higher keys: a determination pressed to its extreme does not harden into permanence but tips over into its opposite. The exclusive One has not yielded an indifferent heap; it has yielded the unity whose continuous and discrete moments the next installment will unfold. Quality becomes Quantity — not because qualitative determinacy has vanished, but because the limit that was constitutive of being now persists in it as an indifferent, sublated moment. And the reader who has followed from pure Being to here has now seen the first complete sphere of the *Logic* trace its arc and pass beyond itself — not exhausted, but sublated, carried forward into a sphere whose indifference must now be developed with the same patience by which its qualitative ground was won, and where everything we have learned about determinacy will have to be learned again in the strange new element of the indifferent, the external, the more-and-less. *What was hardest to win — that determinacy is never merely fixed but always in movement — is exactly what makes the next sphere legible before we enter it.*

# Quality, Something, and Other

*The second installment of Section III — a close reading of the first internal articulation of Dasein: its determinacy as **Quality**, distinguished into **Reality** and **Negation**; the emergence of **Something** (*Etwas*) as simple, mediated self-relation; and the development through the **Other** and alteration to **being-in-itself** and **being-for-other** — with misreadings of "negation" answered inline.*

---

## I. Where We Are, and What Dasein Contains

The previous installment closed with *Dasein* as the result of Becoming's self-cancellation. Its immediacy is *resulted*: Being and Nothing no longer stand as independent abstractions but are preserved in a determinate unity. Non-being belongs to that unity internally; it is not something subsequently brought against an otherwise indeterminate being. This gives *Dasein* an *internal field of articulation* that pure Being and pure Nothing did not have. The promise of the previous installment was that this field — Quality, Reality, Negation — would now be developed.

§02 framed *Dasein* with the introductory phrase "being-this-rather-than-that." As §10 now makes explicit, that phrase anticipates a development; it must not be used as if a Something and its Other had already been derived. The present starting point is more modest: being with non-being taken into its simple unity. The determinacy is not yet a property attached to an independently given thing. It is inseparable from the being itself. To develop this content, rather than extract conclusions from the grammar of our introductory phrase, is the task here.

The **immanence** of Hegel's exposition was already exhibited in the opening move (§10). Here it takes the form of articulating the content of the result: Quality names its immediate determinacy, and Reality and Negation distinguish moments within it. They are not imported ingredients. But naming what is already present is only the beginning; the distinction must itself be examined, and its result must preserve what the examination has established.

## II. Quality, Reality, and Negation

The determinacy of *Dasein*, taken as immediate and as being, is **Quality** (*Qualität*). Quality is not first produced by combining an affirmative and a negative ingredient. It is the simple determinacy from which Hegel distinguishes Reality and Negation (*SL* §§195–197). Being and determinacy are not yet two independently subsisting terms: this is determinate being, not a bare being to which a quality is subsequently added.

With the accent on its **affirmative** being, Quality is **Reality** (*Realität*). Reality is not, here, "the totality of what exists" or "the world out there." It is Quality taken in the determination of being. Its limitation is already in view: the determinacy belongs to a unity of being and non-being, but when Reality is taken as merely positive, the negation it contains is concealed (*SL* §198).

Quality must therefore also be taken in the **negative** determination already contained in *Dasein*. This is **Negation** (*Negation*): not pure Nothing, but non-being as belonging to a determinate being. Hegel initially characterizes this side as quality counted as a deficiency, to be developed further as limit and restriction. Even so, it is a quality, not the mere absence of all being (*SL* §§197–198). Nor have we yet derived its more determinate form as the relation of Something to an Other.

Reality and Negation are thus distinguishable determinations of the same Quality, not two substances or two ingredients. Reality cannot exclude negation and remain determinate; Negation cannot be reduced to pure Nothing and remain the qualitative determination it is. Both belong to *Dasein*. The task is to hold their distinction without making either side independent of what it excludes.

The order matters: Quality is first the immediate determinacy; Reality and Negation articulate it; their distinction and the sublation of that distinction will yield **Something**. Calling Quality simply the unity of two ready-made sides would obscure the movement that is to produce this result.

## III. Anticipating the Standard Misreadings

Three misreadings are worth confronting directly. Each substitutes a familiar meaning for the particular determination being developed here.

The first is the **representational** misreading: that "Reality" must mean "what really exists out there," "Negation" must mean "what does not exist," and Hegel is therefore making a strange claim about how non-existent things contribute to existent ones. The misreading is understandable, since "reality" in everyday and even much philosophical English carries the meaning of "the totality of what is the case." But the present argument concerns *determinacy*, not the contribution of non-existent objects to existent ones. Reality is quality with the accent on being; Negation is the non-being belonging to that determinacy. Neither term is a name for a separate population of things.

The second is the **formal-operator** misreading: that "Negation" in Hegel must be the formal operator ¬, and Hegel's claims about Negation are claims about ¬ — claims which are then either trivial or false. The formal operator acts on propositions, whereas the present object is determinacy itself. Hegel uses and discusses propositions well before the Doctrine of the Concept; there he develops Judgment as a logical form and distinguishes it from the mere proposition (§28). At the level of *Dasein*, the Negation at issue is the non-being internal to determinate being. Placing ¬ as the fixed, abstract counterpart of dialectical Negation is this synopsis's comparison, not a derivation supplied by the chapter on quality. §27 separately examines Hegel's polemic against logical calculi in the light of his own methodological requirements. Two cautions come with the comparison. Fixing determinations is the Understanding's achievement; stopping at their fixity is what Hegel calls "a **subjective impotence of reason**", and he rejects the usual separation of the two (§27). Nor are these two rival operators, one defective and the other sound: here negation *determines a being*, it does not *deny a proposition*.

The third is the **mere-difference** misreading: that "Negation" is just a fancy word for "difference," and Hegel's whole machinery here is a heavy-handed way of saying things differ. To say that *Dasein* contains Negation is not to compare two already given things. Non-being belongs to the constitution of determinate being itself, before Something and Other have been distinguished. Difference established by external comparison — what §21 will call **diversity**, where the difference has fallen outside the things and into a comparing third — does not supply that argument. This does not make all difference external: §21 develops **difference in itself** as self-related negativity, precisely *not* a difference between two given things. The contrast here is with external comparison, not with every logical form of difference.

A fourth temptation is to hold Reality and Negation as *independently fixed* affirmative and negative sides. Reality is determinate, and therefore already contains Negation. Negation, here, is not abstract Nothing but a *determinate* being, "posited here as it is in itself, as affirmatively present" and belonging to the sphere of determinate being (*SL* §208). Hegel's conclusion is flat: the distinctions are "present in determinate being" and "no less equally void and sublated." What fails is not determinacy but the independence claimed for its sides. Their mutual inclusion does not establish an asymmetry; it establishes why the distinction cannot stand in that independent form. This specific failure, not the mere presence of opposition, drives the next step.

## IV. From Quality to Something — the First Negation of the Negation

Hold the structure firmly. The immediate determinacy of *Dasein* is Quality; Reality and Negation are its distinguished moments. Neither has sustained the independence implied by taking one as merely affirmative and the other as merely negative. The distinction is present, but each side contains what it was to exclude.

Notice: the Negation in *Dasein* is *not* an external negation that some other being supplies. It is the non-being already belonging to its determinacy. Neither a second thing nor an act of denial is required to introduce it.

The second negation is not one we apply. The distinction of Reality and Negation, pressed, sublates *itself* — and Hegel is careful to say what that sublating is not: it is "more than a mere taking back and external omission of it again, or than a simple return to the simple beginning," because "the distinction cannot be omitted, for it *is*" (*SL* §209). Three things are therefore present at once: determinate being, the distinction in it, and the sublation of that distinction. What results is *Dasein* "*again* equal to itself through sublation of the distinction, the simple oneness of determinate being *resulting* from this sublation." And the decisive clause: "this sublatedness of the distinction is determinate being's own determinateness; it is thus *being-within-self*" (*SL* §209). Not our regrouping of two sides into a unity, then — the determinacy's own result.

Hegel calls this self-related unity **Something** (*Etwas*).

Something is the *first negation of the negation* in the *Logic*. The first negation at issue is qualitative Negation, the non-being in *Dasein*'s determinacy — not pure Being's vanishing into pure Nothing counted as the first application of an operator. The second negation sublates the distinction just developed, without deleting it. The result is not a return to undifferentiated immediacy: the sublated distinction is now the determinacy of the unity itself. This is how a determinate being is *one*, related to itself through its own negation.

The phrase "negation of the negation" would explain nothing if merely counting two negations were supposed to prove the result. Its warrant here is the definite movement just traced. The qualitative negation and the sublation of the distinction are not the same operation performed twice. Hegel states the result: "Something is the negation of the negation in the form of *being*; for this second negation is the restoring of the simple relation to self; but with this, something is equally *the mediation of itself with itself*" (*SL* §211). And he explicitly distinguishes the two senses: "Care must be taken to distinguish between the *first* negation as negation in *general*, and the second negation, the negation of the negation: the latter is concrete, *absolute* negativity, just as the former on the contrary is only *abstract* negativity" (*SL* §210). The name records an argument; it does not replace it.

A useful contrast: **Something** names the determinate being whose simple oneness is mediated through the sublation of its own distinction. Merely pointing and saying "this" would not establish that result. Hegel notes that "this" "contains no distinction," that "each and every something is just as well a 'this' as it is also an other," and that the pointing-out is "a subjective designating falling outside the something itself" (*SL* §214). Nor is **Something** a separate bearer beneath its quality: §23 convicts the image of a naked bearer into which properties are stuck. The gain here is the quality's own mediated unity, not the addition of a subject standing underneath it.

## V. Something Is Not Yet an "Object"

A common stumble at this point is to think that we have just smuggled in the rich notion of "an object" — perhaps even a substance, in the heavy ontological sense — under the modest title "Something." We have not, and it is worth seeing why.

The Something of the *Logic* is, at this point, a minimal self-relation: the simple unity by which a determinacy holds together as one. It does not yet have *parts* or *properties* in the sense developed much later in Essence. Nor have we yet developed what self-preservation in alteration means — that belongs to the movement about to follow, not to an empirical identity silently imported here. There is no differentiation between its essence and its appearance. What has been established is only the simple self-relation resulting from the sublated distinction within Quality.

Equally, Something is not yet a *subject* in any sense richer than this minimal self-relation. We are still in the Doctrine of Being. The concrete subjectivity developed in the sphere of the Concept is not already present in this first determination of Something. Something is the beginning of that development, not its completion.

But Something is also genuinely more than *Dasein* — though not because *Dasein* lacked internal unity. §10 established that its moments are its own and that it is the first determination which **stands**. What is new is that the distinction between those moments has been sublated, so that the determinacy is now a *simple* self-relation rather than an articulation spread between two sides. Hegel marks the gain exactly, and then deflates it in the same breath: something is "the mediation of itself with itself" (*SL* §211), yet this mediation "has no concrete determinations for its sides," and the negative of the negative is, as something, "only the beginning of the subject — being-within-self, only as yet quite indeterminate" (*SL* §§210, 212). The gain is real and it is small.

The novelty is therefore not self-reference without qualification. Hegel expressly says that self-mediation is present even in Becoming, though quite abstractly; in Something it is *posited* as simple identity (*SL* §211). The result is a mediated self-relation in the form of being. It does not yet supply a developed self, nor does naming it supply the arguments by which later forms of self-relation will be reached.

## VI. Something as Becoming — the Other

Something is, but it is not thereby released from the content of *Dasein*. It remains determinate being, and its self-mediation still has no concrete determinations for its sides. Hegel therefore returns to the movement implicit in it: Something is also, in itself, Becoming (*SL* §212).

But the moments of this Becoming are no longer just pure Being and pure Nothing. Its affirmative moment is now a determinate being, a Something; its negative moment is likewise a determinate being, but as the negative of that Something. This is the **Other** (*das Andere*). Because the moments themselves are determinate beings, their transition is **alteration**: Becoming has acquired a more concrete content (*SL* §212).

This is a development of the determination, not the deduction of a second empirical object from a first. Hegel also limits the result: Something initially alters only in its concept; it is not yet posited as mediating and mediated. Its negative initially counts simply as an Other in general.

Accordingly, Something and Other first confront one another as indifferent determinate beings. Each counts as a Something on its own; calling one the other's Other can still be an external comparison made by a third (*SL* §§213–215). The relation has not yet been developed in the form in which both sides are moments within one Something. To attribute that completed form to the beginning would conceal the work still required.

Hegel states the symmetry plainly: "If of two things we call one A, and the other B, then in the first instance B is determined as the other. But A is just as much the other of B. Both are, in the same way, *others*" (*SL* §214). But this sameness "arises only from external reflection, from the *comparing* of them" (*SL* §216). Swapping labels does not develop what belongs to the Other itself. To do that, Hegel takes the Other in its own determination: it "is not the other of something but the other in its own self, that is, the other of itself"; and such an other is "that which is absolutely dissimilar within itself, that which negates itself, *alters* itself" (*SL* §§217–218). This is otherness turned against itself, not the original Something with the labels swapped. Its positive result will be decisive.

This is a more delicate move than it sounds, and the standard misreadings around it are worth pre-empting.

The first standard misreading is that Hegel is here proving that "everything is related to everything else," in some vague holistic sense. The argument is more limited: it develops Something and Other from determinate being and alteration. It does not establish every empirical connection, or dispense with investigating how particular things are connected.

The second standard misreading is that the logical transition is itself a succession of events in time, as if we first possessed one object and then produced another. Hegel is developing pure determinations, not supplying such a history. Alteration here articulates the Becoming implicit in Something; it does not identify a temporal process or its empirical cause.

The third standard misreading is that the Other must already be a *specific* counterpart assigned to a given thing. Not yet. The starting determination is Other in general. No particular stone, planet, number, or line has been deduced as the counterpart of another; the present argument must develop otherness before it can support more determinate claims.

What we have, then, is not yet an explanation by reciprocal naming. The movement must proceed through the Other's own alteration, and show what that alteration preserves as well as what it negates.

## VII. Alteration, Self-preservation, and the Two Moments of Something

The Other alters itself. Yet what it alters into is again Other: at this level it has no further determination. In becoming other, it therefore unites with itself. Hegel's continuation is not simply another disappearance; the Other is reflected into itself with otherness sublated, and is a self-identical Something (*SL* §218). Otherness remains a distinguished moment of this result. The argument concerns the determination and its self-relation; it has not established the persistence of a material bearer through every empirical change.

Something thus preserves itself in the negative of its determinate being. It is essentially one with its otherness, but also distinct from it: it relates to otherness and "is not simply its otherness" (*SL* §219). This preserves the gain of Something rather than emptying it into a placeholder for whatever is not the Other. Its self-relation is mediated through negation; its otherness is both contained in it and distinguished from it.

The relation to otherness, in which Something is not simply lost, is **being-for-other** (*Sein-für-Anderes*). Its preserved being, self-related in opposition to its relation to the Other, is **being-in-itself** (*Ansichsein*) (*SL* §§219–221). The distinction has not been supplied to rescue a contentless relation by adding an independent core. It articulates the self-preservation just reached: relation to otherness, and self-equality in opposition to that relation.

These are **moments of one Something**, not independent pieces or an isolated interior beside an external relation. Hegel contrasts the two pairs: Something and Other initially fall apart as indifferent beings; being-in-itself and being-for-other are those determinations posited as relations within one unity, each containing the other moment (*SL* §221). The next installment will develop what this inseparability entails.

An earlier structure has returned, but transformed. Being and Nothing, already transformed in *Dasein*, recur within Something as being-in-itself and being-for-other (*SL* §222). Their content is no longer the same: self-relation is now mediated through the negation of otherness. This is a definite result of the present movement, not a rule that any pair of opposed names must generate the next pair. An expectation of further articulation is useful only if the next step earns it.

## VIII. A Word on Method: What This Step Has Made Visible

Before turning to the next installment, three observations about what has happened in this one are worth registering — observations whose reach must be re-tested at each later step rather than assumed to carry.

The first is that **each new determination must arise from the content already reached**. Quality is *Dasein*'s immediate determinacy; Reality and Negation articulate it. Something results from the sublation of their distinction. Its implicit Becoming acquires determinate moments as Something and Other, and the Other's alteration yields self-relation with otherness retained as a moment. The warrant lies in these particular transitions, not in applying the phrase "immanent development" to a sequence of names. Neither grammatical extraction nor reciprocal relabelling would have supplied the missing movement.

The second is that **the misreadings substitute a different object or level of determination**. Some import a richer category too early — a thing with properties, a substance, a developed subject. Others substitute propositional denial for qualitative negation, or the external comparison of given things for the development of a determination. These are not all the same mistake, and exposing them does not discredit formal inference or empirical comparison in their own tasks. The corrective is to identify what the present argument is about and what it has actually earned.

The third is that **the compulsion has to be exhibited in the particular determination, and cannot be read off from the presence of opposition as such**. Reality and Negation could not be held apart, because each proved to contain the other; Something and Other required a different development, through alteration and self-preservation. The next installment must likewise show its own movement, not deduce it from a schema. §10 distinguishes the Absolute Idea's logical completion and free release into Nature from the ordinary transition of an inadequate category, while retaining the urge to sublate its confinement to pure thought; §19 will show the mode of movement changing as Being passes into Essence; and §27 shows the Concept's universal *particularizing itself* as "creative power," which Hegel says expressly is "**not a transition**, for this occurs only in the sphere of **being**." These distinctions forbid a general law that every articulated unity must collapse in the same way. Here, the independence initially attributed to the sides has failed; the form and result of that failure had to be shown.

## IX. What Comes Next

Something now contains its relation to otherness and its self-relation in distinction from that relation. The next installment will develop these moments — **being-in-itself** and **being-for-other** — rather than introduce them to fill an empty placeholder. From their further articulation come the Something's **determination** (*Bestimmung*) and its **constitution** (*Beschaffenheit*), and the **limit** (*Grenze*) at which the Something both is and is not itself. From the limit, the move to **finitude** — the Something as that which has its end *in itself*, which is the entry into the long arc that will close the first major movement of Quality in the determination of **infinity**.

---

## Coda

A reader who has followed this installment may notice that the pace of the *Logic* has begun to tell its own story. The opening already preserved content through transformation: Becoming broke on an articulated contradiction, consisting in the vanishing of the very distinction it required, and its sublation yielded *Dasein* rather than an empty result (§10). Here the determinations grow richer, and their articulation carries more and more of the work. But collapse does not cease with *Dasein*: §19 will close the Doctrine of Being with the collapse of absolute indifference, and that collapse is the hinge on which the whole sphere turns into Essence. This is not a change of method — it is the same immanent development throughout — but it is a change of *texture*, and it is worth feeling as a change. The *Logic* will become more and more articulated as it proceeds, and the reader who has felt the texture shift here will be ready to feel it shift again at the major junctures ahead.

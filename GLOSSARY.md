# Glossary: German — our prose — Miller

**This table governs the synopsis's own prose. It does not govern quotations.**
A quotation is given in the words of the edition it was taken from; our own
sentences are given in the words of this table. Where the two diverge and the
divergence carries weight, it is *named* in the text rather than smoothed over
(§27 is the model: it says outright that where we write *Concept*, Miller writes
"Notion", and where we write *singular*, he writes "individual").

Introducing a term, look for it here first. Introducing a new one, add a row.
If the corpus already uses a word other than this table's, that is a defect:
either the table or the corpus, but not both.

The **§§** column lists the installments in which the gloss actually occurs, so
every row is checkable against the corpus rather than taken on trust.

## Where Miller's word is not ours

These are the traps that have actually reached a draft. In each case the right
move is *not* to harmonise: our prose keeps our word, the quotation keeps his.

| German | Our prose | Miller (*SL*) | How it was checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Begriff* | **Concept** | **Notion** | "the Notion" occurs ~1 086× in the source text; "Concept" is Miller's word only in di Giovanni |
| *Schein* | **shine** (see note below) | **illusory being** | ~131×, printed with Hegel's own bracket: "the negative has an illusory being [*scheint*]" |
| *Schranke* | **Restriction** | **limitation** | Miller's heading reads "the limitation and the ought" |
| *das Einzelne* | **the singular** | **individual** | "individuality" ~321×; "singular" is vanishingly rare |
| *die Sache selbst* | **the fact itself** | **the fact itself** | agrees — "the totality of the determinations of the fact — the fact itself" |

Two consequences, already binding in `.github/copilot-instructions.md`:
`Concept` must never appear **inside** quotation marks, and a **parenthesised**
gloss inside quotation marks is always ours and always wrong — Miller's own
glosses are **square**-bracketed.

## The Absolute Relation (§26)

| German | Our prose | §§ |
|---|---|---|
| *Substanz* | substance | §26 |
| *Akzidenzen* | accidents | §26 |
| *Aktuosität* | actuosity | §26 |
| *absolute Macht* | absolute power | §26 |
| *Gewalt* | violence | §26, §27 |
| *Kausalität* | causality | §24, §25, §26 |
| *erlischt* | is extinguished | §26 |
| *Wechselwirkung* | reciprocity | §03, §08, §24–§26 |

*Aktuosität* is Hegel's own coinage against Spinoza's inert substance, and
**actuosity** keeps it strange on purpose. Do not soften it to "activity".
*Gewalt* is **violence**, not "force" — *Kraft* is force, and the §26 argument
turns on substance exercising *power* over its accidents without *Kraft*.

## Settled questions

- ***Bestimmung* vs *Bestimmtheit* vs *Beschaffenheit*.** *Bestimmung* is
  **determination** — what a Something is *in itself*, its vocation. *Beschaffenheit*
  is **constitution** — what it is through its relation to other. *Bestimmtheit*
  is **determinateness**, the bare category. The §11–§13 argument dies if these
  three collapse into one English word.

- ***Grenze* vs *Schranke*.** **Limit** is the determinacy a Something *is*;
  **Restriction** is that same limit taken as a *defect*, which is why the Ought
  follows from it and not from the limit. Miller renders both with "limit /
  limitation"; our split is sharper and is kept deliberately — but a quotation
  keeps his word.

- ***Dasein* vs *Existenz*.** **Determinate being** is the immediate being of
  §10–§13; **existence** is the *mediated* immediacy that has come out of ground
  in §22–§23. They are not synonyms, and the difference is the whole point of the
  Doctrine of Essence's second third.

- ***die Sache selbst* vs *Ding*.** **The fact itself** is the self-grounding
  totality of ground plus all its conditions (§22); **the thing** is the category
  that arrives only afterwards (§23). Never render *Sache* as "thing" here — it
  collides with the *Ding* that follows.

- ***Erscheinung* vs *Schein*.** **Appearance** is essence's own showing, and is
  true; **shine** is the merely-illusory moment inside essence. English blurs
  them under "appearance"; keep them apart.

- **Capitalisation.** Category names are capitalised where the text is naming the
  category as such (the One, the Ought, the Absolute, Measure) and lower-case in
  ordinary predicative use. The corpus is not perfectly uniform on this and
  uniformity is not being retro-fitted.

## Open

- ***Schein* is not yet uniform in the corpus.** Four renderings are in use —
  "mere show", "the shine", "a mere illusory being", "Illusory Being" (§08, §20).
  This table settles on **shine** for our prose, reserving *illusory being* for
  quotation of Miller. §08 and §20 still need to be brought into line; until they
  are, this row describes the intended canon rather than the corpus as it stands.
